## Bounded question completed

The [H18 synthetic identifiability experiment](../../analyses/h18_identifiability/2026-11-08/report.md) tests whether invented contributions from reduced urge generation and greater action control can be separated from measured changes. It uses no observations from people and does not claim these equations describe Tourette biology.

Under a known, calibrated and invariant measurement model, the two artificial contributions are algebraically identifiable. Sparse measurement alone is not proof of structural ambiguity. With an unknown systematic reporting shift, however, two different latent explanations can generate the exact same observed distribution. Repeating the measurements cannot resolve that missing assumption. Known, unequal transfer coefficients can add identifying information from two context-specific tic means; unknown coefficients cannot do so by themselves. These limits do not erase identification already supplied by a valid independent measurement anchor.

The fixed experiment includes 14 measurement scenarios and six transfer scenarios, with 4,000 simulated datasets each. Repeated observations improve precision under the specified model, while treating repeated rows as independent gives undercoverage. In one prespecified hidden-shift scenario, the model chooses the wrong population-level explanation in 94.58% of simulated datasets. This is not individual classification accuracy, an estimate of real reporting bias, or clinical evidence. The two hidden-shift scenarios both use eight repeats; they do not test the effect of increasing repeats in that setting.

## Next bounded decision

Next audit what external measurement anchors and invariance checks existing public aggregate evidence could support for global or tic-specific urge reports and independent observed-tic measures. Specify temporal ordering, participant clustering and context-transfer estimands. Separate measurement feasibility from actual validation. If no defensible anchor exists, retain the proposed latent mechanism as unidentified rather than fitting a more elaborate predictor or assigning a treatment component.

Persistent severe adult symptoms, pain, effort, participation, adverse effects and co-occurring burdens remain central alongside all-age tic evidence. The artificial units have no age or clinical burden and cannot establish applicability to these groups. Do not collect new symptom records or videos, repeat closed source audits without an informative new lead, or infer treatment advice from the simulation.
