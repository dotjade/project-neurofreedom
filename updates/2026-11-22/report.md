# 22 November 2026: Can injury registries answer the short-term severe-tic question?

## Bounded question and prediction
Longer-term registry data may identify recorded injuries that symptom scales miss, but a study can answer the question about immediate tic-related injury in adults with severe active tics only if it has a defined adult sample with measured baseline severity, a prespecified short observation window and a known denominator, and an injury outcome whose relationship to a tic can be adjudicated. We expected the candidate registry studies to improve outcome coverage without jointly meeting those conditions.

## Methods and results
Checked the two primary studies against the existing evidence ledger and the [19 November comparison](../2026-11-19/report.md) and [20 November endpoint audit](../2026-11-20/report.md). Neither candidate identifier was found in the ledger. Reviewed the Swedish full text and the Taiwan abstract, retained their separate comparators, and checked the cohort definitions and ascertainment against the three requirements. These are historical observational studies, not new treatment evidence.

| Endpoint and primary record | Population, window and result | What it cannot tell us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serious transport injury or death, [Mataix-Cols et al., DOI 10.1002/mds.28301](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.28301) | Swedish registry, ages 18–65 at entry; follow-up from 1997 or age 18, whichever was later, through an event or censoring by end of 2013; mean cohort follow-up 13.46 years. Hospital/specialist-treated transport injury or transport death (ICD-10 V01–V99) in 271/3,449 with recorded TS/CTD and 340,669/6,123,841 without it. Sex- and birth-year-adjusted HR 1.50 (95% CI 1.33–1.69); full sibling comparison 1.41 (1.18–1.68). Excluding recorded ADHD from both groups: 1.20 (0.99–1.44). | No contemporaneous severity or driver/passenger distinction, no evidence a tic caused the event and no fixed short window. Two or more adult diagnoses identified a subgroup (166/1,570 with an event; HR 1.68 [1.45–1.96] vs general comparators), but care contact is not measured severity. The ADHD sensitivity changes the estimate; it does not establish causation or the absence of association. |
| Medically recorded traumatic brain injury, [Chen et al., PMID 30833232](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30833232/), DOI 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2019.02.033 | Taiwan national claims, 2,261 TS cases and 20,349 age- and sex-matched controls, enrolled in 2000–2012 and followed through 2013. Published abstract reports HR 1.59 (95% CI 1.37–1.85). | The abstract does not establish severity at baseline, a severe-adult subgroup, a fixed short window or tic attribution. Full modeling and coding details were not independently verified. Medication comparisons were not used. |

Neither study meets the joint requirements. The Swedish study describes transport-related harm, not driving performance or all injury. The Taiwan study covers a different medical outcome and country. The Swedish transport and [previously reviewed cervical study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2783518) share a patient registry and overlapping years; individual overlap is unknown. Do not pool hazard ratios across outcomes or treat their agreement in direction as independent replication of tic-related injury. These estimates cannot be converted into individual, annualized, or immediate risks from the reported counts.

## Next decision
Use registry outcomes for distinct long-term associations only. Next prioritize an adult prospective cohort with clinically assessed active severe tics, a prespecified short follow-up interval, known denominator, and independently confirmed injury and attribution. Keep registry injury, self-reported behavior, clinical service use and trial eligibility separate. No clinical, treatment, or activity change follows from this comparison.
