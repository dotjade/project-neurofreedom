# 19 November 2026 | Three different questions about tic-related harm

## Bounded evidence scan
On 19 November UTC, PubMed ESearch returned zero for `(Tourette[Title/Abstract] OR "tic disorder"[Title/Abstract] OR "tic disorders"[Title/Abstract]) AND ("2026/11/18"[Date - Publication] : "2026/11/19"[Date - Publication])`. ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 returned zero for condition `Tourette OR chronic tic disorder` and `AREA[LastUpdatePostDate]RANGE[11/18/2026, 11/19/2026]`. The direct [randomized TICNET record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06271083) still had no posted results and a last posted update of 21 February 2024. A supplementary indexed search for recent tic research, preprints and safety did not verify a qualifying item. This is a limited scan, not a systematic or regulator-wide safety search; dates, indexing and access can lag.

## Question and prediction
Can historical registry outcomes or a short self-report scale fill the missing estimate of near-term injury for people screened out of adult TICNET because urgent care was required? Prediction: neither establishes that estimate. Compare populations, denominators, observation windows and what was measured. Compute crude recorded proportions as counts divided by cohort size; compare these descriptively with the authors' adjusted hazard ratios, without pooling datasets or annualizing proportions.

| Evidence stream | Denominator and time | What it establishes, and what it does not |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate risk at trial eligibility | Adults screened for the [pilot](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44247-026-00284-7) and [separate randomized protocol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1518666/full); eligibility decision, not a follow-up window | Both designs exclude tics posing immediate injury risk and requiring urgent care. An exclusion criterion is not prospective injury ascertainment. A screen-out count attributable specifically to this criterion was not established in this pass. |
| Self-reported burden | [SIBS-T validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11291896/), 123 adults with tic disorders, four-week lookback | Five-item severity measure with internal consistency α=0.88 in this sample. This does not establish reproducibility over time, an imminent-risk threshold, or sensitivity to treatment change. |
| Recorded clinical outcomes | [Swedish registry study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2783518), 6,791 specialist-diagnosed TS/CTD cases and 67,910 matched controls, median follow-up 17 years | 237/6,791 (3.49%) vs 1,483/67,910 (2.18%) had a cervical disorder record, a crude difference of 1.31 percentage points; fully adjusted HR 1.39 (95% CI 1.22–1.59). For the subgroup with at least two recorded diagnoses at age 18 or older, 123/1,921 (6.40%) vs 695/19,210 (3.62%), crude difference 2.79 points; adjusted HR 1.52 (1.26–1.83). |

## Interpretation and decision
The prediction held. The registry association is evidence of long-term clinical burden in the study population, not a forecast for the urgent-risk group. Its endpoint includes vascular and nonvascular disorders and cervical surgery, not only injury attributable to tics. The registry did not measure tic severity or body location; the persistent-diagnosis proxy is not an urgent-risk definition. The subgroup is nested in the main cohort, not an independent replication. Crude proportions and adjusted hazard ratios answer different questions. No short-term risk estimate can be recovered by dividing by median follow-up or by applying these results to the trial exclusion group.

Keep three separate extraction fields for future work: (1) numbers screened and screened out for immediate injury risk, (2) numerator and denominator for patient-reported burden over a stated lookback, and (3) independently ascertained injury or urgent-care events with follow-up completeness, exposure time and attribution. The next prerequisite for an acute estimate is a cohort including the urgent-risk group with a prespecified short follow-up and documented event capture. This is a research evidence gap, not individual medical advice.
