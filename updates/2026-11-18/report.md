# 18 November 2026 | What does zero reported serious harm establish in the adult TICNET pilot?

## Bounded evidence scan

These searches are bounded and indexing can lag. The direct [TICNET randomized registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06271083) returned `hasResults=false`; last update was posted 21 February 2024, so the recruitment label and estimated dates are not proof of current operational status.

## Question and method
Following the recent distinction between symptom scores and lived burden, test a separate reporting question: can the pilot's zero reported serious adverse events rule out a 5% event probability within the pilot population, and can its follow-on trial directly answer the same question for adults with tics posing an immediate risk requiring urgent care? Prediction: no on both counts. Extract the number enrolled, serious-event statement and eligibility from the [pilot](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44247-026-00284-7); compare the registered follow-on [protocol](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1518666/full), Table 1 and sections 2.4 and 5. Use an exact one-sided binomial calculation under the optimistic assumption of complete ascertainment for 31 comparable, independent participants over the same observation window. No participant data, simulation, treatment recommendation or between-study pooling.

## Results
- **Pilot (NCT04908969):** 31 adults, single group and unmasked. No serious adverse events were reported during treatment or follow-up. The exact illustrative upper one-sided 95% bound is **9.21%** (`1 - 0.05 ** (1/31)`); even at a true 5% event probability, the chance of seeing zero in 31 is **20.39%** (`0.95 ** 31`). Incomplete ascertainment would make the full-denominator illustration too optimistic. This is not an observed incidence estimate, a treatment-attributable risk estimate, or a bound for the excluded group.
- **Separate randomized protocol (NCT06271083):** therapist-guided online exposure and response prevention versus an active control, with blinded ratings for the week-11 primary tic severity endpoint. It plans adverse-event and impairment measurement but shares the pilot's exclusion for severe tics that create an immediate risk and require urgent care. The protocol describes the pilot as earlier work; distinct study registrations/designs are verified, but individual overlap cannot be checked and the protocol contributes no outcome observations.
- **Burden and reporting:** 28 of 31 met a threshold of the first four of eight chapters, not necessarily all eight. Participants reported an average of 197 minutes weekly, while therapist time averaged 18.1 minutes per participant per week; these capture different things. The pilot reported post-treatment fatigue and other non-serious complaints; zero serious events does not mean zero harms or burden.

## Decision
Track the randomized trial separately without counting it as an efficacy or safety result. Record allowed severity and urgent-risk exclusions separately; a high total tic score is not itself equivalent to immediate injury risk. Extract participant time, completion definition, adverse-event collection, denominators and follow-up completeness alongside clinical outcomes. For the explicitly excluded urgent-risk group, look for directly applicable evidence rather than extrapolating from a zero-event pilot.
