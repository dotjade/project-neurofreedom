## Question, prediction and test
Yesterday's [measurement audit](../2026-11-16/report.md) kept sleep separate and rejected unvalidated cross-age pooling. Today's distinct question: does the positive impairment–sleep association survive both the full and reduced published adjustment models across two different post-childhood samples? Prediction: the direction remains positive in both, but nominal significance is sensitive to variable selection. Extract standardized impairment coefficients and nominal p values from the paired full/final models. Use the adult Tourette subsample as the closer diagnostic comparator, not the full mixed-disorder survey or a new cohort. No pooled coefficient, independent validation or causal estimate.

| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| [Tooker et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1594347), Table 3; 10-week clinician-rated tic-related impairment versus later Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index | +0.28 / .094 | +0.34 / .014 | 80 of 126 retained, ages 16–30 at follow-up, about 11 years later. Sleep measured only at follow-up (internal consistency .49), no objective sleep measure. This is a secondary follow-up of pediatric trial NCT00218777, not an independent cohort from related follow-up and secondary trial papers. |
| [Ricketts et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35150595/), Table 5, Tourette subgroup of 125 total adult internet respondents with persistent tic disorders; concurrent self-rated impairment and PROMIS Sleep Disturbance | +0.19 / .31 | +0.32 / .01 | Cross-sectional, imputed regressions, and no formal validation in a severe adult subgroup. The two measures, adjustment sets and timescales differ. |

Result: 2/2 pairs preserved the direction, 2/2 selected models cleared nominal .05, and 0/2 full models did. The adult p values are rounded as reported. These comparisons are descriptive and exploratory: neither a binary p-value classification nor a larger selected coefficient establishes a true effect or an invalid full model. The consistent direction makes separate sleep measurement worth retaining, but does not demonstrate a robust individual predictor, comparable scores across ages, a treatment effect, or a causal tic-to-sleep pathway.

Next decision: retain sleep as a separately defined endpoint, but do not use tic-related impairment as a proxy or predictive threshold. Seek an adult cohort across severity strata with repeated sleep measurements, explicit missingness and within-person versus between-person estimates, a prespecified adjustment set and out-of-sample validation.
