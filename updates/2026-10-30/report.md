**Prediction:** the direct-condition and indirect-condition statements available for [PMID 42947547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947547/), DOI 10.1002/mds.70590, will not by themselves identify a formal group-by-condition contrast or its precision.

**Executed fallback:** the [synthetic methods test](../../analyses/sensory_contrast/2026-10-30/model/report.md) fixes arbitrary direct/indirect group differences at 0.6 and 0.2, with standard errors 0.2 each. Marginal two-sided normal p values remain about .0027 and .3173. Changing only admissible covariance of the two difference estimates changes the formal contrast p value from about .2482 to .0000077 across four scenarios. The contrast itself stays 0.4.

**Actual inference:** a difference in significance is not a significant difference. Even identical marginal estimates and errors do not fix contrast uncertainty without their covariance or an appropriate fitted model. This analytic Gaussian construction is not patient data, a simulation of Tourette biology or a reconstruction of the paper. Nine implementation tests and 42 independent numerical checks passed. Inputs, code, output and review are saved; no random seed or paid compute was required.

**Uncertainty:** the illustration uses known covariance and pointwise normal intervals, whereas real repeated force-matching data may involve force levels, missingness, covariates and different degrees of freedom. It establishes no real interaction, equivalence, mechanism or treatment effect. Current severity association also remains different from future prediction or causal mediation.

## Reconciling seemingly conflicting sensory findings

Self-generated force attenuation and repeated externally delivered tactile gating ask different questions. The older [paired-pneumatic-stimulus study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42810670/) retains its gating null; smaller initial P50 responses are not the same outcome as the gating index. These studies neither directly replicate nor negate each other, and their outcomes should not be pooled as one sensory-filter measure. A shared mechanism would require an explicit bridge between constructs, not a broad label.

Adult recruitment does not establish persistent-severe-adult subgroup performance. Pediatric short-duration associations cannot substitute for adult evidence. Pain, impairment, communication, co-occurring conditions and daily function remain separate outcomes, not presumed to improve because a laboratory marker differs.
