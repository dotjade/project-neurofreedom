# Project NeuroFreedom weekly synthesis

18 October 2026 | Work reviewed: 12–18 October | Second weekly synthesis

## Bottom line

The week's strongest result is an endpoint decision: **tic reduction, pain relief, functional improvement and durable benefit need their own evidence.** Existing symptomatic treatment evidence remains valuable. The reviewed reports do not validate tic severity as a substitute for adult quality of life or pain, do not establish treatment-free durability, and do not identify a cure or disease-modifying mechanism.

This synthesis advances the [11 October review](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/updates/2026-10-11-weekly-synthesis.md); it does not repeat its synthetic cycle benchmark, MNS attrition calculation or closed generic dataset search. Historical papers newly analyzed this week are distinguished from newly published research. Persistent severe adult symptoms, single-modality tic disorders, pain, participation, comorbidity and unmet care needs remain central. No childhood or adult-dominant sample is silently substituted for an adult-only target.

## What the week's tests changed

### 1. Direct burden measurement survives the adult-registry and validation tests

**Prediction:** the available aggregate reports would not identify a validated severe-at-every-visit adult group or justify replacing patient-reported burden with tic scores.

**Learning:** the Calgary reports name relevant repeated measures, but do not supply the joint severity–QoL outcomes, justified diagnostic thresholds or outcome-level missingness required. Of 95 enrolled adults, 63 and 55 had completed the respective follow-ups; the possible overlap is 23–55, conditional on their being subsets of that enrollment. These are completion-set bounds, not a measured severe-persistence rate or known retention among everyone due.

The six-report validation pass supports language/version-specific GTS-QOL domains alongside separate tic severity and impairment. It supplies neither universal adult severity thresholds nor an adult anchor-based QoL change threshold. Jeon's pooled mixed-age response labels disagree in 34/232 cases (14.66%; conditional Wilson 95% interval 10.68–19.78%). That is tic-threshold versus clinician-improvement agreement, not adult QoL surrogate validation. Stable-status retest reliability, cross-sectional correlation, change concordance and treatment-effect surrogacy answer different questions.

**Decision:** retain direct, version-specific burden outcomes. The proposition that severity explains some burden remains plausible but was not established from these registry aggregates. Do not call the registry reports independent cohorts or the overlapping CBIT-derived validation reports independent replications. [Adult endpoint audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/adult_burden/2026-10-12/report.md); [validation audit and primary-source links](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/burden_validation/2026-10-13/report.md).

### 2. Behavioral treatment benefit is outcome-, time- and population-specific

**Prediction:** observed tic benefit would not automatically establish generalized function, off-treatment durability or shared individual response.

In **ONLINE-TICS**, the prespecified adult primary contrast was −1.28 YGTSS-TTS points (95% CI −2.58 to 0.01; p=.053), so primary superiority and its gated confirmatory non-inferiority claim were not established. Later exploratory tic, impairment and GTS-QOL contrasts are encouraging at particular visits. Four of six nominally favorable focal contrasts survive a post hoc nine-comparison sensitivity; that restricted exercise cannot restore the failed gate or adjust the entire trial. Early unfavorable urge/life-satisfaction signals, missingness and reporting conflicts remain visible. Access continued during follow-up. Ordered point estimates do not demonstrate growing efficacy or benefit after access/practice stops. [Primary report](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/11/1/250); [audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/online_tics/2026-10-14/report.md).

In **Wilhelm's CBIT trial**, the acute randomized tic-severity advantage was 3.3 points (95% CI 1.4–5.2), with a separately reported secondary impairment advantage. At six months, 12/15 returning early CBIT responders were positive; nine of the original 24 early responders were unobserved. The identified proportion within those 24 is 50–87.5%, not an 80% all-randomized response rate. Those bounds are not confidence intervals. Boosters were offered, control nonresponders could cross over, and ages 16–69 include minors. [Primary report](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/1307556); [maintenance audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/cbit_maintenance/2026-10-15/report.md).

The **same trial's McGuire functioning report** has no nominally significant advantage on its 12 acute disability/family/social contrasts. It supplies no contrast intervals sufficient to rule out important benefit. Positive responder associations and selected follow-up findings answer different questions; none of their functional positives survives the explicitly restricted post hoc multiplicity sensitivities. This is not a second independent treatment trial. [Primary manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190082/); [functioning audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/cbit_functioning/2026-10-16/report.md).

**Reconciliation:** established acute tic improvement and inconclusive broader function can coexist. The outcomes, treatment comparisons, timing and analysis populations differ. A significant result on one endpoint and a nonsignificant result on another neither proves their effects differ nor proves absence of functional benefit. Preserving this distinction strengthens, rather than dismisses, patient-relevant treatment research.

### 3. Pain is directly measurable, but these surveys cannot establish treatment efficacy

**Prediction:** pain ascertainment, recall windows and denominators would prevent a single adult prevalence or care-success estimate.

**Małek 2025** contributes adult intensity and unmet-care measurements: 56/76 Polish support-group respondents reported past-or-present pain. This is neither current nor population chronic-pain prevalence. Present pain and recalled worst pain are distinct; the latter's window is unspecified. Help-seeking counts, percentages and subgroup membership conflict, as do some displayed pain means. No corrected care denominator is adopted. Perceived usefulness is not causal effectiveness. [Primary source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full); [audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/adult_pain/2026-10-17/report.md).

**Taylor 2022** adds an explicit past-week interference window. Entry required current tics and tic-related pain; its 181 respondents aged 16–71 cannot estimate pain prevalence or an adult-only effect. All 13 printed tic-interference means exceed pain-interference means by 0.05–1.12 points, including rounding sensitivity. Missing item counts, N/A rules and paired covariance prevent a paired effect or clinical-importance inference. Qualitative reports of pain being the main problem for some respondents remain compatible with these aggregate means. [Primary source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.914044/full); [audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/pain_interference/2026-10-18/report.md).

**Decision:** keep intensity, interference, participation and treatment exposure separate. Do not pool the surveys, equate care receipt with relief, interpret subgroup significance differences as interactions, or infer pain relief from lower tic scores. Neither study identifies a persistently severe adult subgroup.

## This weekly run's bounded analysis

The new [evidence-structure computation](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/evidence_structure/2026-10-18/report.md) tests whether the behavioral-treatment reporting chain supplies independent randomized evidence for broader functional benefit after verified cessation of trial-provided access/boosters. Three fixed publications resolve to two trial clusters: ONLINE-TICS (NCT02605902), and Wilhelm/McGuire (NCT00231985). The acute primary outcomes retain different results: one supported contrast and one missed criterion.

No included report documents the full stronger conjunction of randomized functional follow-up and verified cessation of access/boosters. This is a bounded documentation finding, not a pooled clinical effect, proof of no benefit or a requirement to stop learned skills. The code audits prespecified structural predicates over hand-checked source classifications; it does not infer missing outcomes. Input, code, output and mechanical verification are archived with the analysis. Twenty-four mechanical checks passed; a separate rerun returned byte-identical output with five additional count/status assertions. This verifies the fixed coding implementation, with source/interpretation review against prior audits; it is not independent clinical validation.

The parallel [Marras 2001 focal-tic audit](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/analyses/focal_tic_pain/2026-10-18/report.md) reached a different kind of result. Lawful [NIHR secondary evidence](https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2003385) corroborates a treated-tic-area pain measure (page 210; Figure 216 on page 211), placing it under Safety and leaving its scale unspecified. The prediction that pain measurement would not be identifiable therefore cannot be retained as an absence claim. Primary methods/tables remained inaccessible. Whether this endpoint estimates relief from tic-related pain, procedural discomfort, or another construct remains unresolved at the required primary-source level, as do its denominator and crossover analysis. No numerical pain-efficacy calculation was justified. A secondary review is neither the original trial nor independent replication.

## Which inferences are retired, and which hypotheses remain open

Retire the following **unsupported extrapolations**, not the interventions or biological possibilities:

- Adult burden can be substituted by a tic score or a universal borrowed severe-state threshold.
- ONLINE-TICS demonstrated confirmatory primary superiority/non-inferiority, uniform QoL benefit or increasing efficacy.
- CBIT's 80% returnee result represents everyone randomized, uninterrupted remission or treatment-free durability.
- Same-trial functional analyses are independent replications or prove generalized benefit merely because tic scores improved.
- The pain surveys establish representative adult prevalence, a causal professional-care failure rate or an individual tic–pain effect.
- Registry postings, neural target engagement, an in-sample classification result or arithmetic reproducibility establish clinical benefit or disease modification.

Keep **H07/H13** durable pharmacology–learning synergy, **H17** mechanism-specific peripheral stimulation, **H18** personalized behavioral matching and **H19** developmental resilience as speculative extensions. The week's analyses do not directly test those causal predictions. Symptomatic behavioral-treatment anchors remain. The adult severity–burden association, individual change concordance, pain-mediated functional benefit, clinically meaningful gating differences and severe-adult treatment effects remain incompletely tested. No hypothesis is promoted because a report or an implementation check was added. [Hypothesis definitions](https://github.com/dotjade/project-neurofreedom/blob/main/hypotheses.md).

## Next informative experiment and help needed

The next scientific question should be **whether an intervention's tic improvement is accompanied by worthwhile pain-interference and participation benefit in adults with sustained burden**, including people with little tic variability and single-modality disorders. A future clinician-led study would retain randomized assignment, assess everyone at repeated visits, measure pain/function directly, document continued treatment/boosters, and keep benefit under continued care separate from any safely supervised treatment-free claim. It must not condition the main follow-up on acute response.

Before human execution, the missing prerequisites are a clinical investigator and institutional sponsor; patient/clinician agreement on outcomes and meaningful-change anchors; an appropriate comparator and safety/rescue plan; ethics review and consent; lawful data access, privacy governance and a secure environment; and a separately authorized protocol. No clinical study, recruitment, outreach, treatment change or sample-size claim is started here.

The immediate public-only next step is a **prespecified two-outcome design-discrimination experiment**: can tic improvement alone distinguish a true pain-interference benefit from no pain benefit? Publish a pre-analysis plan defining the synthetic joint-outcome mechanisms, observation schedule, missingness assumptions, estimands and disconfirming result before execution. Contrast scenarios that share the same tic response but have different pain outcomes; assess what direct paired pain measurement adds, including informative missingness. This is distinct from the completed one-outcome attrition bounds and response-label agreement checks. Stop at whether the stipulated designs distinguish the stated scenarios; do not claim clinical event rates, a meaningful-change threshold, sample-size/power sufficiency or biological mediation. No new simulation is executed in this weekly run.

**Owner help: none required now.** If an already lawfully held Marras methods/tables manuscript is available, it could resolve the focal-trial access gate; no subscription purchase or author contact is requested. Institutional and clinical help is needed only before moving beyond public/synthetic work. No family health information or home experiment is needed.
