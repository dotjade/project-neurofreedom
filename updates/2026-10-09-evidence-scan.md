## Recent primary studies prioritized

### 1. Newly indexed, previously published: brain–heart coupling pilot

Ali and colleagues, *Brain-heart coupling in tourette syndrome: a precision functional mapping study of discordant siblings*. DOI **10.3389/fnetp.2026.1881914**, PMID **42845759**. Published **23 September 2026**; PubMed entry **8 October 2026**. [Primary full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/network-physiology/articles/10.3389/fnetp.2026.1881914/full); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42845759/).

Four participants (two TS/unaffected sibling pairs) contributed repeated resting fMRI and pulse recordings. Individual autonomic–network associations differed, but the study is descriptive and hypothesis-generating. Dense repeated sampling does not increase the independent participant count. Tic events were not independently annotated; respiration was not measured; overlapping windows and clinical heterogeneity constrain interpretation. The paper cannot show whether physiology precedes tics, follows them, or reflects a shared cause. No treatment or remission endpoint was tested. Full-text methods and limitations were inspected; underlying data were not reanalyzed. Funding: Kavli, Tourette Association of America and NSF; authors report no commercial/financial conflict. No linked correction/retraction notice appeared in the retrieved PubMed record. Next verification: require concurrent tic annotation and held-out temporal prediction before promoting an autonomic-precursor hypothesis.

### 2. Overlap counterevidence: tactile sensory gating

Isaacs, Conley, Eckland and Key, *Somatosensory gating of pneumatic stimuli in adults with chronic tic disorder*. DOI **10.1016/j.biopsycho.2026.109408**, PMID **42810670**. Published **29 September 2026**. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42810670/).

A cross-sectional EEG comparison included 28 adults with chronic tic disorder and 34 neurotypical adults. Gating indices for P50, N140 and P300 did not differ between groups and were not associated with reported tactile responsivity. An altered first-stimulus P50 response was associated with tactile responsivity. This weakens a simple general sensory-gating-deficit account under this paradigm; it does not disprove every gating mechanism or establish treatment relevance. Abstract-only extraction: effect sizes, confidence intervals, exclusions, multiplicity handling, medication effects, funding and full bias assessment remain unverified. No intervention, longitudinal remission measure or safety outcome was tested. No linked notice appeared in the PubMed record. Next step: retrieve full methods and quantify precision before treating the null result as equivalence.

### 3. Overlap phenotyping: “just right” perceptions

*“Just Right” Perceptions and Obsessive-Compulsive Symptoms in Tourette Syndrome and Chronic Tic Disorder: Implications for Tourettic Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder.* DOI **10.5765/jkacap.260022**, PMID **42840857**, PMCID **PMC13639753**. PubMed publication date **1 October 2026**, entry **7 October**. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42840857/).

Retrospective clinic records covered 71 people older than 10 years: 8 with comorbid OCD and 63 without. Reported “just right” experiences were more frequent/severe in the comorbid group, and urge severity correlated with obsessive-compulsive symptoms. This supports separating sensory urges, incompleteness and compulsions in research measurement. It cannot establish a causal subtype or a mechanism of remission. Abstract-only extraction; small OCD subgroup, clinic selection, retrospective ascertainment and unverified covariate handling limit inference. Effect sizes, attrition, medication status, funding and conflicts were not extracted. No linked notice appeared in the PubMed record. Next step: inspect full methods before adding a stronger mechanistic claim.

## Conference/backlog ecopipam update

The relevant MDS abstract collection was first published **1 October 2026** (DOI **10.1002/mds.70480**), before the prior scan date. [Primary conference abstracts](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70480).

The early-treatment post hoc pool included 292 ecopipam-treated participants from phase 2b and the phase-3 open-label period. At week 8, the ≥25% tic-score response was **178/255 evaluable participants (69.8%)**, not 69.8% of all 292 enrolled. During eight weeks, **37/292 (12.7%) discontinued because of adverse events**, and 2/292 had a serious adverse event. Missing outcomes and the uncontrolled component prevent a clean pooled placebo-adjusted efficacy estimate. A separate subgroup abstract reported relapse HR 0.6 (95% CI 0.3–1.1) with psychiatric comorbidity and 0.3 (0.1–0.9) without; these do not establish equivalence across subgroups. These are secondary analyses of existing cohorts, including the already recorded phase-3 trial (PMID 42189524; NCT05615220), not independent confirmation or disease modification.

A **2 October sponsor release** describes an interim 18-month open-label extension (118 treated; median exposure 14.9 months), a 45.6% mean tic-score reduction and no new safety signal observed. This is sponsor-reported, uncontrolled interim evidence, not proof of rare-event safety, durable benefit after stopping treatment or cure. The exact 18-month analysis denominator and attrition need primary-report verification. The release describes ecopipam as investigational/under FDA review; no approval is inferred. [Sponsor release](https://ir.tevapharm.com/news-and-events/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Teva-Presents-New-Efficacy-and-Safety-Data-with-Ecopipam-an-Investigational-Treatment-for-Pediatric-Patients-with-Tourette-Syndrome/default.aspx).

## What this changes

1. Preserve a distinction between **newly published**, **newly indexed**, and **newly found backlog** in future daily records.
2. Keep the autonomic-precursor hypothesis proposed: the new-to-repository pilot does not test temporal precedence of physiology over tics.
3. Add the somatosensory-gating null finding as counterevidence to an overly general gating-deficit narrative; its uncertainty still needs full-text extraction.
4. Treat the MDS ecopipam findings as overlapping conference-level evidence and preserve both the evaluable denominator and adverse-event withdrawals. Do not turn ongoing symptomatic improvement into a cure claim.
5. Next routine pass should use the saved identifiers for deduplication and check whether official registry results or a primary extension report appear. No clinical action follows from this scan alone.
