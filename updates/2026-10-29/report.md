## New adult sensorimotor evidence

[Reduced Sensory Attenuation Scales with Tic Severity in Tourette Syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947547/) is a peer-reviewed primary study, DOI [10.1002/mds.70590](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70590), electronically published October 28. Thirty adults with TS and 30 controls reproduced a target force directly or through a button. Lower direct-condition force overestimation in TS was interpreted as reduced attenuation. The association with YGTSS total tic severity accounted for additional variance beyond medication load, obsessive-compulsive symptoms and urge severity, according to the abstract. No indirect-condition group difference was observed.

The public abstract supplies neither effect sizes nor confidence intervals. It does not establish a formal group-by-condition interaction, and a significant result in one condition alongside a null result in another is insufficient for that inference. Contemporaneous variance explained does not establish future prediction, temporal precedence or mediation. Adjustment for selected measured factors does not remove all confounding. Adult inclusion does not establish validation in persistent-severe adults or chronic motor/vocal tic subgroups.

The result is relevant to yesterday's [causal synthesis](../../analyses/causal_synthesis/2026-10-28/report.md), but it does **not** test the proposed input-variability by control-retuning interaction. Nor does it erase the retained null reaching-compensation and EEG associations. Attenuation, sensory gating, aperiodic activity, force variability and retuning latency are different constructs. Recent publicity concerning an older sensory-gating study was not conflated with this new force-matching paper. No first-ever novelty claim is independently established here.

## Bounded question advanced beyond collection

**Prediction:** the transdiagnostic 36-gene union will not mean that all 36 genes pass a CTD-analysis discovery threshold. Combining data may strengthen some signals while weakening others.

**Method:** inspect the publisher's separately identified aggregate figure source data and crosswalk high-confidence gene-list membership across CTD, OCD and combined analyses. Retain combined q values and aggregate de novo damaging counts for these genes only. Do not access the mixed individual-variant/participant supplement. Compare with the paper's high-confidence and probable rules, preserve discordant classifications and distinguish threshold membership from a formal between-disorder comparison.

**Actual result:** the [gene-specificity audit](../../analyses/gene_specificity/2026-10-29/report.md) maps all 36 union genes: 3 pass high-confidence thresholds in both disorder analyses, 7 in CTD but not OCD, 9 in OCD but not CTD, and 17 in neither disorder-specific list but in the combined list. These are analysis-membership categories, not biological exclusivity. CELSR3 and WWC1 are in the CTD high-confidence list but outside the combined high-confidence list; their combined q values are approximately .1275 and .1161. Both meet the reported combined probable classification with three de novo damaging counts each.

**Uncertainty:** absence from a high-confidence list does not establish no effect. Different significance labels do not establish significantly different disorder effects. The same samples contribute across analyses, so overlap is not independent replication. Separate disorder-specific q values and formal heterogeneity estimates were not reconstructed from restricted mixed tables. A q value is not a personalized risk or a gene-specific probability of causation. These gene associations are not validated treatment targets.

**Decision:** close the aggregate membership question. The union is now resolved without conflating CTD-specific threshold support with biological specificity. Remaining disorder-effect comparisons require separately available aggregate estimates and covariance/heterogeneity methods. Do not repeat yesterday's union arithmetic or promote these associations to a universal cause.
