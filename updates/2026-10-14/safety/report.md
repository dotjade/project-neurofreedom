## Intuniv finding and date provenance

[EMA Intuniv EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/intuniv) identifies 13 October 2026 as the page, English product information, RMP and procedural-history update date. Its tracked-changes DOCX was first published that day.

The [procedural history](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/procedural-steps-after/intuniv-epar-procedural-steps-taken-scientific-information-after-authorisation_en.pdf), pages 1–2, identifies variation EMA/VR/0000334464, with opinion/notification date 3 September 2026. This type-II variation updates SmPC section 5.1 using final results of SPD503-401 and updates Annex II; RMP version 5.0 accompanied it. Thus web publication, regulatory opinion, and underlying study/RMP dates are different.

The [RMP v5.0](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/rmp/intuniv-epar-risk-management-plan_en.pdf), page 2, is signed 13 February 2026 and uses a 10 October 2025 data lock. These data are not newly collected October 2026 evidence.

### Study content now in the label

The [SmPC, section 5.1, printed page 16](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/intuniv-epar-product-information_en.pdf#page=16) describes SPD503-401 as a planned 97-week post-approval ADHD study in ages 6–17, initially with a 49-week 1:1:1 randomized double-blind guanfacine/atomoxetine/placebo phase followed by open-label guanfacine.

The primary analysis was shortened to week 18; the label says this prevents reliable conclusions about long-term neurocognitive effects. It reports:
- 138 double-blind completers (47.9% overall); completion was 39.6% guanfacine, 67.7% placebo, and 36.5% atomoxetine
- Adverse-event discontinuation: 14.6% guanfacine, 5.2% placebo, 8.4% atomoxetine
- 121 entered open label; 36% discontinued treatment and 5.8% discontinued for adverse events
- Overall safety was consistent with the known profile; week-18 secondary efficacy outcomes were comparable to pivotal studies

The paragraph does not explicitly give total randomized or per-arm denominators; none is inferred. Its population and efficacy endpoint are ADHD, not tic severity.

### What changed and what did not

The [EMA tracked-changes DOCX](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information-tracked-changes/intuniv-epar-product-information-tracked-changes_en.docx) was downloaded and its OOXML revisions inspected. It marks the long-term study heading/paragraph as inserted and the completed post-authorisation study obligation in Annex II as deleted, alongside editorial/table-format changes. No new substantive warning text was found among the tracked textual changes.

The procedural history separately attributes addition of QT-prolongation adverse-event/warning/overdose text to variation EMA/VR/0000269598 dated 11 December 2025. That older change must not be misdated as October 2026.

RMP pages 2–3 and 38 describe removal of long-term safety from the missing-information list and QT prolongation from important safety concerns. Page 38 says QT remains addressed by the label and routine pharmacovigilance. Its wording shifts between “potential” and “identified” risk, so this report avoids interpreting that terminology shift as a new clinical finding. The current SmPC still contains QT precautions and postmarketing QT reports.

Interpretation: record a new regulatory-document/safety-context update. Do not convert the RMP list change into a claim that long-term neurocognitive safety has been established; the newly added SmPC explicitly limits that conclusion. No new prescribing recommendation is inferred.

## Limits and reproducibility

This is a bounded incremental public-notice scan, not an exhaustive review of every product label, national EU safety notice, raw adverse-event report, recall-database record, or private manufacturer communication. FDA SrLC was not individually enumerated for every active ingredient. The device recall candidate demonstrates why headline communication indexes alone cannot establish that no recalls exist.

The first search batches used literal site.fda.gov/site.ema.europa.eu/site.gov.uk strings rather than valid site: operators, so those batches were not reliably domain-restricted. Later batches used explicit domain filters. Search filters returned historical and occasional off-domain material; all conclusions used regulator pages/document dates, not search freshness labels. Newly indexed material may appear later. Publication pages usually provide dates rather than exact UTC timestamps; the overlap avoids unsupported within-day timing claims.

Initial guessed FDA-device and MHRA-FSN URL paths failed and were corrected through official search results. The web extractor failed for the Intuniv RMP and DOCX; direct downloads from links extracted from the official EMA page succeeded (HTTP 200). A later web retry returned 429; no access-control bypass was attempted.
