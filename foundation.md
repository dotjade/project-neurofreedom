# Project NeuroFreedom: scientific foundations and discriminating research gaps

Evidence checked 8 October 2026. This is a research synthesis, not medical advice, a patient protocol, or a claim that a cure is near. Primary papers are emphasized. The proposed experiments below are research hypotheses and design recommendations, not findings. This is a targeted evidence review rather than a formally exhaustive systematic review.

## Deliverable coverage and review scope

- **D1: State of Science** — Sections 1–6 describe natural history, circuit/cellular evidence, sleep and current treatment evidence.
- **D2: Cure Discovery Gap Analysis** — Sections 7–8 translate uncertainties into falsifiable work packages and measurement requirements.
- **D5: Remission Mechanism Investigation** — Sections 2–4 and work packages A–D distinguish developmental remission, compensation and sleep-state modulation.

**Search scope:** targeted public-web searches on 8 October 2026 for prospective tic trajectories, objectively assessed adult persistence, video-polysomnography, developmental MRI/MRS, genetics, organoids, postmortem multiomics and representative controlled treatment trials. Sources were checked through PubMed, PMC, journal publishers and official clinical/public-health records. Foundational studies were included alongside relevant 2025–2026 publications; there was no publication-date exclusion.

**Limitations:** no exhaustive database export, dual-reviewer screening, registered review protocol, formal risk-of-bias scoring, meta-analysis or comprehensive trial-registry audit was completed. English-language search terms and accessible records may miss evidence. Some primary studies were assessed from abstracts or indexed excerpts because full text was unavailable; the 15-study companion evidence ledger records retrieval depth. Consequently, a gap means a plausible unresolved question in this targeted map, not proof that no investigator has studied it. Proposed mechanisms and experiments are explicitly distinguished from established findings.

## Executive judgment

The best-supported starting point is a developmental, heterogeneous circuit disorder with modifiable expression, not a single established biochemical defect. Developmental improvement and changing brain states provide valuable natural experiments. Neither observation, by itself, establishes that the original vulnerability has disappeared or identifies a safe way to remove it permanently.

Three corrections should anchor the project:

1. **Sleep does not universally switch tics off.** Video-polysomnography has identified tics during REM and non-REM sleep. The useful question is which aspects of sleep state, arousal and motor gating alter tic expression, in whom, and with what temporal ordering. [Cohrs et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11160467/)
2. **Developmental improvement is common; complete, durable disappearance is a different outcome.** A large longitudinal clinic cohort found most older adolescents had absent, minimal or mild tics, while a small blinded video follow-up found residual adult tics in many people who believed them absent. These results are compatible once measurement and ascertainment are considered. [Groth et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28335874/); [Pappert et al., 2003](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/01.WNL.0000086370.10186.7C)
3. **Effective treatment is not evidence of cure.** Current public-health guidance states that there is no cure; behavioral and medical treatments can reduce burdensome tics. A research program should preserve symptom relief as valuable while separately testing sustained changes in vulnerability and long-term trajectory. [CDC, updated March 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/tourette-syndrome/treatment/index.html)

## 1. Define what success would mean before selecting targets

These are proposed operational distinctions, not an established regulatory cure definition for Tourette syndrome:

- **Acute tic suppression:** fewer or less intense observed tics during a particular condition or intervention. This does not necessarily improve urges, effort, pain or participation.
- **Clinically meaningful control:** sustained improvement in impairment and patient-prioritized outcomes, with tolerable burden and adverse effects. Residual tics may be compatible with excellent quality of life.
- **Clinical remission:** symptoms are absent or below a prespecified threshold across an explicit follow-up period and observation protocol. Document whether control requires medication, a device, ongoing learned strategies or substantial suppression effort.
- **Disease modification:** an intervention changes the subsequent course or underlying vulnerability beyond its immediate symptomatic action. A durable between-group difference, a plausible causal mechanism and evidence against ordinary maturation, regression to the mean and residual treatment effects are needed. Biomarker movement alone is insufficient.
- **Cure:** a much stronger claim of durable resolution. An ethical project should not promise proof of lifelong non-recurrence from a finite study.

CBIT can produce durable benefits through learned skills; calling those benefits valuable does not require claiming removal of the initiating biology. Conversely, a lasting compensatory circuit change might eventually qualify as disease modification even if it does not reverse every developmental difference. The label should follow the evidence rather than predetermine the preferred mechanism.

The AAN guideline recommends assessing impairment from the individual's perspective and recognizes watchful waiting when tics do not cause functional impairment. It also states that evidence does not show that earlier treatment is more effective. This is a warning against presenting an untested early-intervention strategy as prevention of persistent Tourette syndrome. [AAN practice guideline, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6537133/)

## 2. Natural developmental improvement: real signal, heterogeneous outcomes

### Strongest useful longitudinal observations

**Groth et al., 2017.** Prospective Danish specialist-clinic cohort: 314 participants aged 5–19 at baseline; 227 reassessed six years later. Among those older than 16, 17.7% had no tics, 59.5% had minimal/mild tics and 22.8% had moderate/severe tics. Tic severity declined with age. Comorbidities or other psychopathology remained in 63% at follow-up. This is stronger trajectory evidence than a one-time adult recollection, but referral selection and incomplete retention limit population inference. A clinic-defined absence of tics is not a lifetime recurrence assessment. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28335874/)

**Pappert et al., 2003.** Thirty-one adults received repeat standardized videos after being filmed as children; raters were blinded. Objective severity improved, but 90% still showed tics, and half of those reporting themselves tic-free had objective tic evidence. Only 13% used tic medication as adults versus 81% in childhood. The central lesson is measurement discordance, not that 90% is a universal persistence rate: this was a small tertiary-center sample, with substantial loss between the original 56-child video database and adult reassessment. Five-minute recordings also imperfectly sample waxing and waning. [Primary paper](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/01.WNL.0000086370.10186.7C)

**Burd et al., 2001.** An epidemiologically ascertained childhood cohort supplied 39 follow-up interviews among 54 eligible individuals. Tic severity fell by 59%; 44% were described as essentially symptom-free. This offers a useful less-clinic-selected counterpoint, but interview-based outcomes and attrition are not equivalent to blinded extended observation. Do not average its remission percentage with video studies as if they measured the same endpoint. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11417610/)

**Groth et al., 2019.** In the Danish longitudinal cohort, childhood severity of tics, OCD and ADHD predicted corresponding early-adult outcomes. These associations can support stratification; they are not determinative individual forecasts or proof that reducing a baseline score changes the later trajectory. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31411102/)

### What remains unresolved

Several mechanisms can generate apparent remission:

- Lower intrinsic propensity to generate a tic or premonitory urge
- More efficient automatic or effortful suppression
- Changing learning, attention, social context or exposure to triggers
- Improved coping or reduced impairment despite residual tics
- Measurement missing infrequent, subtle or privately expressed tics

These are not mutually exclusive. Comparing persistently severe adults with healthy controls misses the crucial contrast between people who shared childhood liability but diverged in outcome. The project should therefore study **persistent, low-burden and objectively remitted trajectories**, with treatment history and repeated assessments, rather than treating adulthood or self-reported recovery as a binary proxy for biological repair.

## 3. Developmental biology: leads rather than validated remission mechanisms

**Caudate structure.** In 43 children scanned before age 14 and clinically followed about 7.5 years, smaller childhood caudate volumes were associated with worse later tic and OCD symptoms, although they did not correlate with concurrent childhood severity. Prospective timing is useful, but the modest sample, clinic recruitment, medication exposure and lack of demonstrated individual clinical calibration mean this is a candidate vulnerability marker, not a validated test or proof that enlarging the caudate would help. [Bloch et al., 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16247053/)

**Localized inhibitory compensation.** A 7-T MRS study in 15 adolescents with TS found elevated supplementary motor area GABA, related to lower local BOLD response and lower motor-cortex excitability. The authors proposed a compensatory tonic-inhibition mechanism. This is cross-sectional evidence: MRS concentration does not directly establish extracellular GABA, synaptic inhibition or causal remission. It is a useful hypothesis, not support for indiscriminately increasing GABA. [Draper et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4188813/)

**Age and measurement matter.** A separate 7-T study in younger children did not find the same GABA group differences; premotor glutamate was elevated. Age, comorbidity, anatomy and acquisition differences could explain discordance. The scientific response is harmonized longitudinal measurement, not selecting whichever neurotransmitter result fits a preferred story. [Mahone et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5815927/)

**Recent longitudinal network evidence.** Zito et al. studied 64 adolescents with TS and 61 controls; 57 participants with TS returned after approximately 15 months. Tic improvement correlated with increasing connectivity among pre-SMA, anterior cingulate and inferior frontal regions; other clinical changes had different cerebellar/network associations. The study appeared online September 2025 and in the April 2026 issue of Brain. Limitations matter: eligibility required the ability to control tics for at least ten minutes; suppression was not instructed during MRI, although voluntary suppression remained possible, medication exposure was common, healthy controls were not followed longitudinally, and associations between simultaneous changes do not establish which changed first. Its classifier accuracy was about 65%, not diagnostic-grade validation. [Primary paper](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/149/4/1278/8250701)

**Inference:** developmental improvement could include strengthened control over a persisting vulnerability. That is testable and potentially useful, but current evidence does not distinguish it cleanly from intrinsic normalization or their combination.

## 4. Sleep: a state-dependent experiment, not an established cure blueprint

### What the primary observations actually show

**Glaze et al., 1983.** Overnight recordings with video and accelerometry compared 14 untreated TS participants with 11 controls. Motor tics occurred across sleep stages; several sleep/arousal measures differed. The small, age-mixed sample and historical sleep scoring constrain estimates, but the existence finding directly contradicts universal cessation during sleep. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6573576/)

**Cohrs et al., 2001.** Twenty-five adults with TS and 11 controls underwent polysomnography plus simultaneous split-screen video to distinguish tics from ordinary movements. Tics occurred in all sleep stages and were more frequent in REM than non-REM in this sample. Daytime severity correlated with some sleep-fragmentation measures. This establishes that sleep and tic expression interact; correlation does not decide whether tics fragment sleep, arousal triggers tics, or shared physiology causes both. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11160467/)

**Kostanecka-Endress et al., 2003.** Seventeen unmedicated children with TS, without ADHD, were compared with 16 matched controls. Sleep latency, efficiency and wakefulness differed, and brief arousal-related movements increased, but several REM/slow-wave measures did not. The study helps separate some medication/ADHD confounding, while its size and selected population limit generalization. No significant correlation was found between the reported sleep measures and daytime tic severity. [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12842228/)

A UCLA pilot registered four weeks of forehead temperature-regulating therapy for adults with TS and insomnia, with sleep as the primary focus and tic severity exploratory. The existence of a study is not proof of efficacy; the accessible institutional record does not establish a cure or published positive result. [Institutional trial record, NCT05499741](https://ucla.clinicaltrials.researcherprofiles.org/trial/NCT05499741)

### Critical distinctions for a sleep workstream

- Sleep comprises changing states; a nightly average discards informative transitions and brief arousals.
- Recorded movements require expert adjudication. Tics, stereotypies, periodic limb movements, ordinary twitches and parasomnias should not be merged by an automatic movement counter.
- Actigraphy alone cannot reliably identify tics or replace full sleep staging.
- Lower movement during a state could reflect motor-output inhibition without resolution of tic-generating vulnerability.
- Better next-day function from better sleep would be worthwhile even if the core tic mechanism remains unchanged.
- A mechanism that produces less movement through sedation or cognitive slowing would not meet a goal of preserved alert, voluntary function.

**Best near-term question:** which physiological changes precede a reduction or re-emergence of expertly identified tics within the same person, after distinguishing stable sleep from microarousals and measuring ordinary movement in parallel? This is narrower and more falsifiable than asking how to reproduce sleep permanently.

## 5. Current biological discoveries that could inform target selection

**Common genetic risk is distributed.** A GWAS meta-analysis of 9,619 tic-disorder cases and 981,048 controls reported enrichment in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical regions and relevant neuronal classes. Its genome-wide lead hit did not replicate in the independent sample. Risk-associated tissue enrichment helps prioritize mechanisms; it does not identify a universally causal target, explain remission, or justify a gene-editing cure claim. [Primary paper, 2025 issue](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12036792/)

**Rare variants offer stronger causal handles but narrower generalizability.** A 2010 family study identified a rare loss-of-function HDC mutation. Follow-up human/mouse work linked histamine deficiency to altered dopamine-related physiology and tic-like behaviors. This supports histamine–dopamine interactions in a rare etiologic context, not the proposition that all TS is a histamine deficiency. Rodent stereotypy and sensorimotor-gating phenotypes cannot fully model human urges, complex tics or developmental recovery. [Family study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20445167/); [translational primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24411733/)

**Human developmental models.** Basal-ganglia organoids from five adults with nonremitted TS, compared with control donors, showed altered regional patterning and reduced differentiation of GABAergic/cholinergic interneurons, with SHH/GLI/cilia-related changes. This is a plausible developmental mechanism and a platform for controlled perturbation, not proof of an in-vivo causal lesion or an available repair therapy. Donor number is the biological sample size; multiple lines and organoids do not create independent people. Remitted donors were not the key comparison. [Brady et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949887/)

**Postmortem multiomics.** A 2025 study examined six adult TS and six matched control striata using single-nucleus RNA and chromatin-accessibility assays. Interneuron proportions were reduced by roughly half, accompanied by neuronal metabolic/synaptic signals and microglial immune activation. Severe, persistent adult cases, lifetime treatment, tissue-quality effects and small donor numbers limit causal interpretation. Microglial activation does not establish that an immune process initiated TS or that immune suppression will modify it. These samples also cannot tell us what changed in people whose tics remitted. [Published primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2024.12.022)

**Inference:** studies of inherited liability, early cellular development and adult persistent illness are beginning to converge on circuitry and inhibitory cell populations. The missing bridge is which features cause ongoing symptoms, which are compensations or consequences, and which distinguish remission from persistence.

## 6. Symptom control versus disease modification in actual trials

**CBIT:** A randomized, assessor-blinded trial assigned 126 participants aged 9–17 to eight behavioral sessions or supportive therapy/education. At ten weeks, 52.5% versus 18.5% were much/very much improved, with a tic-severity effect size of 0.68. Most available behavioral responders retained benefit at six months, but responder follow-up and booster sessions do not establish an altered decades-long natural history. This is strong evidence for useful symptom management, not evidence that tics are voluntary or that unsuccessful patients failed to try. [Piacentini et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2993317/)

**Ecopipam:** The phase 3 report, published online 26 May 2026 and in the July 2026 issue, enrolled 216 participants into an open-label phase and then randomized responders to continuing the selective D1 antagonist or tapering to placebo. The primary pediatric comparison included 90 participants; continued treatment reduced relapse risk (hazard ratio 0.47, 95% CI 0.26–0.84). The adult subgroup had only 14 randomized participants. This is responder-enriched evidence for maintenance of symptomatic efficacy over the studied interval, not an estimate of response in every patient or proof of durable off-drug disease modification. Reported adverse events included somnolence, anxiety, headache and insomnia; disclosed industry employment and financial relationships also matter for appraisal. The publication itself does not establish current regulatory approval. [Gilbert et al., 2026](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2849573)

**DBS:** A multinational registry enrolled 185 people with treatment-refractory TS. Substantial symptom improvements were reported, but this observational cohort lacked a randomized concurrent comparator; 56 of 158 people with relevant safety data experienced adverse events. Continued stimulation, heterogeneous targets, selection and incomplete data complicate mechanistic conclusions. This illustrates that changing circuit output can help without establishing eradication of the initiating vulnerability. [Martinez-Ramirez et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29340590/)

**Median-nerve stimulation:** A sham-controlled home trial found a larger four-week tic-score reduction with active stimulation, a promising symptomatic result. Separately, a 32-person crossover study found benefit during both rhythmic and arrhythmic stimulation and no demonstrated persistence after stimulation ended. The latter challenges a specific rhythmic-entrainment explanation without proving that all stimulation effects are placebo. A mechanism should survive an active comparator that preserves sensation/attention while removing its supposedly essential component. [Home trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37133932/); [mechanism-challenging crossover trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37048598/)

## 7. Prioritized falsifiable gaps

The following are proposed work packages. None is a proven cure route.

### A. Disentangle reduced tic drive from strengthened compensation

**Hypothesis:** people who improve develop measurable control changes before sustained reductions in tic burden, while some biological liability remains.

**Design concept:** a longitudinal cohort spanning the high-change developmental window, with repeated blinded videos, naturalistic sampling, suppression conditions, urge reports, effort/cognitive-load measures and harmonized neurophysiology. Include persistent, low-burden and remitted trajectories rather than severe cases and controls only. Clinical follow-up should continue through ordinary care; research should not require untreated suffering.

**Discriminating result:** a preregistered control marker prospectively predicts improvement in held-out participants beyond age, baseline severity, medication, comorbidities and context; temporal change precedes symptom change.

**Falsification/pivot:** the signal disappears after motion/context correction or predicts only consciously observed suppression, not naturalistic relief. Then treat it as a measurement/context marker rather than a remission target.

### B. Map state transitions instead of assuming a sleep off-switch

**Hypothesis:** a reproducible arousal or circuit-state feature changes tic probability beyond generic changes in movement.

**Design concept:** repeated natural-sleep video-polysomnography with EEG/EMG and blinded movement adjudication; analyze within-person transitions, temporal lead/lag and ordinary movement controls. Stratify medication, sleep disorders and relevant comorbidities. Avoid causal claims from nightly averages.

**Discriminating result:** a feature precedes tic-specific changes consistently across nights and replicates in another cohort.

**Falsification/pivot:** apparent suppression is explained by microarousal misclassification, reduced ordinary motor output or recording sensitivity. Preserve any useful sleep-health findings while dropping a tic-specific mechanism claim.

### C. Determine whether sleep treatment changes next-day tics or only parallel burden

**Hypothesis:** improving a defined sleep disturbance improves tic burden through a sleep-mediated pathway, rather than through expectancy, direct drug action or reduced distress alone.

**Design concept:** clinician-led randomized comparison of an appropriate sleep intervention with a credible control; stable background care; sleep, tic, alertness and functioning outcomes; mediation analyses planned before unblinding. This requires formal ethics approval and is not a self-experiment proposal.

**Discriminating result:** improved sleep precedes and statistically mediates independently rated tic improvement, with preserved daytime function. Statistical mediation and temporal ordering alone do not establish a causal pathway: mediator–outcome confounding and direct intervention effects require sensitivity analyses and alternative-mechanism checks. Replication matters more than a single significant mediation estimate.

**Falsification/pivot:** sleep improves but tic scores do not, or tic changes precede sleep changes. That would still justify treating insomnia on its own merits, but would not support sleep as a disease-modifying TS mechanism.

### D. Find recovery biology, not only risk biology

**Hypothesis:** protective developmental processes distinguish remission from persistence among people with similar initial liability.

**Design concept:** link longitudinal phenotype cohorts to consented genetics and cellular models. Compare well-characterized remitted and persistent donors, and test candidate changes with matched or isogenic controls when appropriate. Separate donor effects from repeated cell-line measurements.

**Discriminating result:** a candidate pathway associates with prospective human outcome and a targeted perturbation rescues a reproducible, disease-relevant functional phenotype without broadly impairing neuronal function.

**Falsification/pivot:** rescue affects only generic cell survival, appears in one donor, or is equally present in controls. Do not promote cell-count normalization alone to clinical target validation.

### E. Build a trial architecture that can recognize durable change

**Hypothesis:** a candidate can produce sustained improvement beyond its acute action.

**Design concept:** preregister the causal claim, target engagement, symptom/impairment endpoints and a suitable long-term comparator. Account for spontaneous improvement and repeated measurements. Any withdrawal or altered care must be ethical, clinician-managed and justified; disease-modification research must not entail arbitrary treatment cessation.

**Discriminating result:** durable clinical separation after acute effects have reasonably resolved, supported by convergent mechanism evidence and no compensating harm to cognition, mood, voluntary movement or participation.

**Falsification/pivot:** benefit occurs only while the intervention is active, or only on a tic-count metric while overall function deteriorates. Report it honestly as symptomatic, ineffective or harmful according to the complete results.

## 8. Measurement and analysis requirements shared by every workstream

1. Prespecify YGTSS total tic score versus total score including impairment; these are not interchangeable scales.
2. Track pain/injury, urges, suppression effort, school/work/social participation and quality of life, alongside observed tics and important comorbidities.
3. Sample repeatedly across contexts; a single short visit can confuse waxing/waning with response or remission.
4. Standardize whether participants are instructed to suppress. Record attention, observation and reward conditions rather than silently mixing them.
5. Use independent blinded raters, explicit missing-data and attrition analyses, and an external replication plan.
6. For imaging, treat motion and recruitment of people able to suppress during scanning as core biases; prospective prediction is harder than classifying established cases.
7. Separate a predictive biomarker from a causal therapeutic target. Separate target engagement from clinical benefit.
8. Validate any automated video/wearable measure against expert labels across tic types, ages and settings before using it as the main endpoint; preserve privacy and consent.
9. Treat age, sex, ancestry, ADHD/OCD, sleep disorders and treatment exposure as meaningful sources of heterogeneity, not merely nuisance variables to remove.
10. Publish negative results and preserve a record of what would invalidate each mechanistic claim.

## Bottom line

Natural developmental improvement is a credible discovery opportunity. Sleep-state changes are a credible mechanistic probe. Neither justifies a universal off-switch model or a cure promise. The highest-value foundation is a longitudinal, carefully measured comparison of persistence, low burden and genuine remission, connected to reversible state changes and causal model-system work. A project becomes stronger when it can distinguish what merely suppresses expression from what durably changes vulnerability, while treating meaningful symptom relief as a legitimate achievement in its own right.
