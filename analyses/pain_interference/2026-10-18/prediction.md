# Pre-extraction decision rule

Recorded 18 October 2026, before inspecting the Taylor 2022 primary article.

Question: Does the public aggregate report support an adult-specific, time-defined numerical estimate of tic-related pain interference, separate from recruitment-selected pain experience and qualitative themes?

Prediction: The report will support describing pain-related interference experienced by respondents, but its mixed 16–71-year sample and probable ascertainment constraints will not identify representative adult prevalence or a persistent-severe-adult effect.

Disconfirming evidence: A documented age ≥18 subgroup with outcome-specific denominators and numerical interference outcomes, an explicit common recall window, and pain-unselected ascertainment would weaken the predicted measurement limitation. Representative prevalence additionally requires a defensible population sampling frame and accounting for response/missingness. A positive numerical adult subgroup alone would not establish representative prevalence.

Method: Inspect primary methods, questionnaire wording, supplement, numerical tables and source dates; distinguish recruitment, entry eligibility, response/analysis flow, recall windows, qualitative themes and numerical outcomes. Check for linked corrections. Reproduce only well-defined published aggregate arithmetic where useful; no invented inputs, individual quotations or participant data. Retain contradictory or negative evidence. Compare design and measurement to the October 17 Małek audit without pooling.

Stopping rule: If adult-only outcome numerators/denominators and age-stratified interference summaries are absent, record precisely what public aggregate reporting is missing and stop adult effect estimation. No participant-data request, outreach, intervention or general prevalence extrapolation.
