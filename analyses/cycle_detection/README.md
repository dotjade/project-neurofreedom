# SYNTHETIC cycle-detection benchmark

Project NeuroFreedom · executed 8 October 2026

**Methods validation using generated numbers only. This is not a biological result, an analysis of anyone's tics, a medical device, or a validated clinical sampling protocol.** No patient records, family information, videos, medications or personal observations were used. Time steps and scores have arbitrary units; they do not stand for days, visits or a clinical rating scale.

## What was actually done

Ran 240 independently seeded replicate trajectories per scenario, seven scenarios, and three training-history lengths: 5,040 history/forecast evaluations. The same replicate is reused across scenarios and lengths to provide matched comparisons; these 5,040 evaluations are not 5,040 independent subjects. Eleven implementation checks pass. The lightweight archive includes the script, tests, aggregated results, seed, software versions and logs. All replicate-level errors and three PNG plots were generated and checked locally; they are regenerated by the command below and may be omitted from the archive.

This bounded analysis tests whether a visually or statistically appealing repeating fit continues to predict future observations, and which alternative explanations remain possible. It does not estimate how frequently tic waxing/waning is periodic.

## Main findings in this simulator

- **Correlated fluctuations often looked rhythmic in short histories.** With 96 observations, 76.7% of autocorrelated, nonperiodic trajectories met the illustrative apparent-cycle flag. The harmonic model then had 92.3% higher average error on the untouched next 48 observations than the nonperiodic baseline chosen using past-only validation. At 384 observations, that flag occurred in 5.0%; harmonic forecast error was still 14.4% higher.
- **A stable injected rhythm was recoverable under these chosen assumptions.** For a true 48-step sinusoid plus noise, harmonic forecasting reduced held-out mean squared error by 45.7% with a 96-step history and 62.8% with a 384-step history relative to the past-selected nonperiodic baseline. The 95% Monte Carlo bootstrap intervals for these improvements are in the output summary. The sinusoid lies inside the model's searched period range, an intentionally favorable condition. These numbers compare candidate forecasts; the past-only overall selector chose harmonic in only 23.3% of 96-step histories and achieved an 8.8% error reduction there, versus selecting harmonic in 100% of 384-step histories. Short-history model choice remains unreliable even when a true oscillator was injected.
- **More reporting noise weakened evidence.** Tripling independent reporting-noise standard deviation from 0.6 to 1.8 reduced corresponding forecasting improvements to 13.6% and 25.5%. The arbitrary in-sample flag also missed many genuinely periodic synthetic series. It is not a calibrated diagnostic rule.
- **Prediction does not identify the source of a rhythm.** The fixed latent oscillator and periodic reporting-bias scenarios have exactly identical observed values, fits and prediction errors. Their latent trajectories differ. No analysis of that observation channel alone can distinguish these constructions.
- **A fixed-period model can fail when a genuine oscillator changes pace.** The drifting-period scenario had a 100% in-sample flag rate at history 192 but 48.8% worse future error than the past-selected baseline. Failure of a stationary sinusoidal model cannot establish the absence of all oscillatory mechanisms.
- **Comparator choice matters.** The harmonic model contains a linear trend. It sometimes beats the adaptively selected baseline even in nonperiodic scenarios, because baseline selection can make a poor choice with limited history. All six prespecified baseline errors are retained. The additional fixed-linear comparison isolates whether adding a sinusoid helps beyond the same trend component; see `comparator_sensitivity.png`. Against the fixed linear comparator, harmonic error was worse by 27.2% for the slow-trend scenario at 96 steps and 6.2% at 384; for reporting noise it was worse by 9.1% and 2.2%. Neither prediction gains nor bootstrap intervals are tests proving a biological oscillator.

The apparent-cycle flag is simply at least a 25% reduction in training residual sum of squares after adding a searched sinusoid to a linear trend. It was chosen for this illustration, not derived from clinical data or calibrated to a false-positive probability. Do not call its percentage a clinical false-positive rate or a p-value.

## Generative models

Every replicate starts with independent Gaussian random draws and a phase uniformly distributed on [0, 2π). Across scenarios, the same replicate uses matched draws.

Let A(t) and B(t) be stationary unit-variance AR(1) processes with coefficients 0.85 and 0.94, respectively:

    Z(0) ~ Normal(0, 1)
    Z(t) = rho * Z(t-1) + sqrt(1-rho^2) * eta(t)
    eta(t) ~ Normal(0, 1), independent over time

Let E(t) be independent standard Gaussian reporting noise, D(t) = 0.0025*t, and W(t) = 1.5*sin(2*pi*t/48 + phase). Observations are latent(t) + reporting_bias(t) + reporting_error(t).

| Scenario | Latent trajectory | Reporting bias | Reporting error |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed_period | D + 0.45*A + W | 0 | 0.6*E |
| autocorrelated | D + 1.3*B | 0 | 0.6*E |
| slow_trend | 0.012*t + 0.55*A | 0 | 0.6*E |
| reporting_noise | D | 0 | 1.5*E |
| noisy_fixed_period | D + 0.45*A + W | 0 | 1.8*E |
| drifting_period | D + 0.45*A + 1.5*sin(theta(t)) | 0 | 0.6*E |
| periodic_reporting_bias | D + 0.45*A | W | 0.6*E |

For the drifting oscillator, instantaneous period P(t) = 36 + 0.10*t and theta(t) = phase + (2*pi/0.10)*log((36+0.10*t)/36). These are hypothetical mathematical trajectories, not parameter estimates from Tourette research. Gaussian scores can be negative and are not intended to reproduce a bounded count or symptom scale.

## Forecast models and leakage controls

The harmonic candidate is ordinary least squares:

    y(t) = intercept + slope*t + a*sin(2*pi*t/P) + b*cos(2*pi*t/P) + error

Time is centered/scaled for numerical stability. P is chosen by minimum training residual sum of squares on a fixed grid from 24 to 96 in steps of 0.5. Each refit searches again using only its own training history. No future observations choose the period, coefficients, trend or baseline.

Six simple nonperiodic candidates are evaluated:

1. Full-history mean
2. Linear trend
3. AR(1) deviations around the full-history mean
4. AR(1) deviations around a fitted linear trend
5. Mean of the last 12 training observations
6. Last observation carried forward

For the two AR predictors, the empirical lag-one coefficient of centered/detrended observations is clipped to [0, 0.995], and residuals decay geometrically. These are deliberately transparent forecasting baselines; they are not latent-state inference models and do not correct AR estimates for measurement error.

For each outer history n, forecast the next 48 observations as one contiguous untouched block:

- n=96: inner validation trains on observations [0,48) and validates on [48,96)
- n=192: inner origins 96 and 144, each with a 48-observation validation block
- n=384: inner origins 288 and 336, each with a 48-observation validation block

The nonperiodic candidate with lowest average inner validation MSE is selected, then refit to the full outer history. The harmonic candidate's inner score also defines an optional overall model selector. Both candidates, that selector, and every fixed baseline are evaluated on the outer block. No shuffling, random pointwise split, future imputation, global detrending or future-based model selection occurs.

There is no temporal gap: this is forecasting immediately after the available past, where autocorrelation is part of the prediction task, rather than claiming independent observations across the boundary. The parameter-recovery/stability diagnostics are not part of selection.

The shortest series supplies only one inner validation block; this is an intentionally limited-data stress case. Stronger validation would need more independent future blocks and richer models. The outer blocks occur at different absolute times for different history lengths. Hence the comparison also changes the oscillator's phase of development in the nonstationary drifting-period scenario; do not treat that curve as an isolated causal effect of collecting more history.

## Metrics and uncertainty

- `fit_gain`: 1 - harmonic_training_SSE / linear_training_SSE, clipped to [0,1]. If linear SSE is within floating-point tolerance (100*machine_epsilon*max(1,sum(y^2))), gain is set to zero: an already perfectly constant/linear history has no incremental cyclic structure to explain
- `apparent_cycle`: fit_gain >= 0.25, an illustrative uncalibrated flag
- `harmonic_skill`: 1 - mean(harmonic_outer_MSE) / mean(past_selected_baseline_outer_MSE). Positive favors the harmonic model; negative means it predicts worse
- `skill_vs_linear`: same calculation against a prespecified fixed linear trend
- `harmonic_oos_win_rate`: fraction of replicate trajectories on which the harmonic model has lower outer MSE; chance wins are possible under null scenarios
- `inner_harmonic_selection_rate`: how often past-only validation selects harmonic over the baseline family
- `stable_half_period_rate`: periods fitted separately to nonoverlapping first/second halves differ by at most 10% of their mean, and neither hits a search-grid boundary
- `period_recovery_rate`: fitted period within 10% of 48, only reported where a 48-step observed component was injected
- `selected_mse_mean`: performance of the actual past-only overall selector, which can be worse than retrospectively knowing the true scenario

Intervals use 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the 240 independent replicate trajectories. Shared resampling indices preserve matching across scenarios and histories. Individual autocorrelated time points are never treated as independent bootstrap units. These intervals reflect Monte Carlo uncertainty under the specified simulator, not uncertainty about clinical populations, plausible parameter ranges or biological mechanisms. There is no significance test, calibrated detector, power claim or multiple-testing-adjusted biological claim.

## What observations would help discriminate explanations?

These are research-design implications to investigate, not instructions for an individual or a validated collection schedule:

- A stable-period hypothesis predicts continuing phase and timing in genuinely future windows. Repeated held-out forecast checks and stable estimates across separated windows are more informative than a selected peak in one short series.
- Competing correlated-noise, trend and time-varying models need to be tested on the same prespecified outcomes and future windows. Rejecting one narrow model is not identifying the correct mechanism.
- Independent measurement channels with different reporting biases would be needed to address the exact nonidentifiability demonstrated here. Agreement must be assessed while considering shared confounding and measurement reliability; corroboration is not automatically causal proof.
- Recording relevant contextual covariates and changes would allow future governed research to assess whether rhythm-like structure follows context or ascertainment. This benchmark contains no such covariates and cannot assess those explanations.
- Coverage spanning the phenomenon of interest and measurement quality both matter in the simulator. The arbitrary 48-step period and 96/192/384 histories cannot be converted into recommendations about a person, frequency of observations, or duration of clinical follow-up.

Before any real-data use: define an ethically approved question, consent and privacy arrangements, appropriate validated measures, burden limits, missingness mechanisms, held-out analysis plan and independent expert review. Do not change care, medication, sleep, stress or behavior to create an experiment from this benchmark.

## Reproduce

Tested with Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5 and Matplotlib 3.10.8; no additional packages were installed. From this directory:

    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m unittest -v test_simulate.py
    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python simulate.py --replicates 240 --seed 20261008

Defaults produce `outputs/`. An optional `--out /tmp/cycle-check` creates a separate rerun for comparison. Numerical output is expected to reproduce with the same software; cross-version BLAS differences can change tiny floating-point details. The script saves its SHA-256 and actual library versions in metadata. Plot byte identity is not the criterion for numerical reproducibility.

## Limits and next bounded work

The simulator is intentionally small: one main fixed period/amplitude, two AR coefficients, one period drift, regular sampling, additive Gaussian errors, no missingness, no count/ordinal observation model, no interventions, no multiple people/sites, no abrupt baseline shifts, and no external data. It does not survey all parameter regimes or calibrate a clinical detector. The model search uses a known bounded period range; real uncertainty about that range is larger. No results should be generalized beyond these assumptions.

A defensible next methods step would prespecify a small sensitivity grid for AR persistence, signal/noise ratio, variable periods, irregular or informative missingness, then compare a measurement-error-aware stochastic model and a time-varying oscillator on untouched simulator settings. Any real-data analysis is a separate task with separate governance and validation requirements.
