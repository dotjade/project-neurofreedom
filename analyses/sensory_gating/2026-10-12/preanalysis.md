# Adult somatosensory gating: prospectively stated analysis check

Recorded 12 October 2026, before new source retrieval and any new calculation. The study abstract and repository interpretation were already known. This is a dated analysis plan, not a registered study or a prediction made before the original result.

## Question and predicted pattern

The strong account under test is that a generalized deficit in suppressing repeated non-noxious tactile input characterizes adults with chronic tic disorder and explains greater self-reported tactile responsivity. In this paired-pneumatic paradigm, that account predicts less attenuation of the second response relative to the first in CTD than controls, and a relationship between poorer gating and greater tactile responsivity. Component-specific directions will be translated only after verifying the paper's exact index definitions; a smaller first-stimulus P50 alone is not the predicted gating deficit.

## Result that would weaken or disconfirm it

The already known abstract reports no group difference in gating indices and no association with responsivity. That fails to provide the expected positive evidence and weakens the strong account in this paradigm. More decisive disconfirmation of a quantitatively meaningful deficit would require a valid uncertainty interval excluding a defensible, independently justified deficit size, or a suitable equivalence test with its prespecified margin. Nonsignificance alone cannot provide this. No clinically or physiologically meaningful equivalence margin has yet been established for these indices; none will be invented after viewing the results.

## Extraction and analysis rules

1. Retrieve lawful public publisher, indexing or author-manuscript sources for PMID 42810670 / DOI 10.1016/j.biopsycho.2026.109408. Do not purchase, bypass access restrictions, contact authors or save full text.
2. Separate S1 and S2 component amplitudes, paired-response indices and clinical associations. Verify group/analysis counts, exact formulae, units, statistical model, covariates, multiplicity, missingness and exclusions.
3. Assess adult CTD phenotype, severity and functional burden, comorbidity and medication/treatment handling. Do not infer a severe persistent adult stratum from the adult diagnosis alone.
4. Compute an observed estimate and uncertainty only when published aggregate inputs and model information suffice. Record all inputs, formulae, assumptions, code and actual output. Do not infer SDs, within-person covariance, point estimates, exact p-values or participant data from a qualitative null statement.
5. If required quantities are unavailable, return a field-by-field missing-prerequisite record and narrower interpretation. Do not substitute sample-size-only power, an invented zero effect or a generic confidence interval for the study's precision.
6. Keep altered first-stimulus P50 as a distinct, cross-sectional signal that needs effect/uncertainty and multiplicity checks. It does not establish impaired repeated-stimulus gating, causation, treatment response or cure.

## Stopping condition

Complete a bounded source-access and extraction pass across official indexing, publisher and lawful institutional/manuscript routes. Stop when the required quantities have been extracted and reproducibly analyzed, or when the available sources demonstrably lack them. Further retrieval, institutional access, or author clarification is a prerequisite rather than a result. Do not repeat completed MNS work or reopen the closed generic adult-cohort search.
