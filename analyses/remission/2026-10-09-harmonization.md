# Developmental tic remission: four-study endpoint harmonization

9 October 2026 | Bounded primary-evidence extraction | Public scientific information only

## Result and hypothesis decision

**Supported in part:** changing ascertainment or the endpoint changes the apparent outcome within the same cohort. Consequently, low burden and persistent observable tics are logically compatible. **Not established:** ascertainment alone explains every difference between reported remission proportions. Different developmental windows, recruitment, treatment exposure and missing participants remain competing explanations. No pooled remission estimate, individual prognosis, disease-modification claim or cure inference is justified by this extraction.

The bounded test used these decision rules; this was not a preregistered review:

- **H1:** More sensitive ascertainment will find residual tics among some people classified as tic-free by interview or routine examination; low-impairment endpoints will classify more people favorably than an any-tic-absence endpoint.
- **Support criterion:** A discordance demonstrated within the same people, rather than an ecological comparison between studies.
- **Counterevidence criterion:** Consistent tic absence across history and sufficiently sensitive observation, or no difference between clinically meaningful burden and tic-presence outcomes.
- **Stronger H2:** All cross-study differences disappear after endpoint harmonization. The available aggregate data cannot test this stronger proposition. It remains unresolved, not confirmed.

## Source-level extraction

“Not established” means the retrieved source does not establish the requested property. Rounded percentages are retained when an original numerator was not reported; no reconstructed count is silently substituted.

### 1. Pappert et al., 2003: childhood-to-adult tertiary-clinic cohort

[Original paper, institutional-hosted full text](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/docs/default-source/members/devolved-nations/rcpsych-in-wales/wales-cap-objective-assess-longit-outcomes.pdf?sfvrsn=d52dec25_2), pp 937–939, Methods, Results and table. DOI:10.1212/01.WNL.0000086370.10186.7C; PMID: 14557563.

- Sample: 56 eligible childhood recordings; 36 adults located; 31 re-examined. Mean ages 12.2→24.2 years; approximately 12 years between assessments.
- Ascertainment: randomly selected five-minute seated recordings; one external neurologist, blinded to the hypothesis, rated randomized segments. Age/timepoint blinding was not established. Subjective adult ratings and recalled childhood ratings were separate.
- Presence endpoint: 90% of 31 retained observable tics. Half of those calling themselves tic-free had video tics; that subgroup’s denominator was not explicitly supplied. Do not label the remaining proportion durable remission.
- Improvement: modified Rush composite mean 9.58→7.52, p=.014; ten participants worsened objectively.
- Treatment: tic medication 81% in childhood versus 13% in adulthood; no randomized treatment comparison or medication-free-remission analysis.
- Function: reported social/educational dysfunction 52%→32%; tic severity was not interchangeable with function.
- Durability/recurrence: no repeated adult remission surveillance. Short recording sensitivity and tertiary selection remain limitations; baseline nonsignificance between retained/lost participants cannot establish absence of attrition bias.

### 2. Groth et al., 2017: Danish clinic cohort

[Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28335874/). DOI:10.1016/j.jaac.2017.01.010. **Journal full text not verified in this run.** The investigator’s [2018 doctoral thesis](https://content.ugeskriftet.dk/sites/default/files/scientific_article_files/2018-08/b5465.pdf), especially pp 5, 7–8, 11, 14–15, supplies additional primary cohort reporting; this is not another independent cohort.

The abstract reports 314 baseline participants, 227 reassessed about six years later; above age 16, 17.7% no tics, 59.5% minimal/mild, 22.8% moderate/severe. Yearly YGTSS decline 0.80 points, CI 0.58–1.01. The abstract does not expose the headline proportion’s denominator.

Additional thesis extraction:

- Follow-up median 5.6 years, range 4–8; 212 clinic and 15 telephone assessments. Nonparticipation: 17 unlocated, 46 declined, 16 unable, 8 severe comorbidities.
- Previous-week YGTSS total tic score: absent 0; minimal 1–9; mild 10–19; moderate 20–39; severe ≥40. Clinician interview/observation and reported symptoms; no standardized blinded video ascertainment established.
- Tic analyses pool visits, including later reassessment if neuropsychological testing occurred >6 months after follow-up. Medication present in 94/518 tic assessments; untreated and treated observations combined.
- **Denominator audit:** Table 3’s 16–20 and 21–26 age bands total 237 assessments; 42 absent and 141 minimal/mild reconstruct 17.7% and 59.5%. Its overall columns contain inconsistent totals and a severe row is absent. These are **provisional reconstructions**, not verified journal numerators or unique-person counts.
- Function: among 204 paired impairment assessments, 28.4% worsened, 54.4% stable, 17.2% improved. DAWBA comorbidity 63.0%=92/146, not a proportion verified in all 227.
- Sustained absence, recurrence and medication-free remission remain unestablished.

### 3. Burd et al., 2001: epidemiologically ascertained cohort

[Publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/088307380101600609); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11417610/). DOI:10.1177/088307380101600609. **Abstract only; full-text access restricted.**

- Recruitment: North Dakota school-age prevalence cohort; 73 original cases, 54 eligible, 39 interviewed as adults. Follow-up 507 person-years; 507/39=13 years per interviewed participant is arithmetic, not a verified distribution.
- Endpoint: 44% described as “essentially symptom free.” Exact tic-absence threshold, required duration and numerator are not supplied in the abstract. This cannot be recoded as observed zero tics.
- Ascertainment: follow-up interviews; structured observation, assessor masking and interview modality not established from the abstract.
- Improvement: cohort tic severity declined 59%; global functioning improved 50%; comorbidity count declined 42%. The scales, absolute changes and uncertainty are unavailable here.
- Treatment: 22% on medication as adults; indication and remission-by-treatment cross-tabulation unavailable.
- Recurrence, sustained untreated absence and reasons for attrition unestablished. The population-based starting point differs from tertiary-clinic selection; follow-up losses still limit generalization.

### 4. Kim et al., 2019: recent-onset New Tics cohort

[Original open-access paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-40133-4.pdf), pp 2–4 and 8–9, Tables 1–2; [author-hosted copy](https://greenelab.ucsd.edu/media/papers/s41598-019-40133-4.pdf). DOI:10.1038/s41598-019-40133-4; PMID: 30850688. **Full text inspected.**

- 43 enrolled; 39 returned; all four losses had ADHD. Mean entry age 8.13 years, 3.3 months after onset. Follow-up was 12 months **after onset**, not baseline. Three had possible earlier episodes.
- History plus remote observation found tics in 39/39; 38/39 showed video tics alone. At least 9/28 had none during clinical examination; 3/29 children/parents reported none during the previous week but video detected them.
- Low burden: 37/39 had YGTSS impairment ≤10. TTS 17.21→14.15; PedsQL total 83.84→85.34, p=.39. Diagnostic classification also changed with impairment/duration criteria: 6/39 met DSM-IV TS, whereas 39/39 met DSM-5 TS/chronic motor tic disorder.
- Medication row: 5/43 baseline, 3/39 retained participants **at baseline**; follow-up cell blank. It is not evidence that medication fell 5→3 over time.
- One child reported an intervening asymptomatic period of months. No lifelong recurrence assessment.
- This selected recent-onset cohort is not an adult TS-remission cohort. Observer masking for these clinical outcome classifications was not established.

## What the comparison resolves, and what it does not

1. **H1 receives direct within-cohort support.** Pappert and Kim contain self-report/observation discordance. Kim additionally permits a same-visit distinction between residual tics and little reported impairment. These observations support separate symptom-presence and burden endpoints; they do not devalue a person’s experience of improvement.
2. **An apparent absence is always conditional on the measurement.** A negative brief video, an uneventful clinical visit, a previous-week score of zero, and a long asymptomatic interval answer different questions. None alone proves eradication of tic susceptibility. Conversely, one elicited or observed tic does not establish continuous symptoms, disability, or need for treatment.
3. **Between-cohort reconciliation remains partial.** Burd’s endpoint cannot yet be translated into a video-confirmed absence rule. Groth’s published percentages need an original-table denominator check. No individual-level recoding adjusts all four cohorts for recruitment, age, time since onset, observation context, medication and attrition.
4. **Severity-category boundaries are not automatically comparable.** The New Tics report uses TTS ≤10 for minimal severity and 11–20 for mild at baseline; Groth’s thesis uses 1–9 and 10–19. Functional impairment is another scale entirely. A shared instrument name does not guarantee a shared estimand.
5. **No common durable-remission endpoint was recoverable.** None of the extracted comparisons establishes repeatedly confirmed, medication-free absence over a common prespecified interval. Recurrence cannot be estimated by subtracting a one-visit remission percentage from 100.

The evidence weakens a claim that these proportions directly contradict one another. It does **not** establish the stronger claim that the numerical differences are completely explained or that there is a single true remission rate hidden underneath them.

## Reproducible arithmetic and exclusions

Retention, calculated from the extracted recruitment counts: Pappert 31/56=55.4% of eligible versus 31/36=86.1% of located; Groth 227/314=72.3%; Burd 39/54=72.2%; Kim 39/43=90.7%. These are retention descriptions, not bias adjustments.

Kim’s observed persistence in all returnees is compatible with between 39/43 and 43/43 persistence in the enrolled cohort if the four missing outcomes are unrestricted. This is a missing-outcome identification range, not a confidence interval or population estimate. The paper reports a 95% CI of 78.4–96.3% for the conservative 39/43 calculation. Do not combine these different types of uncertainty.

No weighted average, binomial interval for Groth’s possibly repeated assessments, inferred Burd numerator, remission mechanism analysis or new participant data were produced.

## Evidence-maintenance actions recommended

- Upgrade Pappert’s retrieval field to full text inspected; retain the hypothesis-blinding limitation and eligible/located/retained denominators.
- Qualify Groth’s 17.7% as a reported age-category proportion with original denominator verification outstanding. Record the author-thesis reconstruction separately, not as an established 227-person result. Clarify 63% refers to 146 DAWBA respondents.
- Keep Burd’s “essentially symptom free” wording tied to its abstract-only status; do not call it objectively established complete remission.
- Add Kim with separate clinical-examination, remote-observation, history, impairment and diagnostic endpoints; preserve field-specific missingness.
- Link overlapping cohort reports before future synthesis. Groth 2019 is a later report from the Danish cohort. The expanded New Tics report, [Grossen et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11373539/), overlaps the 2019 cohort and must not be counted as independent replication. It warrants separate full extraction rather than silently replacing these denominators.
