## New weekly question

Does counting the ONLINE-TICS results publication, Wilhelm 2012 primary/maintenance publication and McGuire 2019-online/2020-issue functioning publication as three independent confirmations preserve their actual recruitment and inferential structure?

Prediction: three result publications resolve to two independently recruited randomized trial clusters. Secondary functioning and responder maintenance observations do not create new randomizations. Each trial's later favorable outcomes cannot automatically be promoted to replicated, randomized, treatment-free functional benefit.

The disconfirming result for the report-dependence prediction would be verified distinct randomization/cohort identifiers and recruitment for all three publications. The disconfirming result for an individual inferential restriction would be a source showing the required randomized comparison, valid testing hierarchy, population and exposure window actually existed.

## Fixed inclusion boundary and rules

- Include only the three results publications already audited on October 14–16: Haas et al. ONLINE-TICS 2022; Wilhelm et al. 2012; McGuire et al. (2019 online, 2020 issue). Trial protocols and registry entries identify the clusters but are not additional result reports.
- Normalize DOI case and prefix. A publication is one DOI regardless of electronic/issue date, title variation, manuscript version, source URL or number of repository dossiers.
- Cluster by verified trial registration and explicit same-trial methods, not author, treatment name, shared instrument or apparent similarity. Distinct registrations alone are not definitive when methods show reuse; record such conflicts rather than silently splitting. For these two independently recruited trials, recruitment place/time corroborates distinct clusters.
- Extract a small fixed set of outcome inference blocks: ONLINE-TICS acute primary tic test, later tic tests, impairment, disease-specific quality of life and gated non-inferiority; Wilhelm acute primary tic test, acute impairment and selected responder maintenance; McGuire randomized acute functional tests, responder-functional associations and selected functional follow-up. Each block may contain several correlated tests. Block counts are accounting, not evidence weights or independent endpoints.
- Keep test role, comparison type, selection, treatment exposure, nominal positives, inferential status and population applicability separate. Store null findings as failure to demonstrate a difference, never as equivalence. Never infer missing CIs, outcomes, treatment discontinuation or adult-only estimates.
- The two primary contrasts are the only comparable preselected binary primary-status count; do not pool their effect sizes or label a report fraction as an efficacy rate.
- A broader claim of replicated randomized treatment-free functional benefit requires at least two independent trial clusters with source-verified positive functional comparison, retained randomized inference at that follow-up, and verified absence of continuing treatment. Unverified requirements cannot pass. Failure of this documentation predicate is absence of support in this bounded set, not proof the treatment cannot help.
- Positive control: the source-supported acute tic-severity benefit in Wilhelm 2012 must remain recognized. Empirical counterexample to automatic cross-domain inference: randomized functional tests may not establish superiority even within a trial showing acute tic benefit. Synthetic unit controls: duplicating a URL/date/report version must not create a trial; adding a genuinely distinct hypothetical trial identifier/recruitment must create a cluster. The synthetic controls are software checks only.

## Deliverables and limits

Original small aggregate inputs, source identifiers/locators, deterministic Python code (standard library), actual output, tests and a report. No random seed applies; there is no simulation, participant data, pooled effect, meta-analysis, causal estimate, new clinical result or comprehensive review. No previously completed daily p-value or attrition analysis is repeated as a new finding.

Clarification before reporting: the exposure-cessation predicate concerns verification of cessation of structured platform/booster exposure, not an instruction to stop treatment or an attempt to erase learned behavioral skills. It tests an unusually strong claim, not whether ongoing-access benefit is worthwhile or whether behavioral therapy has efficacy.
