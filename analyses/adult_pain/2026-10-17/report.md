# Adult tic-related pain: measurement windows and an unresolved care denominator

Project NeuroFreedom | 17 October 2026 | Historical primary-study audit

## Decision

This study supports treating tic-related pain as a distinct, directly measured adult burden outcome. It does **not** identify representative adult pain prevalence, a severe-persistent-adult subgroup, a causal treatment effect, or a reliable percentage of treatment seekers who benefited.

The most consequential finding is a reporting conflict: **56/76 respondents reported past-or-present pain**, while the help-seeking section says **86%** but gives counts of **19 men and 31 women**, totaling 50. Only 30 women were in the pain subgroup. Ten people reported relief; that is 17.9% of the 56-person pain subgroup, but 20% of the asserted 50 seekers. The stated **18% of seekers** is therefore not reproducible from the supplied counts. No corrected denominator is adopted.

The [prospective question](prespecified_question.md) is supported at the measurement/estimand level: current versus worst pain and pain-group versus care-seeker denominators are not interchangeable. Clinical materiality of a current–worst difference remains unestablished, and conflicting source summaries prevent a definitive pooled estimate. These are findings about what the study can support, not evidence that participants' pain or unmet needs are unimportant.

## Who was sampled, and what was measured

The convenience sample comprised **76 adults aged 18–51** attending support groups in several Polish locations: 38 men and 38 women, 57 with Tourette syndrome and 19 with chronic motor tic disorder. Prior neurologist diagnosis under DSM-5 was an eligibility requirement; independent re-diagnosis/chart verification was not described. Chronic vocal tic disorder had no representation. There was no formal sample-size calculation. Numbers invited, declining, partially responding, and item-specific missingness were not reported. Current support-group attendees who volunteered may differ from other adults, including people unable to attend or not seeking support. These limitations cannot be repaired by a binomial confidence interval.

Questionnaires were completed on paper, without a time limit. An online one-to-one YGTSS interview followed within a week. This is a cross-sectional measurement exercise with recalled past experiences, not a prospective pain trajectory or a treatment trial. The roughly two-decade average disorder duration does not establish persistently severe tics throughout adulthood. No severe-state threshold, duration criterion or severe-adult subgroup effect was supplied. Comorbidities were self-reported rather than screened or independently confirmed. [Source: methods and results, sections 2–3.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full)

Important construct distinctions:

- The reported **YGTSS 0–100 score combines tics and impairment**. It is not the isolated 0–50 total tic score. Component scores were not supplied, so a correlation with this total cannot be interpreted as an association with pure tic severity alone.
- **VAS present pain and worst pain, 0–10**, are separate self-reported quantities. The worst-pain recall period was not specified. Neither typical/average pain nor a bounded past-week current prevalence was established.
- **SF-MPQ-2** supplies pain descriptors and domains. Reported domain scoring/denominators are insufficiently explained for a new pooled domain analysis. Neuropathic-style descriptors do not alone diagnose nerve injury or establish a pain mechanism.
- **CSQ** describes coping strategy use and separate subjective control/reduction items; **BPCQ** describes beliefs about control. Neither measures the causal effectiveness of those strategies or care.
- The blank care questionnaire permits **yes, no and uncertain/cannot remember** responses to help-seeking and perceived relief, and multiple providers/treatments. It supplies no bounded recall window or minimum duration of benefit. Thus unanswered/uncertain/non-positive responses cannot all be counted as treatment failures.

No validated pain-interference change, pain-specific treatment response, disease-specific quality-of-life benefit or durable benefit is established by these measures in this study. [Source: methods and supplementary questionnaire.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full#supplementary-material)

## Present versus recalled worst pain

Among the 56 endorsing past-or-present pain, Table 3 reports:

| Quantity | Men, n=26: mean (SD); median | Women, n=30: mean (SD); median | Between-gender p |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Present VAS | 3.3 (1.3); 3.0 | 2.6 (1.8); 2.5 | .092 |
| Worst VAS | 4.3 (1.5); 4.0 | 4.3 (2.1); 4.0 | .743 |

The p-values compare genders, **not current versus worst pain**. There was also no nominal gender difference within either diagnosis. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Recalled worst values are expected to differ from a present rating; this is not evidence of recovery, deterioration, waxing/waning, treatment benefit, or a clinically important difference. The paper does not provide paired covariance, a relevant meaningful-change threshold, or a duration-defined pain endpoint. Its sample pain proportion, 73.7%, explicitly concerns **past or present** pain, not the number currently experiencing pain. [Source: section 4 and Table 3.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full)

A source conflict prevents accepting all displayed means simultaneously. For women, the diagnosis groups are 20 with GTS and 10 with CMTD. Their worst-pain means of 4.6 and 4.2 imply a weighted displayed mean of **4.4667**, inconsistent with the stated female total of **4.3**. Under conventional one-decimal rounding, the subgroup-weighted range is 4.4167–4.5167, while the total range is 4.25–4.35; they do not overlap. These are deterministic rounding ranges, not confidence intervals. The correct replacement quantity is unknown.

The computation also shows what follows **conditional on the printed sex-total rows**: weighted present VAS 2.925, worst VAS 4.3, and arithmetic difference 1.375. That difference has a rounding-only range of 1.275–1.475 under the stated assumptions. These values are retained for reproducibility of the audit, not promoted as a corrected study result or patient-level change. The female worst-pain conflict remains attached to them.

## Care-seeking and perceived usefulness

Section 9's numerical claims cannot all be true under the stated subgroup structure:

1. The pain group is 56 people: 26 men and 30 women.
2. The reported help-seeking counts total 50, which is 89.29% of 56, not 86%; the reported 31 women exceed the female pain subgroup.
3. If 86% were conventionally rounded from an integer count out of 56, **48** would be compatible. This is an arithmetic scenario, **not a recovered or corrected count**.
4. The ten relief reports correspond to **13.16% of all 76 respondents**, **17.86% of the 56 reporting pain**, **20.00% of the asserted 50 seekers**, or **20.83% of the hypothetical 48**. These are different, conditional quantities. None is an established treatment response rate.

The denominator changes whether the arithmetic is below, equal to, or above one in five. This matters for the abstract's wording, but the small numerical differences are not evidence of a clinically important difference. Treatment receipt, multiple treatment use, missing/uncertain answers and corrected subgroup membership remain unresolved. The survey's option to report uncertain benefit also prevents relabelling everybody else as a treatment failure.

Reported professional relief was described as temporary or insufficient, without a duration distribution. No specialized pain-clinic use was reported. Provider percentages are multiple-response categories with uncertain denominator; they are not mutually exclusive treatment arms. The paper supplies no randomized allocation, untreated comparison, standardized exposure, baseline–follow-up pain outcome, treatment-specific response denominator or counterfactual. **Perceived usefulness and unmet-care experience must not be converted into causal ineffectiveness of professional care or superiority of self-management.** [Source: section 9 and blank care questionnaire.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full)

## Negative findings and reporting limits retained

Table 4 reports present-pain/YGTSS rho=.540 and worst-pain/YGTSS rho=.363 among 30 women, marked p<.01 and p<.05. In 26 men, the corresponding coefficients are .189 and .179 and unmarked. Female GTS current pain is marked (.577), but female GTS worst pain is not (.153). No test of the difference between gender-specific coefficients is supplied; significance in one group and not another does not show an interaction. Cross-sectional associations cannot establish that tic reduction causes pain reduction, especially when YGTSS also includes impairment.

Other counterevidence is preserved: no reported significant pain-intensity difference by self-reported depression; no reported coping-strategy/YGTSS association; no reported BPCQ/pain-intensity association; and no nominal gender differences for the four pain-domain or three belief-domain comparisons. Some coping-strategy contrasts were nominally significant, but strategy use is not tested efficacy, multiplicity control is not described, and causal or general gender conclusions do not follow. No one reported complete subjective ability to reduce pain, which is separate from the control item. [Source: sections 4 and 6–8, Tables 4–7.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full)

Additional concerns limit numerical reuse without overturning the existence of reported burden:

- Table 1 labels ADHD count 10 as 7.6% and sleep-disorder count 20 as 15.2%, neither matching n=76. The female-pain percentage is absent.
- Overall YGTSS SD=16.00 cannot be reconciled with the displayed complete 38/38 sex partition and SDs 19.31/18.67. No corrected SD is adopted.
- Table 5's descriptor percentages and domain-score formulas lack clear respondent denominators. Item cells are not reconstructed or republished; possible singleton attribution is avoided. An additional displayed score-sum discrepancy is noted in the independent review.
- Some correlation symbols are rendered as “p” in HTML/XML while resembling rho in the PDF. They should be treated as coefficient/encoding ambiguity rather than silently interpreted as exact p-values. The reported positive .441 also conflicts with an inverse-direction verbal interpretation. No direction or significance is repaired.
- BPCQ subscale scoring is not documented well enough here to validate rankings of raw subscale totals. A version-specific range concern raised by independent review remains unresolved rather than asserted as a confirmed scoring violation.

Reported uncertainty consists of SDs, medians, selected ranges, rounded p-values and coefficient significance symbols. No confidence intervals for pain prevalence, current–worst differences, correlations or care response are supplied. The small convenience sample, unresolved missingness and multiple exploratory comparisons limit inference. This audit does not manufacture uncertainty intervals, individual values, severe-case counts or causal mechanisms.

Independent review is supplied separately by the parent publication workflow. These checks validate arithmetic and source fidelity, not the underlying study or clinical claims. No simulation or participant data were used.

Close this bounded source audit unless a public correction or new methodological documentation resolves the missing quantities. For a future pain-focused research estimand, keep separate: a stated current/average/worst time window, pain intensity, pain interference, body sites, self-attribution to tics, tic severity/components, impairment, treatment exposure and adequately timed benefit. This is a measurement requirement, not a newly validated instrument or instruction to collect data.

A distinct next bounded task is to audit **pain interference and ascertainment** in [Taylor, Anderson and Davies 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.914044), [PMID 35990066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35990066/). Verify recruitment, whether pain was required for entry, interference questions/time windows, adult-specific availability and the distinction between qualitative themes and numeric prevalence. The verified age range is 16–71, so an adult-only or severe-adult estimate must not be assumed. Stop with an explicit missing-prerequisite decision if adult-specific pain-interference estimates are unavailable; do not pool unlike samples, reuse identifiable quotations, initiate outreach or collect participant data. This is a next-source identity check, not a second completed analysis today.
