# Adult tic-related pain: prospective bounded question

Recorded 17 October 2026, before retrieval/extraction of the target primary paper's full text.

## Question and prediction

Target: Małek 2025, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088; PMID 40808742. The previously completed CBIT report identified this source and its publication date but supplied no pain results.

Prediction: current pain and recalled worst pain, and percentages using all eligible respondents versus treatment users/seekers, describe materially different estimands. Conflating them can change conclusions about pain burden or usefulness of care. A pain-specific measure is needed rather than assuming a tic severity score establishes pain benefit. Self-reported care usefulness in a cross-sectional survey cannot establish causal treatment efficacy.

Disconfirming findings: explicit identical time windows and populations, negligible numerical differences between current/worst summaries, and identical denominators would weaken the measurement/denominator prediction. A randomized or otherwise well-identified causal treatment comparison would defeat the assumption that this paper supplies usefulness only. Missing quantitative information produces an unresolved comparison, not confirmation.

## Planned checks and limits

Read and verify primary full text, identifiers, dates, sampling, diagnosis ascertainment, eligibility, instrument/version/timeframe, nonresponse and selection, outcome-specific counts and uncertainty. Preserve negative or inconsistent findings. No assumption of population prevalence or a severe-persistent-adult subgroup. No reconstruction of participant data, missing covariance, unreported confidence intervals or individualized outcomes. Suppress single-person cells and avoid identifiable quotations.

If defensible, execute one deterministic aggregate audit of reported counts and denominator dependence, plus a descriptive current-versus-worst comparison. Label arithmetic contrasts as descriptions of displayed aggregates, not paired individual change or causal effect. Record source locations, inputs, code, output and tests. No simulations planned. Do not choose a clinically meaningful difference threshold post hoc.

Stop after a measurement/estimand decision and a modest reproducible calculation, or document unavailable prerequisites. No new patient collection, access request, outreach or medical intervention.
