# Independent review: adult tic-related pain measurement and denominators

Review date: 17 October 2026 (UTC). Reviewer: independent audit worker. Status: independent extraction, arithmetic, main-code rerun, and final narrative review completed. No blocking correction found.

## Scope and disposition

This review follows the public-evidence-only boundaries in the repository research protocol. It independently read the primary publisher HTML and PDF text before inspecting the main worker's analysis. No participant-level records, individual scores, source-paper copies, personal data, clinical actions, or repository commits are included.

**Disposition:** retain a bounded descriptive signal of pain burden and unsatisfactory recalled care experiences in a selected adult support-group sample. Do not turn the reported pain percentage into current-pain prevalence, the worst/current contrast into improvement, the relief percentage into a verified treatment-response rate, or questionnaire descriptors into a diagnosis or mechanism. Several source inconsistencies remain unresolved.

Primary source: Agnieszka Małek (2025), *Coping with pain among adults with chronic tic disorders*, Frontiers in Psychology 16:1537088, DOI [10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088), PMID 40808742. [Publisher HTML](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full); [publisher PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/pdf). Source reports a CC BY license. This deliverable contains original review and arithmetic, not the article.

## Sampling and measurement audit

- Data collection took place in July 2023 among available, willing adult support-group attendees in multiple Polish locations. This is a convenience cross-sectional sample, without a sampling frame or stated participation denominator sufficient to estimate selection probabilities. No a priori power calculation was used.
- Inclusion required age at least 18 and a neurologist's GTS or chronic motor tic-disorder diagnosis under DSM-5. The final sample was 76 adults aged 18–51: 38 men and 38 women, 57 with GTS and 19 with chronic motor tic disorder. No chronic vocal-only tic-disorder participants enrolled. Do not extrapolate directly to children, all people with tics, or population prevalence.
- The 56-person pain subgroup (26 men, 30 women) explicitly covers **past or present** tic-related pain. It is not an established count of participants in pain at the questionnaire moment, a fixed-period prevalence, or people meeting a defined chronic-pain-duration threshold. Chronic tic disorder and chronic pain are different constructs.
- Paper questionnaires and an online YGTSS interview within a week constitute one assessment episode. There is no longitudinal before/after intervention measurement. The precise VAS worst-pain look-back window is not specified in the inspected main report; do not relabel it lifetime, recent-week, or most-frequent pain. Current and recalled worst pain have different temporal referents.
- YGTSS is reported on a 0–100 scale incorporating tic burden and impairment. Its total should not be renamed a pure motor-tic-severity score or a pain-specific functional outcome.
- VAS measures intensity. SF-MPQ-2 describes pain quality/intensity. CSQ records coping tendencies and subjective control/reduction; BPCQ records beliefs about control. The care survey asks about recalled consultation and perceived usefulness. These do not provide randomized efficacy comparisons, validated lesion-based neuropathic diagnoses, or a direct pain-interference/quality-of-life treatment endpoint.
- Comorbidities were self-reported. Raw-data availability is stated as an author availability commitment; no public participant dataset was obtained or needed here. Missingness and subgroup analysis denominators are not fully documented for every comparison.

## Independent numeric findings

1. **Pain-history fraction:** 56/76 = 73.6842%, supporting 73.7% for the stated past-or-present sample endpoint. Within-sex fractions would be 26/38 = 68.4211% and 30/38 = 78.9474%; the table's male 34.2% instead corresponds to 26/76. Do not mix whole-sample and within-sex denominators.
2. **Displayed sex-total VAS means:** weighting the printed means by 26 and 30 yields current 2.925 and worst 4.3. Their difference, 1.375, is only arithmetic on rounded summaries. It is neither a participant-level paired analysis nor longitudinal improvement, sustained benefit, a treatment effect, or a clinically important difference established by this study. No confidence interval or inferential paired result is supplied by this review.
3. **Female worst-pain inconsistency:** GTS 20×4.6 plus CMTD 10×4.2 gives 4.4667. With one-decimal rounding, the stratum-weighted interval is approximately 4.4167–4.5167, disjoint from the 4.25–4.35 interval for the printed female total of 4.3. Ordinary rounding cannot reconcile them. Do not choose a replacement value. Other Table 3 sex/outcome combinations have overlapping rounding intervals.
4. **Help-seeking inconsistency:** 19 men + 31 women = 50, or 89.2857% of 56, rather than the printed 86%. The stated female seeker count exceeds the 30 women in the pain subgroup. A hypothetical numerator 48 would round to 86% of 56, but is not a recovered observation. No corrected seeker numerator is selected.
5. **Relief denominator inconsistency:** 4 men + 6 women = 10. That is 17.8571% of all 56 pain reporters, 20% of the printed 50 seekers, or 20.8333% of hypothetical 48 seekers. Thus the source's 18%-of-seekers wording is not arithmetically supported by its seeker count. These are diagnostic denominator scenarios, not alternative efficacy estimates. The narrative describes temporary or insufficient relief, without a standardized threshold or follow-up.
6. **Provider percentages:** several are arithmetically compatible with a denominator of 56, but that alone does not establish their intended denominator. Psychiatry 30.3% has no integer numerator over 56 under conventional one-decimal rounding; the nearest 17/56 = 30.3571% would normally display as 30.4%. Treat 17 as a near-compatible reconstruction only, never an observed count. The blank supplementary questionnaire explicitly allows multiple provider and treatment selections; percentages must not be summed as mutually exclusive care pathways. Two acupuncture users are explicitly reported, unlike the hypothetical reconstructions. No modality-specific causal effect can be estimated.

## Supplementary questionnaire verification

After the independent main-paper extraction was complete, the main worker supplied the officially retrieved blank two-page care-seeking questionnaire. This reviewer independently inspected both rendered pages. The [Europe PMC supplementary-file endpoint](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12344498/supplementaryFiles) provides the source; no copy or screenshots are published with this review.

Both the care-seeking item and perceived-effectiveness item offer affirmative, negative, and uncertain/does-not-remember options. Provider and treatment questions allow multiple selections. The form does not supply outcome counts, VAS recall windows, scale-scoring definitions, or a treatment-specific efficacy endpoint. Therefore, subtracting the affirmative-relief count from a denominator does not establish a count of treatment failures: the remainder can include uncertain responses, and missing response counts are not reported. Multiple reasons for dissatisfaction are offered, but no reason-specific rates can be inferred from the blank options.

## Secondary source-quality flags, outside the main endpoint

These strengthen caution but do not expand the bounded analysis into an instrument-validation project:

- Table 1 ADHD 10/76 = 13.1579%, not printed 7.6%; sleep disorders 20/76 = 26.3158%, not printed 15.2%. Counts and percentages are retained as printed inputs rather than silently repaired.
- Combining the full-sample male/female YGTSS summaries yields mean 38.67 and sample SD 19.00475, inconsistent with the printed overall SD 16.00, assuming the same 76 people. The relevant formula accounts for both within-sex variance and between-sex mean differences.
- Table 5 female continuous-pain score components sum to 100 while the table prints 98. Domain means have unclear person-versus-endorsement denominators; this review does not reverse-engineer individual scores or substitute recalculated clinical scales.
- HTML and text extraction render some prose Spearman symbols as p, including a negative value, while the rendered PDF symbols resemble rho. This symbol/encoding ambiguity is not treated as a definitive negative-p-value error. Separately, the positive 0.441 entry accompanies inverse-direction prose, so the sign/interpretation is unresolved. No inferential result is reconstructed here.
- Table 7 BPCQ internal-control values raise a possible scale-range issue relative to commonly described Polish BPCQ scoring. However, the exact version/scoring used here has not been verified. This is an **unresolved instrument-version question**, not a finding that the paper's BPCQ results are definitively invalid. No treatment recommendation or population claim depends on it.

## Main implementation and interpretation review

After the independent input file and first arithmetic results were saved, the main extraction, inputs, code, tests, output, provenance, report and evidence record were reviewed. All 44 independent input/output comparisons passed. All 18 implementation tests passed on rerun, and regenerating the main JSON output was byte-identical. The main code uses standard-library Decimal arithmetic and explicit conservative rounding ranges. The independent code uses separately entered floats and checks the same substantive conclusions; minor numeric representation differences do not change a result.

The final report correctly labels all pooled current/worst quantities as conditional on inconsistent printed sex-total rows. It does not select a corrected help-seeking denominator, convert uncertain responses to failures, infer a pain-treatment effect, or claim representative/severe-adult estimates. The correlation-symbol concern was revised to an encoding/coefficient ambiguity rather than a definitive negative-p-value accusation. Exact Polish BPCQ scoring remains unverified and peripheral. The main evidence record carries these boundaries into reuse. No blocking methodological or implementation correction remains for this bounded audit.

Passing these checks establishes arithmetic agreement and source-faithful reporting, not reliability of the contradictory underlying source, validation of the instruments, formal clinical peer review, or a replicated clinical finding. Source corrections or new methodological documentation could require reevaluation.

## Decision boundary

The evidence supports keeping tic-related pain and perceived care adequacy as research outcomes worth measuring. A defensible next study would prespecify current/typical/worst time windows, pain duration and interference, nonoverlapping participant-flow denominators, treatment-specific exposure and follow-up, and confounding controls. This paper cannot determine what treatment works, how many adults currently have chronic pain, whether a therapy failed, or whether tic suppression changes underlying disease biology.
