# A trigger is not necessarily the maintaining cause

28 October 2026 | Independent, targeted mechanistic critique | Research hypotheses, not treatment advice

## Useful conclusion

The evidence is more compatible with **multiple vulnerabilities converging on an action-selection system** than with a proven universal chemical leak. Finding an early signal, or stopping a downstream movement, would not by itself identify the initiating disease mechanism. This does not imply irreversibility: a distributed system can have an important bottleneck. It means the bottleneck must be demonstrated rather than inferred from anatomical location, temporal precedence, or response to a drug.

Three separable questions sharpen the search:

1. Why is the system susceptible over months or years?
2. Why is a particular action selected now?
3. What allows that action to propagate into an observable tic?

The following are candidate syntheses of existing work. None is a discovery, validated endotype, or claim that researchers overlooked its components. Their proposed cross-level tests remain unperformed here.

## 1. A vulnerable action-selection circuit plus a permissive input state

**Proposed synthesis.** A persistent circuit vulnerability may specify which motor ensembles are easily recruited, while momentary incoming activity and recent circuit history determine whether an event occurs. Consequently, the location of susceptibility, the trigger of a particular event, and the node where the event can be interrupted need not be identical.

**Primary anchors.** In a rat striatal-disinhibition model, cortical activation affected event timing while striatal state constrained event form and refractory timing. This is strong within-model causal evidence, not a demonstration that human developmental TS universally begins in the striatum. [Israelashvili and Bar-Gad 2015; PMID 26674861; DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2770-15.2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26674861/). In a separate mouse model, blocking striatal glutamatergic input eliminated tic-like movements despite the original disinhibitory manipulation. This shows that experimentally imposed vulnerability need not be sufficient without afferent input. [Pogorelov et al. 2015; PMID 25597650; DOI 10.1016/j.expneurol.2015.01.001](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4361636/).

**Already studied:** cortical–striatal interaction, spatial-versus-temporal encoding, refractory dynamics, distributed circuit models. The repository already addresses network targeting and the insufficiency of phase-leading human signals for causal localization.

**Potentially under-tested refinement:** whether a prospectively specified input-by-susceptibility interaction predicts *future tic probability* across states in persistent-severe adults better than either a tonic biomarker or a single-region signal. This is a candidate gap, not an exhaustive novelty finding.

**Discriminating predictions:**

- Input history should add predictive information beyond recent tic history, ordinary motor preparation, arousal, and subject identity; its effect should depend on an independently estimated susceptibility state.
- Altering a candidate input in a suitable governed model should change timing more than repertoire; altering ensemble selection should change repertoire or spatial recruitment, not necessarily input timing.
- A genuine downstream interruption may abolish movement while leaving upstream event-like activity. That would identify an output bottleneck, not resolution of vulnerability.

**Strongest falsifiers:** with adequate temporal resolution and sensitivity, the proposed interaction adds no held-out predictive value; relevant input changes occur only after movements; or selective input perturbation leaves the putative event process unchanged despite verified engagement. General movement suppression is not a selective rescue. A single generator that predicts events across states and remains necessary under carefully controlled perturbation would favor the simpler account.

## 2. Timing-specific filtering or retuning, rather than uniformly weak brakes

**Proposed synthesis.** A person may possess adequate stopping capacity after a control state is established but be vulnerable while the mapping between input and action is being reconfigured. A useful biological target could therefore be the timing and selectivity of gating, not a global amount of inhibition.

**Primary anchors and counterevidence.** Adult masked-priming results conflict: Stenner et al. found intact automatic inhibition, supported by Bayesian analysis; Rawji et al. found abnormal automatic but intact voluntary inhibition. Their task timings differed, so neither establishes a universal deficit. Rawji's full methods also used different comparison groups for its voluntary and automatic experiments. [Stenner 2018; PMID 30485912; DOI 10.1002/mds.27493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30485912/); [Rawji 2020; PMID 32125364; DOI 10.1093/brain/awaa024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10093221/5/Rothwell_awaa024.pdf).

Reaching-after-switch and sensory-EEG studies motivate state-dependent questions, but do not establish a tic cause. The reaching paper itself proposes state transitions and reports null symptom associations. [Kurvits 2024; PMID 38661451; DOI 10.1002/mds.29775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38661451/). Sensory stimulation can normalize a variability measure without demonstrating clinical improvement, and several sensory timing measures were unchanged. [Gialopsou 2025; PMID 40641597; DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcaf247](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40641597/). A larger mixed-age EEG study found altered aperiodic activity but Bayesian evidence against its association with clinical tic measures. [Adelhofer 2021; PMID 34805995; DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcab250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34805995/).

**Already studied:** neural-noise theory, compensation, state transitions, automatic versus voluntary inhibition. Calling these new would be incorrect. A 2025 adult motor-cueing study directly investigated adaptation to changing cue predictability, including a Bayesian observer model and EEG. It found altered adaptation, not a causal tic mechanism. Its P3b symptom associations were nonsignificant, whereas one P3a association with tic severity was reported; medication, comorbidity and overlapping motor-response activity limit interpretation. Fitted learning/response parameters did not differ between groups; the study does not distinguish predictive-coding from binding explanations. This is additional direct prior art against a novelty claim for predictive retuning. [Schuller et al. 2025; PMID 41394342; DOI 10.1093/braincomms/fcaf458](https://academic.oup.com/braincomms/article/7/6/fcaf458/8339723).

**Potentially under-tested refinement:** a predeclared within-person *input variability × retuning lag* interaction predicting natural tic events, rather than a diagnosis-level difference in average noise. Different assays of variability, aperiodic spectral shape, and inhibition must not be treated as interchangeable measures of one latent chemical quantity.

**Discriminating predictions:**

- Vulnerability should have a defined time course after a change in task/context, with relatively preserved later performance; a generalized capacity deficit predicts more sustained impairment.
- The timing of maximal abnormality should shift consistently with a person's independently measured filtering latency, not merely with fatigue, task difficulty, or cue salience.
- A prespecified lag parameter should predict subsequent tic hazard beyond average signal variability and ordinary movement. A cross-sectional diagnostic classifier is insufficient.

**Strongest falsifiers:** precise replicated null interaction with tic hazard despite robust assay engagement; the candidate predicts only ordinary movement errors; or the effect disappears under matched attention and artifact controls. A normal physiological measure after stimulation without any independent tic change would sever the proposed mediator-to-symptom bridge. Do not rescue the hypothesis by repeatedly redefining the relevant state after seeing outcomes.

## 3. Susceptibility determines event probability; binding determines event content

**Proposed synthesis.** Background excitability alone has difficulty specifying why repeated unwanted actions take organized, recurrent forms. A separate, overly persistent sensory–action association could influence which action a permissive state releases. Developmental susceptibility and learned action organization could then interact without making tics voluntary or reducing TS to an acquired habit.

**Primary anchors.** A 24-versus-24 adult event-file experiment found stronger perception–action binding and an association between tic frequency and conditions requiring unbinding. This is an association in a laboratory task, not evidence that undoing the binding removes human tics. [Kleimaker et al. 2020; PMID 32464659; DOI 10.1093/brain/awaa111](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32464659/). Another adult task-switching study found altered binding-related EEG with unchanged behavioral switching and proactive control. Thus altered physiology need not imply broad behavioral impairment. [Wehmeyer et al. 2023; PMID 37019631; DOI 10.1523/ENEURO.0279-22.2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37019631/).

**Already studied:** event coding, enhanced binding, habit learning, corticostriatal plasticity, computational habit models. These are not newly discovered mechanisms.

**Potentially under-tested refinement:** separate predictions about *how often* an event occurs from predictions about *which tic pattern* recurs, rather than using one total severity score to adjudicate both processes. This requires independent validation, not fitting flexible latent variables to the same tic series.

**Discriminating predictions:**

- An independently estimated binding/unbinding parameter should predict recurrence of particular action patterns or cue-specific transitions after controlling for overall tic rate; a general excitability parameter should explain event probability more strongly than pattern identity.
- A model containing both parameters should outperform single-factor accounts on held-out people and days, including a prespecified persistent-severe-adult stratum.
- In a valid model system, altering association persistence should change action specificity while sparing basic movement, whereas simply lowering excitability should mainly alter event rate. Clinical benefit cannot be inferred from the computational dissociation.

**Strongest falsifiers:** no reproducible separation between rate and content predictions; binding differences explained by generic task learning or comorbid symptoms; or equally strong signatures in neurological comparison groups without corresponding tic-pattern information. A theory requiring conscious urges before every tic also fails as a universal account: tics have been documented in sleep. Lack of reported urges is not by itself proof of absent sensory processing.

## Constraints on all three accounts

### Dopamine: an important modulator is not automatically the original fault

Small human PET studies did not converge on one excess measure: one found similar striatal tracer uptake and D2 binding, while another found enhanced challenge-evoked dopamine release in adults. Different tracers, regions, treatment histories, and tonic versus challenge-evoked processes matter. Neither a small null study nor drug responsiveness settles the primary cause. [Turjanski 1994; PMID 7911827; DOI 10.1136/jnnp.57.6.688](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7911827/); [Wong 2008; PMID 17987065; DOI 10.1038/sj.npp.1301528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17987065/). The present synthesis does not discard dopamine involvement; it rejects inference from receptor-sensitive output to a universal dopamine-leak etiology.

### Sleep changes state, not necessarily the underlying susceptibility

Adult video-polysomnography documented tics across sleep stages, including REM; non-tic movements also varied with sleep state. A decrease in visible tics therefore cannot alone distinguish selective tic control from generic motor-output changes. [Cohrs 2001; PMID 11160467; DOI 10.1136/jnnp.70.2.192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11160467/). Any sleep-based candidate must survive ordinary-movement, microarousal, and detection-sensitivity controls.

### Developmental vulnerability and persistent-adult biology must remain separate questions

Organoid work from five adult TS donors demonstrated altered developmental patterning; postmortem multiomics examined six adults with refractory symptoms. These are important convergent cellular leads, but neither design observes childhood-to-adult change or compares persistent illness with independently confirmed remission. [Brady 2022; PMID 36447010; DOI 10.1038/s41380-022-01880-5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949887/); [Postmortem multiomics study 2025; PMID 39892689; DOI 10.1016/j.biopsych.2024.12.022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006322325000642).

For future discrimination, compare persistent-severe, persistent-lower-burden, and objectively remitted adult trajectories, with treatment and comorbidities characterized. An adult label alone does not establish severe-adult applicability. Do not silently require the ability to suppress tics for long scans. Do not interpret persistence as deficient effort or assume the same process explains every trajectory. A compensatory mechanism may work in one task yet fail to protect everyday functioning.

## Decision

The best next research move is not to name another molecule. It is to force competing mechanisms to make **different predictions about event timing, action identity, and persistence**, while distinguishing liability markers from symptom-generating variables. The strongest under-tested bridge identified here is between dynamic laboratory phenotypes and prospectively measured tic events in the same people. The existing null symptom associations make that bridge a high-risk hypothesis, not an already-supported explanation.
