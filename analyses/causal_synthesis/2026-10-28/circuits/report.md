# Human circuit evidence: where an action is released is not necessarily where susceptibility begins

28 October 2026 | Targeted mechanistic synthesis, not a new daily scan or clinical protocol

## Main inference

The strongest defensible human architecture is **distributed coupling among sensory/salience and action-selection systems, basal-ganglia/thalamic loops and motor implementation**, rather than one demonstrably defective brain region. There is affirmative evidence that these systems participate in tic-related states and that perturbing connected circuits can alter symptoms. There is not yet a demonstrated universal sequence in which an insular urge drives SMA, then striatum, then thalamus to produce every tic.

This distinction matters: a node can causally modulate expression without being the initiating disease lesion; activity preceding a movement can reflect preparation or attempted control; and a signal accompanying a tic can be a useful detector without causing or forecasting it. These are separate claims, not different names for the same mechanism.

## Primary evidence and causal role

### 1. Timing places candidate processes before visible expression, but does not order neuronal causes

[Bohlhalter et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16520330/) (DOI 10.1093/brain/awl050; ten participants) associated the two-second pre-tic modeled period with insular/cingulate, supplementary motor and sensory-association activity; tic expression recruited sensorimotor/cerebellar regions. [Neuner et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00362) (PMID 24904391; ten adults in event-related analysis) similarly found cortical activity in earlier modeled bins, with putamen/insula and other regions in the next bin and thalamic/sensorimotor activity at onset.

**Interpretation:** these observations locate a distributed pre-expression context, consistent with an internally generated action rather than a purely peripheral motor disturbance. They do not establish an axonal order. BOLD is a delayed, regionally variable vascular signal; regressors tied to one- or two-second offsets are not direct millisecond recordings of neuronal initiation. Urge was not experimentally isolated from motor preparation, restraint, attention or incipient movement in these contrasts. Do not turn a schematic temporal sequence into a demonstrated insula-to-SMA causal chain.

The positive result remains useful: candidate processes extend beyond the body-part-specific motor representation. The missing discriminant is whether the same pre-tic signal occurs during an urge without action, during matched ordinary actions, or during an effort to resist an action.

### 2. Severe-adult anatomy triangulates an action network, not simply the motor homunculus

[Baldermann et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55242-6) mapped thalamic stimulation locations in 37 severe adults aged 18–65 to a normative connectome of 1,000 healthy people. Tic improvement correlated with connectivity to the cingulo-opercular network (CON; r=.62) and somato-cognitive action network (SCAN; r=.47). A separate ten-person DBS cohort and a reused map of 22 tic-associated lesions provided convergent evidence. Main-cohort average improvement was 32.5% with SD 21.3 at 6–12 months, retaining marked heterogeneity rather than implying universal response.

The important anatomical distinction is **action integration versus muscle execution**. CON includes insular/opercular and medial frontal/cingulate areas; SCAN includes inter-effector portions of motor cortex, not merely the usual body-part maps. Sensorimotor-network association did not survive the reported FDR threshold (p=.063), and the superior SCAN subdivision was nonsignificant (p=.052), unlike other subdivisions. This is not a uniformly positive cortex-wide association.

**Causal classification:** DBS is an intervention, but this particular connectivity-outcome analysis is observational. It is evidence for a modifiable connected system and a candidate response-modulating anatomy, not a randomized test of network targeting or proof of necessary/sufficient CON activity. Lesions offer a different perturbational line, but lesion-associated acquired tics are not automatically developmental Tourette. Both analyses use an average healthy connectome; they do not measure each patient's effective connectivity. The reused lesion map is not a new independent lesion cohort. No lesion narratives or individual scans were collected here.

**Source discrepancies:** the main replication text gives CON r=.54, while Figure 5 caption prints .054. The age-outcome correlation is printed as −2.84, outside a correlation's possible range. No values are repaired or used in a new computation. The broad pattern is retained with these numerical limitations explicit.

### 3. Deep recordings separate network coordination from local signal amplitude

[Thalamo-frontal recordings, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03220-9) (PMID 40946095) enrolled eight men with implanted thalamic DBS; two were excluded, with smaller analysis-specific samples (five for tic-related analyses and four for suppression). Reduced pre-tic alpha connectivity, not the corresponding local-power pattern, linked thalamus to frontal/sensorimotor/parietal regions. This makes a relational feature a plausible candidate rather than treating regional power as the complete neural state.

Crucially, the authors did not analyze the collected continuous urge ratings in this report. A purported urge mechanism is therefore a hypothesis, not a measured urge-to-network mediation. Voluntary-movement controls were missing; mouse movements could confound task periods. Two minutes off stimulation after months or years of DBS does not recover untreated natural history. Weekly clinical severity reflected the treated period. Selected tics also required a preceding tic-free interval, limiting coverage of closely clustered or persistently dense tics.

A competing interpretation is loss of a compensatory coordination state; another is evolving urge/action preparation. Prior recordings sometimes found increased coherence or no thalamo-motor coherence, so a universal “less connectivity causes tics” rule is unsupported. Frequency, state, coverage, recording history and metric differ. None of these reports licenses a recommendation to increase or decrease a patient's alpha activity.

[Lowor et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40977255/) (DOI 10.1002/mds.70043) adds simultaneous CM–anterior GPi observations in five analyzed severe adults from six implanted. Tic-related low-frequency power/coherence and a reported CM phase lead are an important state contrast. However, onset-centered windows include post-onset information, and cued hand actions are not fully matched to spontaneous motor/vocal tics. Phase lead is not causal initiation, a direct anatomical connection or a prospectively validated predictor. The repository's [full audit](../../../cm_agpi/2026-10-20/report.md) preserves high-frequency nulls, phase-wording conflicts, treatment history and trial-family overlap. The source and linked clinical results must not become separate independent replications.

### 4. Perturbation establishes a promising lever, not a single SMA disease cause

[Conelea et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1720968) randomized 14 youths aged 12–17 to one active or sham SMA-targeted session. Reported effect sizes favored active treatment for free-tic frequency (d=.34) and rewarded suppression (d=.24), but not ordinary suppression (d=0). Urge changes differed across task states. These small exploratory results are a reason to test state dependence, not an adult efficacy estimate. Excluding baseline strong suppressors changes the estimand and should not be promoted to a prospectively validated responder subgroup.

The study measured no concurrent neural function, so behavioral effects cannot confirm that SMA inhibition occurred or that the effect traveled through the proposed pathway. Formal masking assessment was absent; off-target and connected-network effects remain possible. This is controlled perturbation evidence at the intervention level, not local-mechanism identification.

Counterevidence is substantive: [Landeros-Weisenberger et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2014.11.015), a sham-controlled study of 20 severely affected adults, did not establish superior short-course tic reduction. Small null trials do not prove the SMA irrelevant, but prevent the inference that an imaging hotspot is automatically a useful treatment target. Dose, spatial targeting, physiological engagement and endpoint sensitivity remain alternative explanations requiring tests, not post hoc rescue stories.

## A causal-role map, explicitly an inference

1. **Predisposition:** developmental/genetic/cellular features may alter the probability of entering tic-prone states. Acute imaging and DBS cannot by themselves identify their origin.
2. **Context and sensory drive:** interoceptive/salience signals may change urgency or action priority. An urge can contribute without being necessary for every tic; absent reported urge is not proof of absent neural precursor.
3. **Selection/control state:** striatal–thalamic–cortical interactions may alter which action programs are released or held back. Increased activity can be pathological drive, successful compensation, or both in different regions/times.
4. **Expression and feedback:** motor and sensory systems implement the movement/sound and register consequences. A strong signal here can be an excellent detector and a poor preventive target.

There may be several routes into the same final movement. This is a useful model class, not a newly discovered mechanism or proof that tics are deliberate choices. It accommodates severe persistent adult burden without assuming remission, quiet intervals or successful suppression.

## Distinguishing predictions that would change the decision

These are **conceptual tests for authorized institutional research or permitted existing data**, not instructions for home stimulation, suppression exercises, medication withdrawal or recruitment.

| Rival interpretation | Discriminating prediction | Counterevidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| Urge-linked drive | A candidate network feature predicts independently measured urge onset and later tic hazard beyond kinematics, attention and prior tic history; urge-without-tic periods separate drive from output | Feature appears only after movement, or predicts ordinary action equally after matching |
| Compensatory coordination failure | Loss of coordination predicts tic release conditional on measured urge; preserved coordination accompanies successful nonexpression without generalized motor slowing | Feature merely tracks stronger urges, fatigue or movement artifacts; no incremental out-of-sample prediction |
| Execution/reafference marker | Signal aligns better with EMG/kinematic onset or sensory consequence than with earlier urge; matched voluntary actions reproduce it | Reliable pre-movement specificity survives appropriate controls and temporal leakage checks |
| Network engagement, not coordinate alone | Independently measured engagement of a prespecified action-network feature explains an intervention's selective effect while ordinary movement/function remain intact | Clinical change occurs without engagement, engagement fails to predict response, or equally connected control networks perform as well |

An intervention effect need not prove mediation: randomizing stimulation does not randomize the measured mediator. Robust temporal prediction is also not causality. A decisive next computation should use preregistered leakage-free held-out comparisons and competing models, but the required aggregate information here cannot supply a participant-level classifier or causal effect. No such model was fitted in this synthesis.

## Novelty and stopping boundary

The distributed network idea, CON/SCAN proposal, network-guided stimulation and responsive DBS are **not novel project discoveries**. They are present in primary literature and already in repository hypotheses H15 and H16; H12 covers compensatory inhibition and H17 sensory modulation. Our original contribution is a source-linked separation of predisposition, pre-expression modulation, compensation and output, with explicit rival predictions and failures of inference. It is not a claim of prior-art novelty for any biological hypothesis.

The bounded synthesis supports retaining a distributed, state-dependent action-release model while rejecting a universal serial pathway or single-site explanation as established fact. Next work should choose one contrast with actual public data access or published aggregate estimands, rather than add another untestable network story. Current inspected aggregate papers do not provide the matched urge/action/control data needed to choose between drive and compensation. Individual data requests and clinical experimentation remain outside this task.
