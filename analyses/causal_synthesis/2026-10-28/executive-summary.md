# The most useful pattern so far

28 October 2026

**The trigger of one tic, the reason someone remains susceptible, and the place an intervention can interrupt a tic need not be the same thing.** A common control point may still exist even if people reach it through different biological routes.

The highest-priority question in this synthesis concerns timing: incoming activity may meet a vulnerable action-control state before compensation catches up. But researchers already study related state-transition and predictive-control ideas. Adult studies also contain important null results, so this is not a discovery or a proven explanation.

Three priorities emerge:

1. Test whether input variability and control-retuning time jointly predict subsequent tics better than either alone.
2. Check whether different genetic/developmental routes converge on a modifiable functional state, without assuming everyone has the same structural defect.
3. Separate what determines how often a tic occurs from what determines its recurring form.

We completed public-aggregate genetic and negative-trial audits plus an explicitly synthetic causal-inference calculation. The calculation shows why identical symptom improvement can arise from different underlying causes. Its code passed 10 implementation tests and 113 independent checks; it says nothing about any individual's biology.

**What is still missing:** appropriate human evidence connecting these proposed processes prospectively to natural tics, independent replication and meaningful clinical benefit. Persistent severe adults and daily burden must remain central, not treated as exceptions to childhood remission.
