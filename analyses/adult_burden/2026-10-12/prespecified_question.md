# Adult severity, burden and persistence: bounded endpoint check

Recorded 12 October 2026, 02:47 UTC, after reading the four required current repository files and before opening the two primary leads in this run. This is an operational prospective plan for this extraction, not a registered clinical protocol.

## Question and prediction

For adults with childhood-onset primary tic disorders in the Calgary/Paris registry family, can reported tic severity substitute for independently measured functional/quality-of-life burden? Do the published native baseline/six/twelve-month endpoints support specifying and estimating a persistent-severe-adult trajectory?

Working prediction: tic severity will explain some burden, but will not be a sufficient substitute for impairment or quality of life. Evidence against a severity-only proxy would include material independent relationships of psychiatric symptoms with burden, weak/absent or domain-specific severity relationships, or longitudinal discordance. Evidence against this working prediction would require convincing individual-level concordance across severity, independent burden domains and change, including adequately adjusted and repeated analyses. A simple positive cross-sectional correlation alone will not validate substitution. If the papers do not test interchangeability, record that specific uncertainty rather than claiming formal surrogate invalidation.

Persistence prediction: published methods will identify useful repeated adult outcomes at six/twelve months, but aggregate summaries may not identify a severe-at-every-visit adult stratum. Disconfirm this limitation if reports supply an explicit applicable severe threshold plus linked adult counts across visits, burden trajectories, treatment status and denominators sufficient for the specified estimand. Do not infer persistent severity from a group mean, childhood onset, referral status or a single high baseline score.
