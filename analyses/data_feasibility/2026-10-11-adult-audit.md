# Adult longitudinal tic resources and the antecedent analysis decision

Project NeuroFreedom | Bounded public evidence audit | 11 October 2026

## Decision

**Do not start the proposed day or week scale antecedent analysis. None of six additional adult-relevant resource families documents the complete design and access prerequisites.** This is a bounded compatibility result, not evidence that antecedents are absent or that suitable unpublished data cannot exist.

The main advance is identifying where the design actually comes closest. The Nottingham home-stimulation trial provides repeated independently coded observations, while the University of Florida responsive-DBS program directly represents severely affected adults. Neither closes the original question. Adult observational registries offer a separate route to studying persistent burden at longer intervals. The source-level evidence and precise gaps are below.

This audit extends the 10 October five-cohort audit rather than repeating it. The Maiquez treatment trial was already in the project's evidence portfolio; its detailed ascertainment/access assessment is new work. A linked September 2026 follow-up is an older publication found during this audit, not a newly published October result.

Only public study metadata, methods and published aggregate findings were examined. No participant files or recordings were downloaded, no individual-level analysis was performed, and no access application, outreach, recruitment or clinical experiment occurred.

## Hypothesis and falsifiable prediction

**Feasibility hypothesis:** an adult-relevant resource documents all prerequisites for testing whether an earlier independently ascertained exposure predicts later independently observed tic burden at day or week scale, including adults with severe persistent symptoms.

**Prediction:** at least one resource will pass every gate below. A definite mismatch at any gate rules out the full question on the documented design; an undocumented field remains unknown rather than being assumed absent.

- G1: Repeated, timestamped exposure and outcome windows at the target cadence, with recall/integration periods and completion delays recoverable
- G2: Tic observations independent of exposure reporting, with rater masking specified where applicable; an interview is distinguished from direct observation
- G3: Sufficient nonoverlapping lag pairs, treatment/adherence histories, linkage and missingness information for a within-person analysis
- G4: Adult persistence and severity/impairment ascertainment that permits an adult severe-persistent stratum, without requiring quiet phases
- G5: A documented route covering the relevant modalities and lawful secondary use, followed by the separate permissions needed for this project

The biological prediction remains untested: a prespecified earlier exposure should improve forward prediction of independently observed burden beyond recent burden, individual differences and appropriately timed treatment/context. Reversed-time prediction, only concurrent/shared-report associations, or no held-out improvement would weaken it. Prediction alone would not establish causation.

## Search method and stopping rule

The targeted search began at approximately 03:05 UTC on 11 October 2026, after reading the current repository. Searches had no publication-date limit. Discovery covered adult home observation/intensive sampling, adult stress and sleep cohorts, severe-adult neuromodulation, and adult registries. Primary methods, official registry records and access statements supplied the compatibility evidence; reviews and third-party registry mirrors were discovery aids only.

**Operational stopping rule, not a preregistered search:** resolve the adult intensive, adult observational and severe-refractory branches into at most six core resource families; follow linked protocols and reports sufficiently to establish a full match or an explicit decisive gap. Stop when all six have a documented incompatibility, rather than adding progressively less relevant cohorts. One older responsive case and one observation-method study were retained only as scope checks. They are not extra independent cohorts or participants in the tally.

The stopping rule was met. Six core families received structured extraction and zero complete matches were demonstrated. Linked reports were grouped, not counted as replication. Search-result totals were not exhaustively recorded, so no PRISMA-style denominator or sensitivity estimate is claimed. Exact query strings are retained in the companion search log.

### Nottingham home MNS trial

Maiquez et al., first published 3 May 2023, allocated 135 participants aged at least 12. Direct videos covered five baseline weekdays and the first two treatment weeks, not all four treatment weeks. Three of five daily videos per week were randomly selected; 110-second segments were coded masked to allocation, time and stimulation state. Reported active/sham analyses comprised 82/91 sessions from 16/17 participants. Adult-only counts and sustained-severe adult strata were not established. Weekly interviews, anxiety/urge scales, impairment and quality-of-life instruments do not themselves establish nonoverlapping antecedent windows. The paper offers corresponding-author requests with privacy restrictions. [Primary methods and access statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10947020/).

**Judgment:** closest repeated independent-observation design in this pass. Repeated stress/sleep exposures linked to those observations are undocumented; anxiety symptoms are not interchangeable with independently timed stressful events. An assigned stimulation exposure supports a different estimand. Pre-session recordings during repeated treatment are not automatically untreated natural history.

### Linked MNS follow-up and its durability limitation

Jackson et al., 7 September 2026, analyzes the same trial, not replication. It describes device-use telemetry, fixed settings and weekly interviews, followed by a three-month assessment. Its active-arm motor-tic analysis reports 41 participants at baseline, 36 after four weeks and 15 at three months. Motor-tic scores remained below baseline among returnees, but follow-up lacks a contemporaneous sham comparison. No dense independent-video follow-up or repeated stress/sleep exposure series is established. [Primary report](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jnp.70080), [publication date and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42704310/).

**Interpretation:** retention for that analysis is 15/41, approximately 36.6% (arithmetic from published counts). This does not erase the observed within-returnee change, but makes selection and natural fluctuation serious alternatives to a durable treatment effect. The analysis cannot establish disease modification or cure. The follow-up's analytic denominators should not silently replace the earlier paper's denominators.

### Calgary and Paris adult registry

Pringsheim and Martino, online 7 December 2024, describe Calgary assessments at registration, six and twelve months, including YGTSS severity/impairment, GTS quality of life, psychiatric conditions and treatments. The report covers 95 adults aged 18–73; 63 and 55 had completed the respective follow-ups at analysis. It offers author-request access under privacy/ethical restrictions. Dense independent video or antecedent measurements are not documented. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11952939/).

The expanded Calgary/Paris report, 23 July 2025, includes 227 adults with childhood-onset primary tic disorders, but its published analysis is cross-sectional. It also offers restricted author-request access. Do not infer that all sites share Calgary's exact follow-up availability, or equate clinical referral with verified sustained severity. [Primary methods and access statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12284832/).

**Judgment:** a substantive adult persistence/function lead at its native timescale. It cannot resolve unobserved day/week changes. Completion counts at an interim analysis are not automatically all permanent losses to follow-up, and treatment exposure requires explicit handling in any eventual longitudinal use.

### Vanderbilt LISA-TS adult stress cohort

The official NCT04851678 record, updated 5 May 2026, specifies baseline and two-year assessments of lifetime stress with STRAIN, clinician-interview YGTSS, depression and GTS-QOL. It lists recruiting, estimated enrollment 140 and completion June 2027; the older narrative still says 70. No results are posted and IPD sharing is undecided. Adults are eligible, but severe persistence is not an explicit entry threshold. [Official study record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04851678), [official API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04851678).

**Judgment:** directly relevant to adult stress and broad health burden, but neither dense sampling nor independent observed-tic outcome is documented. It tests longer-term prognostic associations, not within-person day/week antecedents. Planned dates do not establish data readiness. A separate 2024 Vanderbilt focus-group paper informs adult priorities but is not proof of an operational intensive registry or an IPD route. [Focus-group study, 9 February 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10863457/).

### UCLA adult insomnia pilot

NCT05499741 plans 25 adults aged 18–50 with tic disorder and insomnia. The institutional protocol describes five weeks of actigraphy/sleep diaries, comprising one baseline week and four treatment weeks, with pre/post clinician tic assessments. The official API still lists recruiting, last updated 17 March 2025, an estimated January 2026 completion, no posted results and no IPD-sharing module. These stale estimates do not establish completion. [Institutional study description](https://ucla.clinicaltrials.researcherprofiles.org/trial/NCT05499741), [official record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05499741), [official API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05499741).

**Judgment:** promising independently instrumented sleep exposure, but it lacks a documented equally dense independent tic outcome. An open, single-group sleep intervention also changes the interpretation of sleep–tic associations. Severe persistent adult coverage and data-use terms remain unestablished.

### International Tourette DBS registry

Deeb et al., 25 April 2016, describe a multisite clinical/DBS registry with variable assessments and limited programming information; physiology was not then collected. Management by UF and a contributor framework do not amount to open participant-data licensing. [Primary registry design](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2016.00170/full).

Martinez-Ramirez et al., online 16 January 2018, report 185 implanted participants, surgery ages 13–58, with baseline, six- and twelve-month outcomes. Complete total-YGTSS data across these visits were available for 89; medication effects could not be assessed because those data were not collected. This is an observational treatment registry, without a documented registry-wide dense blinded video design. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5885852/), [author manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10044830/1/Foltynie%2026994_2_art_0_8w023w.pdf).

**Judgment:** relevant severe-treatment outcomes, not adequate day/week natural-history data. The word “public” in a registry title or outcomes website must not be translated into public access to raw longitudinal records. The registry website could not be retrieved during this pass.

### UF responsive and closed-loop DBS program

Cagle et al., 12 September 2022, report four analyzed embedded closed-loop participants from six recruited. Eligibility required age at least 21, disabling refractory tics and YGTSS tic score above 35/50. Monthly programming visits over six months included YGTSS and videotaped MRVRST. The primary visit randomized stimulation-state order with masking; YGTSS referred to the preceding 14–15 days, while videos assessed current expression. Settings were symptom-guided. Listed protocol/data-sharing supplements were not successfully retrieved. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9468946/).

Okun et al., 18 March 2024, expand the same program to ten adults aged 21–45, mean disease duration 22.6 years. It includes sensed physiology, programming decisions and MOS-36 quality of life, but no documented dense functional follow-up. Its access statement points to ClinicalTrials.gov and otherwise reasonable corresponding-author requests. [Primary report and access statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10948908/).

**Judgment:** closest severe-adult multi-person design. Immediate physiological tic markers cannot be reclassified as day/week antecedents. Symptom-responsive changes to therapy, stimulation artifacts and different recall windows prevent a clean natural-history interpretation. Requested raw modalities and secondary-use terms remain unverified.

The official NCT02056873 API confirms completed status, ten enrolled, posted aggregate results and last update 5 September 2024; it does not expose an IPD plan. The registry's actual completion is 24 August 2023, whereas the 2024 article says 30 June. Its listed endpoint is a 40% total-tic reduction; the paper mixes percentage and point-change descriptions. Those discrepancies are preserved, not resolved by assumption, and no efficacy estimate is used in this audit. [Official record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02056873), [official API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT02056873).

## Scope checks that do not repair the gaps

- Molina's earlier responsive-DBS case, online 29 September 2017, describes twelve months of device detections and work/rest context in one severely affected adult. Stress attribution depended on patient report; detection depended on treatment state. Dense independently validated outcomes and a reuse route were not established. It appears to precede the later UF trial, but overlap is unverified, so it is not added as an independent cohort. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7007215/).
- Goetz et al., July 2001, compared office/home video conditions in ten participants within 48 hours. It demonstrates context-sensitive observation rather than a sustained longitudinal exposure design. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11481693/).
- Pediatric follow-ups, yesterday's five families, single-session experiments, unrelated EMA methods papers and reviews were not substituted for adult eligibility.

## Exact prerequisites and next decision

The search task is closed with a negative full-design result. **Do not repeat a generic adult cohort search or begin a participant-data model from these findings alone.** Reopen feasibility only if a new release/protocol establishes the missing fields, or an explicitly authorized custodian clarification supplies them.

For any candidate, all of the following would be needed before a go decision:

1. A modality-level inventory showing which dated exposure measurements can be linked to independent tic observations within the same adults
2. Completion/recording times and recall windows sufficient to place the whole exposure window before the outcome window; enough actual lag pairs after exclusions
3. Treatment start/stop/dose or device-state history, adherence, observation context, rater masking and missingness reasons
4. An identifiable severe-persistent adult stratum and repeated burden/function outcomes, not only a high group mean or initial symptom threshold
5. Custodian confirmation of retained modalities, consent-compatible reuse, data-use restrictions, appropriate institutional/ethics review and a secure analysis setting; videos need special privacy safeguards

These prerequisites are not a request to collect new patient information. No contact or application is authorized by this audit.

**Preserve the sustained-burden estimand.** Do not require a quiet-to-peak transition for inclusion. Analyze observed burden levels, pain, interference, participation and quality of life across prespecified intervals, separating tic observation from patient-reported experience. If little within-person variation makes a lag coefficient unidentifiable, retain those adults for persistence/function analyses and report the identification limit. A low visible tic count is not automatically comfort or functional recovery.

For an immediately feasible public-data next step, the September MNS follow-up supports a bounded aggregate attrition/tipping-point analysis, explicitly limited to the published returnee outcome and its missing-data uncertainty. It must not be presented as answering the severe-adult antecedent question. Any longer-term adult registry or DBS reanalysis remains contingent on a separately approved scope and demonstrated data access.

## Limitations and provenance

This was a targeted search, not an exhaustive systematic review or clinical peer review. English-language discovery, indexing gaps, inaccessible supplements and sparse registry rendering limit negative findings. Full methods were available directly or through substantive primary-source indexed text; some direct PMC and publisher opens failed. Official ClinicalTrials.gov API metadata were retrieved successfully and a small public metadata snapshot was saved. No inaccessible field was treated as definitely uncollected.

No correction/retraction notice was identified in the inspected source views and limited targeted checks, but a comprehensive correction/retraction clearance was not performed. Reporting inconsistencies were retained rather than harmonized into invented precision. Registry plans do not establish completed data, and a data-availability statement does not establish this project's eligibility. Published article licenses do not automatically license underlying health records.
