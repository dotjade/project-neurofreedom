# Longitudinal Tourette dataset feasibility

Project NeuroFreedom | Bounded public-science audit | 10 October 2026

## Decision

**The proposed independent-rater, within-person days-to-weeks antecedent analysis is not demonstrated as executable from the five audited cohorts.** This is a specific feasibility failure, not a finding that antecedents do not exist or that relevant data exist nowhere.

The strongest available designs have different gaps: Yale offers approximately monthly clinical ratings; EMTICS offers weekly parent diaries but much sparser clinical and biological assessments; an adult-heavy four-week EMA study offers publicly listed files but self-rated, treatment-linked outcomes; Hoekstra offers weekly adult stress/tic reports without an independently rated longitudinal outcome; New Tics has a verified controlled-access archive but a developmental, largely annual schedule. Sources and qualifications appear below.

The project should retain **persistent, severe adult tics as a central population**, alongside all other ages and severities. Childhood improvement cannot substitute for explaining persistent adult burden. None of these sources verifies an adequately characterized persistent-severe-adult subgroup with the complete required measurement design.

No participant-level dataset was obtained or analyzed. No researcher was contacted, access application submitted, or clinical intervention undertaken. The result is an original study-metadata compatibility audit.

## Question and decision rule

**Feasibility hypothesis:** at least one documented existing cohort provides a lawful access route and enough repeated, appropriately timed measurements to separate a proposed antecedent from concurrent tic severity within people at days-to-weeks resolution, with adult persistence and severity coverage established.

**Prediction:** a qualifying cohort will document all of the following:

1. Repeated exposure and outcome observations with actual timestamps, completion delays, and explicit recall/integration windows. A weekly label on a diary is insufficient if its relevant entries or timing cannot be recovered.
2. An outcome observed independently of the exposure report, ideally assessed by someone masked to the exposure. An expert interview that relies on the same participant or parent is distinct from independently observed tics. Biological-assay masking is distinct from masking to psychosocial stress.
3. Enough nonoverlapping exposure–outcome windows and within-person variation for prespecified day-scale or weekly lags. Longer monthly lags can answer a narrower question but cannot reconstruct unobserved daily transitions.
4. Treatment timing, missingness, participant linkage, and appropriate confounder information. Sample size alone does not establish usable lag-pair counts or statistical power.
5. Documented adult inclusion, persistent symptom history, and severity/impairment coverage. Severe adults must be identifiable rather than inferred from a cohort mean or one high baseline score.
6. A verified route to the relevant records and terms permitting the proposed use, followed by the separate approvals required to obtain and process them. Public article access is not participant-data authorization.

These are the audit's explicit compatibility criteria, not a registered protocol or a retrospectively claimed preregistration. A “not demonstrated” item can mean an actual design mismatch or missing documentation; those are separated below.

The eventual scientific prediction remains: an earlier exposure improves later independently rated burden prediction beyond recent burden, between-person differences, time, and appropriately timed treatment/context. Reversed timing, only concurrent association, only shared-rater association, or no held-out improvement would weaken that prediction. This audit tests whether such an analysis can be mounted; it does not test the biological prediction itself.

## Source-level extraction

### Yale clinic cohort reported by Lin in 2007 and 2010

The two-year cohort enrolled 45 cases aged 7–17, including 37 with TS/chronic tics and eight with OCD alone, plus 41 controls. Baseline tic-score mean was 15.4, range 0–34, in the tic subgroup. Monthly expert YGTSS and parent PSS-P ratings were accompanied by four-monthly youth DLSS and clinician YCGSI ratings. Medication classes and changes were monitored monthly. The papers use different measurement schedules and a nearest-previous-observation lag; a standard fixed lag should not be assumed. Exact study-specific recall windows for all stress instruments were not recovered. [Lin 2007, Methods and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3073143/).

The overlapping 2010 report clarifies that routine monthly contacts were telephone interviews, with in-person visits and blood/throat samples about every four months. The mean contact interval was 32.3 days (SD 11.9); encounters ranged from 4 to 26, with eight intervals exceeding 100 days. Symptom raters were masked to laboratory results; laboratory staff were masked to clinical status. Event-triggered exacerbation visits and follow-ups added biological sampling. These reports are one cohort, not independent replication. [Lin 2010, Longitudinal Evaluations and Biological Specimens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2843763/).

**Interpretation:** potentially useful for a governed, monthly-scale reanalysis. Clinical expertise and laboratory masking do not establish psychosocial-exposure-independent outcome ascertainment. Monthly sampling cannot identify the onset day of a change. Adult severe persistence is not an established target stratum. No public release, data-use license, or formal request procedure was located in the inspected sources/searches; author correspondence details alone are not a verified access offer. Availability therefore remains unknown.

### EMTICS COURSE and its linked biological measurements

COURSE enrolled 715 children aged 3–16 with chronic tic disorders. Over roughly 16–18 months, four-monthly hospital visits alternated with telephone interviews, producing clinical contact every two months. Weekly parent diaries tracked worsening, illnesses, and events. YGTSS reflected the past week; parent stress questionnaires the past month. Exacerbations could trigger a visit preferably within one week and up to four weeks later, followed two months later. Medication was recorded; refusing specimens or omitting diaries did not automatically end participation. Planned biology included throat cultures/serology, immune measures, hair cortisol, baseline DNA, and event-linked RNA. [Schrag et al., protocol, COURSE procedures and Tables 2–3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00787-018-1190-4).

The executed GAS analysis reports 4,384 post-baseline observation points, including 2,272 visits. At least one GAS specimen was missing at 20.9% of those visits. It analyzed contemporaneous exposure/exacerbation associations and recurrent events; some proximate telephone/clinic observations were merged. Its data-availability statement offers de-identified demographic, clinical, and laboratory data upon request by qualified investigators to the authors. This is a documented request route, not an open license or approval already granted. [Martino et al. 2021, Data Analysis, Data Availability and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8032367/).

Hair cortisol needs special temporal care: the related ONSET analysis used four-monthly samples and interpreted them as cumulative exposure over earlier months, not a point measurement on collection day. That pediatric onset analysis and its reasonable-request statement do not prove access to every COURSE assay. [EMTICS hair-cortisol study, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10295562/).

**Interpretation:** promising for coarser, hypothesis-specific biological association work. Weekly diaries do not supply weekly independently rated severity. Symptom-triggered sampling can place the biomarker after the rise and change observation frequency when symptoms worsen. Hair's integration period can overlap the outcome window. A daily or weekly driver claim is therefore unsupported without additional timing documentation. Blinding to stress and complete assay-level availability were not established. No adults were recruited into this cohort.

### Hoekstra adult and pediatric weekly life-event cohort in 2004

The adult group comprised 32 people aged 18–64, with 28 analyzed; baseline clinician YGTSS mean was 23.4, range 10–50. Twelve fixed-weekday questionnaires assessed previous-week tic severity on a 0–10 self-rating and 46 undesirable life-event frequencies. Clinician YGTSS was baseline-only. Adults returned 317 questionnaires, an 82.6% response; the analytic subset contributed 290. Two early withdrawals and two people reporting no undesirable events were excluded. Weekly medication tracking recorded 38 changes. No repeated biological measure was described. Same-week adult association was reported, but no overall one-week-lag association. [Hoekstra et al. 2004, Methods, Tables and Results](https://www.psychiatrist.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/11020_association-small-life-events-self-reports-tic-severity.pdf).

**Interpretation:** the closest adult exposure design for a weekly stress question, but with shared-report outcomes and short individual series. Excluding people without events changes the target population. A baseline severe score does not document sustained severe adult symptoms. No public individual-data deposit, availability statement, formal request route, or reuse license was found. That is unverified availability, not evidence the records were destroyed or cannot be shared.

### Adult-heavy four-week EMA extension reported by Iverson in 2023

Thirty-one participants aged 15–64, mean 34.5, entered an open-label median-nerve-stimulation extension. Baseline mean YGTSS tic score was 24.9. They received two random daily prompts plus self-initiated pre/post-stimulation surveys over four weeks. Tic-frequency windows varied across minutes–hours; intensity was self-rated 0–5. Longitudinal EMA outcomes were self-reported; masked video ratings belonged to the preceding two-session laboratory RCT. Repeated exposures concerned device use/settings/discomfort, not repeated psychosocial stress, sleep, or biological assays. Treatment timing was self-selected. Recent/planned treatment changes were excluded in the parent RCT. One participant supplied no EMA; prompted responses were sometimes absent or delayed; 27 completed the final survey. [Iverson et al. 2023, Methods and Limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10095435/).

**Access:** the [OSF project](https://osf.io/2y7fs/) is public; metadata lists a study-data folder, two CSV files, and blank survey forms. Only public project/wiki/license/file-inventory metadata were inspected. No participant file contents were retrieved. The current [OSF project metadata](https://api.osf.io/v2/nodes/2y7fs/) links CC BY 4.0, whereas the [registered IPD-sharing plan](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05016765) describes CC0. Preserve this discrepancy and check file-specific terms before reuse; do not silently choose the more permissive statement.

**Interpretation:** a candidate for a separately authorized self-report measurement/missingness methods pilot, after schema and license checks. It cannot answer the specified stress/biology-to-independent-outcome question. Frequent measurement does not resolve confounding from symptom-driven treatment use. An adult-only count, adequate lag pairs, and a persistent-severe subgroup remain unverified.

### New Tics developmental cohort

The expanded report includes 89 recent-onset participants, chiefly recruited at ages 5–10, with a few older pilot participants. Visits were baseline and the 12-month tic-onset anniversary, then selected participants at 24, 36, and 48 months. Return counts were 79, 37, 36, and 32. Later visits were affected by pandemic restrictions and shortened observation. Assessments combined history, clinical examination, YGTSS, and remote observation during the tic-suppression paradigm. This is the expanded cohort containing the 2019 sample, not an independent replication. [Grossen et al. 2024, Methods and Tables 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11373539/).

The protocol includes clinical/neuropsychological measures and structural/functional MRI, with stable-dose psychoactive medication permitted at entry. It identifies NIMH Data Archive C2692 and an earlier Figshare project as data destinations; the older protocol's embargo statement is historical, not verified current status. [Black et al. 2020, Methods and Data Availability](https://jpbs.hapres.com/htmls/JPBS_1245_Detail.html).

**Access verified:** [NDA collection 2692](https://nda.nih.gov/edit_collection.html?id=2692), DOI [10.15154/msnp-s176](https://doi.org/10.15154/msnp-s176), currently lists 126 shared subjects, YGTSS, tic-suppression measures, diagnoses, clinical data, and imaging. The archive count covers its collection and is not the 89-person outcome denominator; nor does it prove that every variable exists at every visit. Record-level access requires an account and an approved request; public summaries may be inspected without access. [NDA access information](https://nda.nih.gov/landing_page.html).

**Interpretation:** a concrete access lead for developmental prognosis and ascertainment research. It cannot establish days/weeks dynamics or persistent-severe-adult mechanisms. A separate 2020 imaging paper names public supplementary individual data, but these were not downloaded; their existence does not change the sampling mismatch. [Imaging paper, Data Availability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7355974/).

## What was actually established

- Five primary cohort families were compared against the same explicit requirements. None documents the complete target design.
- One public file inventory and one controlled-access archive were verified. EMTICS has a published investigator-request route. Yale and Hoekstra access remains unverified.
- The most consequential incompatibility is the combination of cadence and ascertainment, not merely a shortage of papers or participants.
- Access does not fix missing instruments. Combining separate cohorts cannot manufacture within-person links between exposures and outcomes measured in different people.
- No phase model, stress-lag regression, participant-level missingness analysis, treatment estimate, or biological test was run. The companion JSON records this audit's categorical decisions, not individual health information.

## Next decision and minimum viable analysis

**Do not begin the proposed full lag analysis or label it feasible yet.** The next public-science step is a bounded protocol/schema audit of adult intensive cohorts and severe-adult longitudinal resources, looking for repeated independently observed tics plus independently measured exposures. Stop that audit when it either identifies one fully documented eligible design/access route or produces a dated, reproducible negative search with the exact remaining gaps. Do not replace the adult target with an available childhood sample.

If the scope is deliberately narrowed, three options remain distinct:

1. **Monthly pediatric stress association:** Yale may support this only after a verified access route, timestamps, rater procedures, and instrument windows are obtained. It would not establish a daily mechanism or adult applicability.
2. **Self-report methods work:** Iverson's public inventory is a real lead for assessing response delay, missingness, and treatment-linked EMA sampling. Any participant-data inspection needs a separately approved scope and appropriate data handling. Repeated stress/sleep/biological exposure is not documented, so the scientific target would have to change.
3. **Developmental prediction:** New Tics offers a structured controlled-access route for a different estimand. Such work should remain parallel to, rather than replace, persistent-adult mechanism research.

For any eventual qualifying dataset, prepare a prespecified analysis with exposure windows entirely preceding outcome windows; separate within-person deviations from between-person means; compare with a recent-severity baseline; evaluate forward in time; and report missingness/treatment-sensitive analyses. Verify that enough usable windows remain after those restrictions before power calculations. Do not interpolate sparse clinical visits into apparently observed daily data.

Persistent severe symptoms need a primary analysis of sustained burden, pain, impairment, and function, even when no dramatic quiet-to-peak transitions occur. A transition-only inclusion rule would select away precisely this population. When within-person outcome variation is too small to identify a lag effect, retain those people for the appropriate persistence estimand and report that identification limit instead of treating them as irrelevant.

## Nearby studies do not yet close the gap

An older Hoekstra infection cohort used weekly subjective reports for 24 weeks with monthly cultures and eight-weekly antibodies, but its “adult” category included ages above 15 and still lacked weekly independent tic ratings. It was not counted as a sixth full extraction. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15910212/).

The registered Wellen study plans 16 weeks of four-times-daily EMA, continuous wearables, and weekly tic videos. Its sample is 30 youths aged 12–17, with treatment during the middle eight weeks. The inspected registry record reports recruiting, estimated completion May 2029; this is a promising pipeline design, not an available adult dataset. [NCT06679790](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06679790). Registry plans and expected completion dates do not establish collected data, usable timestamps, or future data access.

## Access and governance caveats

A restricted-data route is a prerequisite, not an authorization. NDA uses permission groups and data-use conditions. Published NDA instructions describe research-institution affiliation, a research-use statement, named recipients, institutional signature, and access review; current collection-specific terms must be verified before any application. Some retrievable instructions are older, and the public C2692 view did not expose a usable permission-group value in this audit. Do not infer unrestricted research use. [Official access tutorial](https://nda.nih.gov/ndarpublicweb/Documents/Accessing%2BShared%2BData%2BSept_2021-1.pdf), [current access-information route](https://nda.nih.gov/nda/access-data-info).

No account, institutional eligibility, ethics determination, consent coverage, or secure analysis environment has been established for this project. Publishing an article under a Creative Commons license does not itself license its underlying participant records. Research-only data, including ostensibly de-identified records or recordings, must not be copied into this repository under its existing privacy rules.
