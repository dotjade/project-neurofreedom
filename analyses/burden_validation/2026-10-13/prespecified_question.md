# Prespecified bounded question

Recorded 2026-10-13 before new primary-source retrieval. Project NeuroFreedom.

Question: Does adult primary validation plus longitudinal evidence justify substituting YGTSS tic severity for patient-reported burden, and does it supply a validated diagnosis-appropriate severe-persistent-adult classification?

Prediction: Adult GTS-QOL will show relevant but incomplete cross-sectional association with tic severity; repeatability will not itself demonstrate responsiveness, meaningful change or treatment-effect surrogacy. Accessible longitudinal reports will not justify a universal tic-severity-only burden endpoint or a single severe cutoff across Tourette syndrome and motor-only/vocal-only diagnoses.

Disconfirming findings: A reproducible adult primary longitudinal validation with prespecified clinically important anchors and strong within-person severity–burden concordance, plus external diagnosis-specific threshold validation, would weaken the predicted no-go. Formal treatment-effect surrogacy needs more than an individual-level change association. Strong cross-sectional correlation alone cannot disconfirm that distinction. A verified adult meaningful-change threshold would permit that bounded claim even if substitution remains unjustified.

Plan: inspect 3–6 primary reports: original GTS-QOL development, an adult language adaptation, YGTSS severity benchmark, and up to three targeted longitudinal/responsiveness or anchor papers. Separate age, diagnosis, language, scale version, sample/cohort overlap and outcome windows. Preserve null/contradictory evidence and do not pool different constructs. Search primary public sources only; no participant records, paid access, source full-text storage, outreach or GitHub writes.

Computation gate: calculate only a published-aggregate quantity with identified inputs (for example interval reconstruction, classification arithmetic or change-estimate uncertainty when denominators/SDs are explicit). Never infer paired covariance from marginal SDs or invent change correlations. If estimates are non-comparable, do not meta-analyze.

Stop: after bounded primary set and focused searches resolve the endpoint/threshold decision, or explicit numerical/access prerequisites prevent it. Do not repeat completed registry, MNS, generic-cohort or sensory-gating tasks.
