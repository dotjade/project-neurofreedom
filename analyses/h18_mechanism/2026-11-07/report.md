# H18 behavioral mechanisms and generalization

Project NeuroFreedom | 7 November 2026 | Historical primary-evidence and causal-contrast audit

## Decision

Keep **H18 personalized component selection proposed**. The inspected evidence supports behavioral treatment and experimentally enhanced suppression, but does not validate a rule that matches a person to behavioral components and improves durable patient-centered outcomes over standard CBIT.

The useful distinction is now sharper:

- Global urge reduction has not been established as the necessary mediator of CBIT benefit.
- A baseline-urge treatment-interaction signal concerns **CBIT versus supportive therapy**, not which CBIT component to choose.
- A randomized reward manipulation improved trained suppression and a short observed-tic measure, while broader severity contrasts did not show a corresponding advantage. That result identifies a component signal, not established everyday generalization.

This advances the [recorded question](plan.md) by separating the causal contrasts, negative findings and missing measurements needed for H18. It is not a new clinical discovery or an exhaustive systematic review. No individual data were obtained, no treatment was delivered and no recommendation for changing treatment follows.

## What each comparison can establish

| Evidence | Actual comparison | What remains unidentified |
|---|---|---|
| Houghton 2017 | Randomized CBIT package versus supportive therapy; secondary analysis of global urge change | Randomized urge manipulation, tic-specific temporal mediation, component selection |
| McGuire 2015 | Package effect conditional on a baseline tic characteristic | Which tic was trained when; an untrained-tic effect; choice among components |
| Rotstein 2024 | Randomized crossover of immediate-contingent gameplay versus randomized outcomes with delayed feedback | Separate effects of timing, contingency and gameplay; clean carryover-free learning; incremental durable function |
| van de Griendt 2023 | Urge trajectories during one ERP cohort | Randomized habituation contrast; within-session clinical mediation; adult severe-subgroup effect |
| Peterson 2016 | Same original CBIT trials, examining new bothersome tics over ten weeks and other possible adverse changes | Equivalence, absence of all harms, positive transfer to untreated tics |

The first, second and fifth rows reuse the two original CBIT trials. They are different questions about overlapping participants, not three independent treatment replications. Participant-level randomization also does not turn every tic, session or time interval into a new independent randomized unit.

## Global urge reduction is not an established mediator

[Houghton et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2017.08.004) used the original youth trial (126 randomized) and older trial (122 randomized; eligibility began at age 16), with global PUTS and tic severity assessed at baseline, week 5 and week 10. Published bootstrap indirect effects were **0.003, 95% CI −0.21 to 0.31** in youth and **−0.03, −0.67 to 0.32** in the older group. Neither interval excludes zero. An adult-responder urge decline did not establish treatment-specific mediation. The manuscript says missing values were imputed but does not fully specify the procedure in the inspected methods; headline randomization counts must not substitute for model-specific analysis counts. [Author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5679290/).

These results weaken a claim that overall urge reduction is the demonstrated route to benefit. They do not establish zero mediation, rule out tic-specific changes, or identify an alternative mechanism. Change scores cover the same treatment interval, the mediator was not randomized, and global PUTS may miss the urges associated with particular trained tics. Responders are a postrandomization group. The appropriate uncertainty is measurement- and design-specific, not a choice between “habituation proven” and “habituation impossible.”

## A moderation signal does not supply a component rule

[McGuire et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2015.05.006) analyzed bothersome tics from the same two trials: 240 had usable baseline data and 207 had complete treatment data. The reported treatment-by-baseline-urge interaction was F(1,1167)=5.59, p<.015, partial eta-squared=.005. An F-distribution consistency check gives p≈.01823 for that statistic and those degrees of freedom, inconsistent with the printed p<.015. Both are retained without repairing the paper or reanalyzing participant data. CBIT favored supportive therapy for tics both with and without a baseline urge, with reported subgroup standardized effects .53 and .22 respectively. [Author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4449823/).

The report explicitly could not determine which tics were targeted at which point. Baseline bothersomeness could affect training priority, and the analysis did not measure a specified untreated-tic transfer effect. Its methods describe tic-level ANCOVA and Sidak pairwise comparisons, without a described participant-cluster adjustment. Within-person tic dependence therefore remains a precision concern; this audit does not reconstruct cluster-adjusted uncertainty. Most importantly, the arms are whole CBIT and supportive therapy. Even a reproducible interaction between those arms would not tell us which CBIT component works best for that subgroup, or whether a matching strategy improves function. This is a hypothesis-generating treatment-moderator signal, not merely prognosis, but it falls short of H18's proposed policy.

## Direct reward manipulation gives an endpoint-specific signal

[Rotstein et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.29875) randomized crossover order in 35 children aged 7–15. Immediate-contingent reward (ICR) linked game success to tic-free intervals; delayed reward (DR) randomized game outcomes. The reported in-game interval improvement was 1442±2250% versus 242±493%, with d=.57 and p=.0000285; these dispersions are SDs. The short Rush observation contrast favored ICR (p=.002, FDR=.03), but YGTSS (p=.98), parent PTQ (p=.42) and urge contrasts were nonsignificant. No equivalence conclusion follows. [Full audit](reward-component-audit.md).

This is a joint reward/gameplay manipulation, not an isolated dopamine, habituation or timing mechanism. Learning can persist across crossover periods, and sequence effects were observed. The authors discuss suppression during brief observation as an alternative to spontaneous improvement. Whole-program pre/post and three-month changes do not isolate ICR's contribution. A separately randomized booster contrast did not show extra PTQ improvement. Confidence intervals for the focal clinical contrasts and supplement-specific harms details were not verified. The main text/registry give 35 participants, while a figure caption gives 36; recruitment counts also differ between paper and registration. These discrepancies remain unresolved, and outcome-specific follow-up denominators were not inferred.

The [ClinicalTrials.gov registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06348511) was posted after the reported study ended. The [OSF registration](https://osf.io/af74w/) was dated during the study and describes registration before analysis, not before recruitment. Blinding applied to assessors and video annotators, not participants or live tic coders. These qualifications matter when interpreting the component result.

## Counterevidence and scope checks

The supportive [Verdellen 2008 ERP study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18285507/) reported within- and between-session urge declines during ten two-hour sessions, associated with tic frequency. Its abstract was inspected, not its full numerical tables; it cannot be treated as randomized mediation. Preserve this positive observation rather than omitting it.

In [van de Griendt et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm12227087), 29 participants aged 7–59 received twelve weekly one-hour visits: two training visits followed by ten measured ERP sessions. The aggregate within-session urge pattern rose during the first 15 minutes and remained elevated for the remainder. The piecewise model fit better than the other tested models but had weak absolute fit (RMSEA=.26; χ²(21)=61.56), so its apparent trajectory is not a well-validated model. The paper reports clinical improvement in this cohort, but did not measure YGTSS every session and pooled sessions for its trajectory analysis. Thus it did not directly test within-session mediation or between-session habituation. Frequent urge questioning could itself affect ratings. This is the one-hour cohort from the [2018 open comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29325470/), not a new randomized duration experiment. One-hour versus two-hour findings across these reports cannot isolate a causal dose effect. The 2023 authors declare no conflicts and state their listed current employers were not involved; this is an author declaration, not independent clearance. Funding was not established in the inspected sections.

The older [ERP-versus-HRT trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15033497/) compared packages in 43 participants. Its abstract reports improvements in both arms without demonstrated superiority on its endpoints; that is not equivalence or validation of shared mechanism. The [four-child sequential component study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8995831/) illustrates different responses to added components but does not randomize an individualized selection policy. These are retained as bounded context, not pooled into an efficacy estimate.

## Generalization and burden must remain separate

[Peterson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2015.09.001) examined symptom substitution in the same CBIT trials: 228 participants had usable data, 207 complete at all three assessments. No higher occurrence of newly reported bothersome tics during the ten-week treatment period was demonstrated for CBIT versus support. This endpoint was not exhaustive detection of all new tics. Adverse events were elicited at visits regardless of attributed cause. This challenges a substitution concern, but does not demonstrate benefit in untreated tics or exclude rare, delayed, effort-related or subgroup harms. [Author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5758863/).

No compatible randomized component-allocation contrast with durable function, pain or participation benefit was verified in this bounded map. The selected pediatric and age-mixed samples cannot establish a severe-persistent-adult effect. Older adults, pain, cognitive effort, co-occurring conditions and daily participation remain required outcomes rather than gaps to fill with tic counts. Persistent symptoms do not indicate inadequate effort or voluntary tics.

## Registry lead and next bounded decision

The currently retrieved [NCT06825520](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06825520) record describes a small single-group urge-focused HRT study, estimated enrollment six, ages 8–17. It has no posted results. Its last update was May 2025; “recruiting” is the saved registry label, not verified present-day availability. Estimated completion dates that have passed do not establish completion. It is not a randomized component-selection trial. Severe tic scores and co-occurring conditions that interfere with participation are excluded. [Selected official fields](registry-selected-fields.json).

Close this fixed source map. Reopen it for a genuinely new randomized matching/transfer result, useful aggregate correction or missing methods, not another search of the same studies. The next feasible H18 step is a clearly labelled synthetic identifiability test: can sparse global urge measurements distinguish reduced urge generation from improved action control despite unchanged urges? Specify competing generative models, measurement error, participant-level repeated measures and a context-transfer check before fitting. A successful simulation would validate only the proposed analysis under its assumptions; it would not identify anyone's mechanism or personalize treatment. Clinical matching would still require a governed, equally intensive randomized strategy comparison with function, effort, adverse effects and durable transfer.
