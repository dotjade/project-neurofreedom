# Original structured extraction notes

Source: McGuire et al., PMID 31451122, DOI 10.1017/S0033291719002150, PMC7190082 author manuscript. Extracted 16 October 2026. The numeric rows are independently organized in aggregate_inputs.json; this file explains their meanings and missing quantities.

## Design and sampling

- Same three-site CBIT/PST randomized trial as Wilhelm 2012, NCT00231985. Recruitment December 2005–May 2009. Eight sessions in ten weeks; computer randomization stratified by site and tic-medication status. Clinician assessors masked to treatment; patients providing self-reports were not thereby masked.
- 122 randomized: CBIT 63, PST 59. Figure 1: acute analyzed 60/53, acute completed 56/49. Table headings use randomized counts, not verified endpoint-specific analyzed n.
- Ages 16–69, mean 31.60 (SD 13.7); 63.9% male, 80.3% Caucasian per source. Diagnoses 103 Tourette, 18 chronic motor, one chronic vocal. Stable tic medication in 31/122; one PST participant changed it acutely. Lifetime comorbidity counts do not establish currently severe symptoms.
- Eligibility: age≥16, at least moderate clinician severity, YGTSS total tic≥14 or≥10 for motor/vocal-only, English fluency, estimated IQ>80. Schizophrenia/pervasive developmental disorders excluded; co-occurring conditions needing more immediate treatment excluded. No severe-only or age≥18 functional estimate verified.

## Measures

- SDS self-report: work/social/family disability and a separate overall item as described by source; reported total's exact scoring reconciliation not given. Lower is less disability. Preserve version ambiguity rather than invent scale bounds or a meaningful-change threshold.
- FAM-III brief 14-item family functioning: four-point items, higher impairment worse.
- SAS-SR: primary occupation/role, leisure, extended family, primary relationship, parental role, family unit; per-item averages for applicable answered items; higher maladjustment worse. Exact analysis denominators are particularly important for relationship/parenting roles.
- Psychiatric outcomes: Y-BOCS total/obsessions/compulsions; ADHD-RS-IV total, hyperactivity/impulsivity and inattention subscales; BDI-II; BAI; trait/state anger. These are distinct from function, tic severity and GTS-QOL.
- CGI-I 1/2 defines acute response. It does not define tic absence, pain resolution or cure.

## Analysis and uncertainty

Source uses repeated-measures ANOVA for treatment and response-status changes. It labels the acute principle intent-to-treat while restricting analysis to at least one postrandomization treatment session. Exact outcome-level n, F degrees of freedom, covariance and confidence intervals for functional contrasts are absent. Do not assume the prior trial's mixed-model specification automatically supplies them here. Tables label marginal means as adjusted but do not fully specify additional covariate adjustment.

The authors explicitly label exploratory analyses and do not correct across hypotheses. Follow-up pairwise tests use Bonferroni. Corrected pairwise tests do not adjust every omnibus outcome test.

## Distinct findings

- Table 1 functional tests: 12, none nominally p<.05. SDS/FAM/SAS results stored with displayed means and SEs, F,p,partial eta-squared. Psychiatric obsession p=.04 is the only named acute nominal advantage; other psychiatric tests are nonsignificant.
- Table 2 responders 28 versus nonresponders 94 pooled across assignment: SDS family p=.02, SAS parenting p=.02, remaining functional and psychiatric contrasts nonsignificant. These are associations after selection on response.
- Table 3: only CBIT acute responders followed, narrative/figure n=15; Table 3 parenting n=1 complete, no inferential statistic. Returnee composition and missingness vary in relevance by outcome; do not assign n=15 automatically to all rows. Three monthly boosters were offered/planned; actual individual attendance was not verified. PST follow-up prose 2/figure 1 conflict; none analyzed in Table 3.
- Table 3 total disability omnibus p=.03 but only posttreatment has the baseline-difference marker; exact baseline-six-month p absent. Work omnibus p=.04, both post and six-month marked, reported pairwise p=.04/.03. Social p=.06; family p=.11; FAM p=.61; overall SAS p=.40. Inattention omnibus p=.02; post-six-month p=.03 but baseline-six-month p=.16.
- No significant tic-change/function-change correlations reported. These nulls neither establish nor disprove a tic-mediated pathway.

## Reporting conflicts and limits

Figure 1 screened 172−excluded 60≠randomized 122; no corrected count is known. Figure PST follow-up 1 conflicts with narrative 2. The report's broad total-disability six-month prose exceeds the specific pairwise documentation. Other visibly unusual numerical cells outside the targeted functional extraction were not silently corrected or used for computations. Public full text is the author manuscript; final-published full tables were not separately inspected.
