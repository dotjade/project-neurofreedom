# Acute CBIT functioning: randomized contrasts, responder associations and selected follow-up

Project NeuroFreedom | 16 October 2026 | Historical secondary-report audit

## Decision

The same-trial functioning report does **not establish a general acute randomized advantage for CBIT over psychoeducation/supportive therapy on disability, family functioning or social adjustment**. All 12 reported acute functional contrasts were nonsignificant at the nominal .05 level (p=.13–.70). This is not equivalence or proof that meaningful benefit is absent.

Positive family/parenting findings concern **postrandomization responder associations**, while later disability findings concern **selected CBIT responders who returned after a booster-inclusive program**. Neither is an independent replication or a preserved randomized long-term treatment comparison. The prospective [prediction](prespecified_question.md) survives: broader benefit must remain endpoint- and estimand-specific. Close this same-trial chain unless corrected numerical reporting materially changes it.

## Sources and population

[McGuire et al., Effect of behavior therapy for Tourette’s disorder on psychiatric symptoms and functioning in adults](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291719002150), online 27 August 2019; Psychological Medicine 50(12):2046–2056, September 2020; [PMID 31451122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31451122/); [PMC7190082 author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190082/). The publisher abstract and publication dates were checked; detailed extraction uses the publicly accessible NIH author manuscript, not a verified line-by-line comparison with final published tables.

This is the Wilhelm 2012 trial, NCT00231985: 122 randomized, 63 CBIT and 59 PST, eight sessions over ten weeks. Ages **16–69**, mean 31.6, include minors. Diagnoses were Tourette disorder (103), chronic motor tics (18), and chronic vocal tics (1). No age-18-and-over or severe-persistent-adult functional effect estimate was supplied in the inspected report. Eligibility and clinical-stability restrictions remain relevant; co-occurring conditions requiring more immediate treatment were excluded. The sample was predominantly male and White, and English/IQ eligibility restricted recruitment. Age-interaction nulls do not establish equal effects across ages.

## Three questions that must not be combined

| Analysis | What is compared | Denominator information | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acute randomized, Table 1 | Baseline-to-week-10 change by treatment assignment | Headers 63/59 randomized; Figure 1 says 60/53 acute analyzed | Incremental acute effect relative to an active supportive comparison, with incomplete outcome-specific reporting |
| Acute response status, Table 2 | Change in CGI-I responders versus nonresponders, pooled across treatments | Headers 28/94; exact row-specific analyzed numbers absent | Postrandomization association, not a randomized response intervention or mediation test |
| CBIT follow-up, Table 3 | Within selected responders across baseline, posttreatment and six months | Narrative/figure 15 CBIT returnees; parental role explicitly only one complete participant | Selected completer time profile; no randomized long-term comparator |

Figure 1 separately reports 56/49 acute completers, so 122 randomized, 113 acute analyzed and 105 completers are not interchangeable. The methods describe an intent-to-treat principle restricted to people with at least one postrandomization treatment session; this does not establish complete outcome data for all 122. No row-specific degrees of freedom, sample counts or comprehensive missing-data procedure are supplied. Role-dependent SAS-SR items especially cannot inherit the headline sample size without verification.

Responders were offered/planned to receive three monthly boosters; actual individual attendance was not verified. Nonresponders did not complete these follow-up assessments. The report says two PST responders returned but its figure says one; no PST follow-up outcome analysis was performed. Returnee baseline comparisons were nonsignificant, which does not establish missing-at-random follow-up. No all-randomized, off-treatment, uninterrupted-benefit or disease-modification inference follows.

## Instruments and direct acute functional findings

The self-reported Sheehan Disability Scale (SDS) addresses work, social and family disability. The methods describe three domain items on a ten-point scale plus a separate five-point overall disability item, but do not clearly reconcile that description with the table's reported total. Preserve the source-specific scoring description rather than silently substituting another SDS version or asserting a verified total range. Lower reported disability is better. The FAM-III short form measures family impairment; lower scores are better. SAS-SR domains are per-item averages of applicable answered items, with higher scores reflecting worse adjustment. These are **not GTS-QOL** and do not directly measure a tic-free state or pain-specific benefit.

Table 1 reports adjusted means/standard errors, F statistics, rounded p-values and partial eta-squared. The functional tests are:

For orientation, displayed SDS total means decline 8.36→5.00 with CBIT and 8.32→5.79 with PST. The arithmetic difference between those rounded changes is −0.83, but that is not a reconstructed model contrast with verified precision. On SDS social, the displayed change is larger in PST; on parental role, the CBIT mean increases. Numerical improvement in selected cells cannot replace the randomized contrast or prove a clinically important advantage.

The paper's one nominal acute psychiatric advantage is the Y-BOCS obsession subscale (F=4.48, p=.04, partial eta-squared=.04). Y-BOCS total, compulsions, ADHD total/subscales, depression, anxiety and anger did not show nominal treatment superiority. Baseline psychiatric scores were generally low and participants needing more urgent comorbidity treatment were excluded. This limits extrapolation to clinically severe comorbid states; the obsession result is exploratory and does not prove treatment of diagnosed OCD.

## What the positive findings actually support

Table 2's response-status associations are SDS family (F=5.67, p=.02, partial eta-squared=.05) and SAS-SR parental role (F=6.94, p=.02, partial eta-squared=.37). The other ten functional contrasts, including total disability, family assessment and overall social adjustment, were not nominally significant. A large partial eta-squared for parenting without a reported row-specific denominator or confidence interval is not a precise general population effect. Selection by observed CGI-I response, potential common causes and pooled treatments prevent a causal interpretation that tic improvement itself produced better family life.

Table 3's selected CBIT follow-up reports omnibus time tests for SDS total (F=4.79, p=.03, partial eta-squared=.42) and work disability (F=4.09, p=.04, partial eta-squared=.39). Crucially:

- SDS total means are 7.60, 4.00 and 4.20. The table marks posttreatment as different from baseline, **not the six-month cell**. The prose reports the baseline-to-posttreatment comparison at p=.02 but supplies no numerical baseline-to-six-month total-disability p-value. Its broader wording about improvement at both visits must not be converted into a verified significant six-month pairwise effect.
- SDS work means are 2.60, 1.27 and 1.20. Both later cells are marked different from baseline; prose gives p=.04 and .03 respectively. These remain selected within-group comparisons.
- Social disability is p=.06, family disability p=.11, FAM-III p=.61 and overall SAS-SR p=.40. The other tested social-adjustment domains are also nonsignificant. Parental role has one complete participant and no inferential test.
- Inattention has an omnibus p=.02, but the reported corrected pairwise result is posttreatment-to-six-month p=.03; baseline-to-six-month is p=.16. Thus “improvement at six months” cannot silently mean a verified improvement relative to baseline for every outcome.
- Reported correlations between tic-score change and psychiatric/functional change are nonsignificant. This neither establishes mediation nor rules out an association: selected samples are small and correlation uncertainty is not supplied.

These functional outcomes should supplement the original trial's separately measured clinician tic impairment; none is interchangeable with tic severity or disease-specific quality of life.

## Executed aggregate sensitivity and uncertainty limits

The [plan](computation_plan.md), [numeric inputs](aggregate_inputs.json), [code](aggregate_checks.py), [outputs](aggregate_checks.json), [tests](test_aggregate_checks.py) and [execution log](execution_log.txt) preserve a deterministic published-aggregate audit. It counts functional tests and applies explicitly **post hoc, restricted-family** Holm/Bonferroni sensitivities separately to 12 acute randomized tests, 12 response-status tests and 11 tested follow-up outcomes.

- Acute randomized: 0/12 nominal positives, and none after correction.
- Response-status: 2/12 nominal positives; each printed p=.02 becomes .24 under either correction.
- Selected follow-up: 2/11 nominal positives; total disability becomes .33 under both, work becomes .40 under Holm and .44 under Bonferroni.

No functional result remains below .05 under these sensitivities. This does **not** establish no benefit, retroactively create a prespecified testing plan or adjust the complete trial's testing. It shows that the isolated exploratory positives are sensitive even to restricted within-estimand multiplicity control. The source explicitly did not adjust across exploratory hypotheses; it did use Bonferroni for follow-up pairwise comparisons. These are different levels of adjustment.

Thirteen implementation tests passed. An independent source/numeric audit matched 363 numerical cells and passed 144 output/marker checks; see the [independent review](review/REVIEW.md). These checks are reproducibility verification, not clinical peer review. Reported p-values are rounded. No F degrees of freedom, endpoint covariance, standard error of a change/contrast, confidence interval, equivalence margin, meaningful-change threshold, joint outcome or missing participant outcome was invented. Marginal standard errors and partial eta-squared point estimates do not provide those missing quantities. The absence of confidence intervals for the functional treatment contrasts prevents a defensible “important benefit ruled out” conclusion.

## Reporting conflicts and safety boundaries

The visually checked [flow figure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7190082/figure/F1/) reports 172 screened and 60 excluded but 122 randomized: subtraction gives 112, not 122. It also gives one PST follow-up returnee versus two in prose. These inconsistencies are preserved; this audit cannot determine corrected replacement counts. They do not change the extracted Table 1 p-values, but strengthen the reason to avoid unreported denominator reconstruction.

No new systematic adverse-event comparison is established in this secondary functioning report. Failure to find worsening in a psychiatric scale is not proof of no adverse effects. NIMH and TAA/AAN/ABF support are disclosed, alongside author consultancy, speaker and publishing/royalty relationships. The authors state no conflicts associated with the work; that statement does not erase the detailed disclosures. No individual medical advice or treatment change follows.

Move from this completed CBIT reporting chain to **adult tic-related pain and unmet-care measurement**. A verified next primary source is [Małek 2025, Coping with pain among adults with chronic tic disorders](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088/full), published 30 July 2025; PMID 40808742; DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1537088. It is not a new October 2026 finding and was absent from the current evidence ledger checked here.

Ask which pain quantities are actually identified: current versus worst pain, multidimensional pain versus tic severity/impairment, denominator-specific proportions, recruitment/diagnostic selection, and self-reported usefulness of care versus causal treatment effectiveness. Test whether the paper supports a pain-specific endpoint rather than inferring it from a tic score. Extract population coverage, temporal design, negative findings and uncertainty before deciding on any aggregate calculation. Stop at a structured measurement/estimand decision if representative prevalence, a severe-adult subgroup or causal treatment data are missing. This advances the broad adult-burden mission without another generic cohort search, unchanged CBIT computation, participant collection or clinical experiment.
