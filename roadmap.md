# Project NeuroFreedom research and wellbeing roadmap

Evidence checked on 8 October 2026. This document covers deliverables 7 through 10. It is an evidence-informed research proposal and a family discussion framework, not a treatment prescription or an approved human-subject protocol. No patient-identifying information is included. No researchers have been contacted, data-access agreements accepted, or participants enrolled.

## 7 Scientific Experiment Roadmap

### The scientific objective

The ambition is to discover interventions that produce safe, lasting, meaningful benefit for people with Tourette syndrome. A cure cannot currently be promised: CDC continues to state that Tourette syndrome has no cure, while effective care can reduce symptoms and impairment. Clinical care should proceed now, independently of whether a research program succeeds. [CDC treatment overview](https://www.cdc.gov/tourette-syndrome/treatment/index.html)

The program should distinguish four claims before evaluating any candidate:

1. **Symptom relief:** improvement during treatment. This is valuable even if treatment must continue.
2. **Durable benefit:** improvement persists over a prespecified follow-up period. Continuing learned behavioral skills or ongoing treatment must be recorded; these cannot be described as treatment-free recovery.
3. **Disease modification:** a controlled study supports a change in the underlying clinical course beyond ordinary fluctuation, development, concomitant care, expectancy, and ongoing symptomatic effects. Mechanistic evidence strengthens this claim but cannot replace clinical evidence.
4. **Cure:** a much stronger claim requiring independently replicated, sustained recovery, acceptable long-term safety, and convincing evidence that ongoing disease-directed treatment is unnecessary. No finite short trial proves lifelong cure. The program must agree on an operational definition with patients, clinicians, and regulators before using the term as an outcome.

Tics can become much less troublesome without disappearing. A prospective recent-onset cohort found persistence on careful assessment even when children were no longer bothered. The distinction matters: children should not be made to feel unsuccessful because benign tics remain. [Prospective natural-history study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-40133-4)

### Target product profile proposed for discussion

- Primary value: less pain, injury, distress, interference, and unwanted effort, with better participation in the life the person wants.
- Tic benefit: clinically meaningful improvement measured with a validated scale and an independent assessment, without substituting sedation or forced suppression for wellbeing.
- Durability: prespecified follow-up extending beyond the active intervention, where safe and scientifically appropriate, with rescue care available and all further treatments documented.
- Safety: acceptable developmental, cognitive, mood, metabolic, sleep, movement, and other modality-specific effects; no reliance on a favorable tic score alone.
- Burden and access: realistic cost, travel, training time, adherence demands, and availability across different communities.
- Scope: a treatment may work for a biologically or clinically defined subgroup. Success in that subgroup must not be advertised as a universal Tourette cure.

These are proposed program criteria, not an existing regulatory standard for a Tourette cure.

### Work package A Establish a reliable evidence and natural-history baseline

**Question:** Which apparent treatment signals exceed expected tic fluctuation, developmental change, placebo or attention effects, and selective reporting?

**Experiment:** A preregistered evidence synthesis and, where approved data can be obtained, participant-level reanalysis of completed trials and longitudinal cohorts. Extract diagnostic criteria, age, tic and impairment outcomes separately, comorbidities, treatment exposure, follow-up duration, attrition, adverse effects, funding, registration, and analysis changes. Compare registered endpoints with published results. Separate randomized comparisons from uncontrolled extensions and case reports.

**Primary output:** An auditable evidence matrix, a map of unresolved questions, and realistic estimates of variability and attrition for future sample-size calculations. A neutral or negative conclusion is a valid result.

**Go gate:** At least one question has a prespecified estimand, usable data, sufficient outcome comparability, and an independent analyst able to reproduce the core result.

**No-go gate:** The conclusion depends on pooling incompatible endpoints, treating a within-person before–after change as a randomized effect, ignoring duplicate cohorts, or claiming that a brief low-tic period demonstrates cure. Keep such evidence descriptive.

**Why start here:** Behavioral treatment already has controlled evidence. The 2010 pediatric CBIT trial randomized 126 participants aged 9–17 and reported greater improvement than supportive therapy and education. The ORBIT trial tested therapist-supported internet-delivered exposure and response prevention against online psychoeducation. These trials provide design lessons, not evidence that an individual child must enroll in research. [CBIT trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20483969/); [ORBIT trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34480868/)

### Work package B Test developmental and clinical subgroups using existing data

**Question:** Do candidate clinical or biological features predict persistent impairment or differential response better than simple clinical information?

**Experiment:** Begin with existing longitudinal, imaging, or clinical datasets. Prespecify a modest baseline model using age, baseline severity and impairment, relevant comorbidities, and treatment exposure. Compare any proposed biomarker against that model. Split data by person and, where possible, by site; hold the external test dataset untouched. Analyze age continuously when feasible and test developmental differences explicitly. Cross-sectional age differences must not be presented as observed within-person maturation.

**Primary output:** An externally tested prediction or stratification model, including calibration, uncertainty, missing-data behavior, subgroup performance, and whether the added information would change a decision.

**Go gate:** A new feature adds reproducible, clinically useful information in an independent setting, beyond the basic clinical model. A prospective validation protocol is needed before clinical use.

**No-go gate:** Performance collapses outside the training site, relies on scanner or medication artifacts, excludes the most difficult cases, or is useful only after extensive unnecessary testing. No predictive label should be assigned to a child from an exploratory model.

**Current opportunity and limitation:** A May 2026 ENIGMA-TS preprint assembled 1,803 participants and retained 1,648 after quality control. It supports a large-scale research opportunity; it is not peer-reviewed clinical validation, and its age-related structural findings do not establish a diagnostic test or an individual prognosis. The full title is “Altered neurodevelopmental trajectories of brain structure in Tourette syndrome and Chronic Tic Disorders,” posted 20 May 2026, DOI 10.64898/2026.05.16.26353368. [ENIGMA preprint record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42238458/); [primary preprint PDF](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.16.26353368v1.full.pdf)

### Work package C Connect genetic evidence to specific biological mechanisms

**Question:** Which genetic signals converge on a reproducible cell type, developmental period, or pathway that is plausible to modify safely?

**Experiment:** Reanalyze appropriately licensed summary statistics and, only with institutional authorization, controlled-access sequence data. Use fine-mapping, functional annotation, colocalization where suitable data permit, and cross-dataset replication. Triangulate common-variant results with rare-variant evidence and relevant human tissue or cell references. Record sample overlap, ancestry, linkage disequilibrium assumptions, tissue limitations, and uncertainty about the direction of effect. Mendelian randomization or a transcriptome association is a hypothesis-strengthening analysis, not automatic proof of a therapeutic mechanism.

**Primary output:** A small ranked target list with a separate evidence dossier for each target: human evidence, alternative explanations, tractability, developmental risks, and one experiment that could falsify it.

**Go gate:** Convergent human evidence supports an interpretable biological hypothesis; the proposed intervention direction is justified; independent replication is possible.

**No-go gate:** A target rests on one suggestive association, a gene near a signal without causal support, a post hoc pathway story, or an ancestry-specific score promoted as an individual clinical test.

**Evidence anchor:** The 2025 issue publication of a GWAS meta-analysis studied roughly 9,600 tic-disorder cases and approximately 981,000 controls of European ancestry. The authors describe separate access arrangements for public results and 23andMe-derived data. This is valuable for discovery, with clear limits on ancestry generalization and participant-level access. [GWAS primary study and data statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12036792/)

### Work package D Replicate and perturb candidate mechanisms in existing human cell models

**Question:** Does a target cause a reproducible cellular or circuit-relevant phenotype, and can that phenotype be corrected without impairing general neuronal health?

**Experiment:** A qualified laboratory can use existing consented iPSC resources, multiple unrelated donors, balanced culture batches, blinded assessment, and more than one independent assay. Genetic perturbation or correction, where appropriate, should be evaluated alongside suitable controls. The donor is a biological unit; many cells or organoids from one donor are not independent patients. Consider isogenic comparisons to reduce background-genome confounding, while retaining diverse donor lines to test generalizability.

**Readouts:** A prespecified mechanistic phenotype plus viability, maturation, cell composition, and functional readouts that could expose a nonspecific or toxic explanation. A rescue should be tested in an independent laboratory or independent donor set before major translational investment.

**Go gate:** The phenotype replicates; target perturbation changes it in the predicted direction; the effect is not explained by altered cell survival or general developmental disruption; a safe translational strategy remains plausible.

**No-go gate:** The result appears only in one donor or batch, uses cells as independent patients, or improves a marker by damaging or silencing the preparation. Stop the target, not the broader search.

**Evidence anchor:** A 2022 study of basal-ganglia organoids from five Tourette donors reported developmental patterning and interneuron differences. This is a small human model study, not proof that all Tourette syndrome has one cause or that changing the implicated pathway would be safe in a child. The proposed work here is replication and validation, not direct treatment. [Primary organoid study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949887/)

### Work package E Test circuit mechanisms and measurement quality

**Question:** Are candidate circuit signals stable enough to measure, specific enough to interpret, and linked to meaningful benefit rather than movement artifact or generalized suppression?

**Experiment:** First analyze existing consented recordings and previously acquired imaging. For any later prospective study, use an accredited research team, ethics approval, proportionate procedures, and age-appropriate risk review. Prefer consenting adults for early novel mechanistic intervention work when scientifically appropriate. Adult findings cannot be assumed to transfer to developing children. Do not implant a device solely to gather exploratory data, or collect research-only scans from the child described by this project.

**Readouts:** Test–retest reliability; artifact sensitivity; correspondence with independently rated tics, urges, and function; and replication at another site. If a device is evaluated, include a credible control, blinding assessment, adverse-event collection, and planned off-intervention follow-up where safe.

**Go gate:** Measurement quality is adequate, the signal survives prespecified artifact checks, and benefit extends to patient-important outcomes. Progress to a clinical trial requires a separate regulatory and safety decision.

**No-go gate:** The biomarker merely reflects visible movement, a candidate reduces activity through sedation, or apparent persistence is based on an uncontrolled selected subgroup.

**Current example:** A September 2026 median-nerve-stimulation paper reports three-month follow-up in participants who received active stimulation. It is a durability signal to investigate. The follow-up description is not a randomized between-group demonstration of disease modification, and it does not establish a cure or justify do-it-yourself stimulation. [Primary follow-up report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42704310/)

### Work package F Improve access to established care while testing long-term value

**Question:** Can evidence-based care become easier to obtain and improve everyday functioning for more people?

**Experiment:** With a clinical service and appropriate review, evaluate implementation of trained-therapist CBIT or evidence-based remote behavioral care. Outcomes should include waiting time, completion, acceptability, cost, child-chosen goals, impairment, quality of life, and adverse experiences, as well as tic severity. If comparing delivery formats, use a prespecified controlled design rather than assuming every digital adaptation reproduces the original trial.

**Go gate:** The delivery model preserves clinical quality and benefit while reducing burden or improving access. Report who did not benefit and why.

**No-go gate:** Better completion numbers conceal lower wellbeing, excessive parental workload, digital exclusion, or pressure to suppress tics for others' comfort.

This package can generate useful benefit without claiming a new biological cure. Current UK implementation work on ORBIT illustrates the distinction between an efficacy study and getting an intervention into routine services. [NIHR ORBIT translation award](https://fundingawards.nihr.ac.uk/award/NIHR205467)

### Work package G Translate only candidates that pass earlier gates

A human interventional program requires a qualified sponsor, clinical investigators, regulatory review where applicable, independent ethics review, an explicit adverse-event and rescue-care plan, and trial registration before enrollment. Candidate-specific toxicology, manufacturing or device quality, interactions, and developmental safety must be addressed by the responsible specialists.

The subsequent sequence is safety and feasibility, controlled proof of benefit, independent multisite confirmation, and longer-term follow-up. These stages can overlap only when justified by the relevant authorities, not to meet this project's calendar. Pediatric involvement requires its own scientific justification, risk–benefit analysis, parental permission, and appropriate affirmative assent. A parent's wish to find a cure does not substitute for the child's willingness. [HHS child-research guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/guidance/faq/children-research/index.html)

### Requirements shared by all work packages

- Write the primary question, outcome, analysis, exclusions, missing-data approach, and success/futility criteria before looking at confirmatory results.
- Base sample sizes on a justified effect size, variability, clustering, attrition, and power or precision analysis. Do not invent a universal sample size.
- Separate exploratory analyses from confirmatory tests; correct for multiplicity where required.
- Use appropriate randomization, allocation concealment, blinded outcome assessment, and intention-to-treat analysis in trials. Record when blinding is impossible.
- Report absolute changes, between-group differences, uncertainty, and harms. Statistical significance alone is not a patient benefit.
- Record age, sex, ancestry where scientifically justified, comorbidities, medication and therapy exposure, and social context without treating people as interchangeable subgroups.
- Prespecify the clinically meaningful benefit with patients and clinicians. Do not select a convenient response threshold after results are known.
- Build an independent replication budget and a publication plan for null findings into every funded package.
- Use appropriately governed existing data before exposing new participants to burden. No private child data belong in public repositories, AI services, or collaborator emails without specific, informed authorization.

## 8 Safe Personalized Study Framework for a Child

### Purpose and boundaries

The suitable family-level activity is an optional, low-burden care-support observation plan. It can help a clinician understand what matters to a child and whether ordinary care is helping. It cannot identify a cause, select a drug, validate a cure, or substitute for clinical assessment. No age, diagnosis details, or current care have been assumed for any specific child.

The child's comfort and participation in everyday life take priority over collecting complete data. The child is not responsible for solving Tourette syndrome. The research program can proceed entirely through literature, existing datasets, and appropriately governed laboratories without making this child a participant.

### Start with a clinician and one child-chosen goal

A pediatric clinician familiar with tic disorders should confirm the clinical picture and identify the main source of difficulty. Discuss tics alongside pain, injury, sleep, school participation, anxiety, OCD, ADHD, learning needs, and bullying when relevant. Tic frequency may not be the main treatment target. Imaging, EEG, and other testing are not routine requirements for establishing a tic-disorder diagnosis; additional tests should answer a clinical question identified by the treating clinician. [European assessment guideline](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00787-021-01842-2)

Ask a simple question such as: “What would make your day easier?” Suitable goals might involve comfortably attending a favorite activity, less pain, easier sleep, or feeling accepted at school. Avoid goals that require hiding harmless tics or pleasing observers. Do not suggest that Tourette syndrome is caused by inadequate effort or parenting.

### Proposed optional observation schedule

This schedule is a practical suggestion for discussion, not a validated clinical instrument.

- Agree on a brief review window, for example four to six weeks, only if useful to the clinician and acceptable to the child.
- Offer a short check-in no more than once a week, roughly one or two minutes, with an explicit “skip today” choice. A parent can keep brief notes privately if the child prefers not to answer.
- Do not interrupt play, school, sleep, or comfortable activities to count tics. Do not ask repeatedly whether the child is ticcing.
- Review the notes at an already planned clinical appointment or another clinician-agreed time. Stop collecting when the notes no longer inform a decision.
- A skipped week is missing information, not a failed task. The child receives no reward or punishment based on tic frequency or study compliance.

### A minimal weekly note

1. The child-chosen goal: easier, about the same, harder, or prefer not to answer.
2. Any pain or interference that the child wants help with: none, some, a lot, or prefer not to answer.
3. Participation and wellbeing: one brief observation about sleep, school, friends, or a valued activity, only when relevant.
4. Naturally occurring context: an illness, a school change, unusual tiredness, or a clinician-directed treatment change, if already known. Avoid detailed surveillance.
5. One question for the clinician, if any.

This is enough to start a conversation. A long daily symptom diary, continuous video, consumer wearable, home EEG, or extensive tic counter is not part of this framework. If a clinician asks for a short recording to clarify a clinical question, agree on the purpose, consent and assent, secure transfer, access, and deletion first. Never record secretly or share videos publicly.

### Meaningful outcomes

The child and clinician choose the smallest useful set. Examples are less pain or injury, better sleep, ability to join valued activities, fewer distressing school interruptions, less unwanted effort or embarrassment, and better quality of life. A trained clinician may use the Yale Global Tic Severity Scale, rating tic severity and impairment separately, and an age-appropriate validated quality-of-life or comorbidity measure if it will inform care. These scales should not be repeatedly self-administered by the family as a performance score.

Record adverse experiences too: fatigue, distress, loss of enjoyment, unwanted attention, or excessive family workload. A lower tic score accompanied by worse daily life is not straightforward success. The assessment guideline explicitly considers impairment, comorbidities, and quality-of-life measures alongside tics. [European assessment guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8521086/)

### Assent and stopping rules

Explain in ordinary language that participation is optional, that there are no right answers, and that the child may stop without losing care or disappointing anyone. Ask again if the activity changes. A quiet or compliant child has not necessarily agreed; formal pediatric research defines assent as affirmative agreement. [HHS assent guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/guidance/faq/children-research/index.html)

Stop observation immediately if the child declines, becomes worried about monitoring, feels blamed, changes activities to produce a better score, or the process creates family conflict. Resume only if the child wants to and the clinician sees a clear benefit. Seek clinical advice rather than waiting for the observation window if there is increasing pain, injury, major sleep or school disruption, or a new concerning symptom. An immediate threat to breathing, consciousness, or safety requires urgent local medical care rather than research documentation.

### What this framework must never include

- Induced stress, deliberate sleep loss, social embarrassment, tic-provoking challenges, or forced suppression to test a hypothesis.
- Home trials of medicines, supplements, cannabis products, restrictive diets, fasting, electrical or magnetic stimulation, or unvalidated devices.
- Starting, stopping, tapering, or changing prescribed treatment to make an observation “cleaner.” Any change belongs to the treating clinician.
- Research-only blood tests, genetic panels, immune panels, microbiome tests, scans, biopsies, or other procedures without a separate approved research protocol and a proportionate rationale.
- Promises that a particular test will reveal the child's root cause, subtype, ideal treatment, or chance of cure.
- A randomized N-of-1 treatment experiment devised by the family or an AI. A prospective research study requires independent clinical and ethical governance.

### Clinician-led options to discuss

If tics are not impairing, education, support, and watchful waiting can be appropriate. When the child wants help with troublesome tics, a therapist trained in CBIT is an established option; suitability and adaptation depend on developmental ability, preference, availability, and comorbid needs. A clinician can discuss medication when benefits may outweigh risks and can address ADHD, OCD, anxiety, sleep, pain, or learning needs in parallel. This document does not rank or prescribe medicines for an individual child. [AAN recommendations](https://www.aan.com/Guidelines/home/GetGuidelineContent/962); [CDC treatment overview](https://www.cdc.gov/tourette-syndrome/treatment/index.html)

Ordinary school supports can be discussed with the care team and, with the family's authorization, the school. The purpose is access, comfort, and inclusion. The child should not need to be tic-free to participate.

### Privacy and interpretation

Keep notes in a family-controlled secure location. Collect the minimum useful information; omit full names, birth dates, school names, exact location, photographs, and recordings from any general research brief. Sharing identifiable health information with a clinic, school, researcher, or digital service is a separate decision with a specific recipient and purpose. A research institution should explain consent, access, retention, withdrawal, secondary use, and the limits of de-identification before collecting data.

Review trends descriptively. “There were fewer difficult evenings this month” is a useful observation. “This food, activity, or treatment caused improvement” cannot be concluded from uncontrolled notes. Natural fluctuation, development, expectancy, sleep, school context, and concurrent care can all change the picture. Missing entries should not be backfilled from guesswork.

**Recommended starting partnership, if the user later authorizes outreach:** one tic-disorder clinician, one quantitative geneticist or neuroscientist matched to the chosen question, one biostatistician, and an independent patient/parent advisory group. ## 10 Cure Discovery Master Roadmap

### Scope and operating assumptions

This is a gated path toward better evidence and potentially durable therapies. It is not a forecast of when a cure will be discovered. Calendar targets describe outputs within the project's control. Data access, funding, ethics review, laboratory validation, and trial results may require longer or prevent a stage from proceeding.

Suggested accountable roles are a clinical lead, scientific lead, biostatistician, data-governance lead, and patient/family advisors. These roles are unfilled until qualified people formally accept them. The family should not have to create a laboratory or become the clinical sponsor.

### First 30 days Build a trustworthy foundation

**Outputs**

1. A dated evidence library covering established care, natural history, mechanisms, failed as well as positive trials, and current research uncertainties.
2. A one-page research charter separating symptom relief, durability, disease modification, and cure; include the child-protection boundaries above.
3. A shortlist of at most two testable research questions with an explicit reason each matters to patients.
4. A data-feasibility matrix distinguishing public information, licensed summary statistics, controlled data, and unavailable resources.
5. A clinical discussion agenda for ordinary care and optional child-chosen goals. The family observation plan is optional and must not delay support or treatment.
6. A realistic resource plan: required expertise, data, institutional affiliation, approvals, and funding categories. No invented budget or partner commitment.

**Day 30 go gate:** Select one question that is important, falsifiable, feasible with available or realistically obtainable resources, and safe to pursue without recruiting the child. If none meets that standard, produce a gap report and refine the scope rather than manufacture a hypothesis ranking.

**Useful outcome even if research pauses:** a clearer care discussion and an evidence map that prevents expenditure on unsupported tests or purported cures.

### By 90 days Produce a reproducible research plan and an initial result

**Outputs**

1. A detailed protocol and statistical analysis plan with primary outcome, comparator, exclusions, confounders, precision or power plan, and stop/go criteria.
2. One independent reproduction of a published result from accessible, permitted data, or a transparent report explaining precisely why reproduction could not be completed.
3. A mechanism dossier or implementation-research proposal chosen from the work packages, including disconfirming evidence and conflicts of interest.
4. If authorized and institutionally feasible, completed collaboration or data-access requests. Approval is not presumed, and no legal agreement is accepted on someone else's behalf without authorization.
5. A reviewed privacy and governance plan, including who may see data, retention, secondary use, and participant withdrawal limitations.

**Day 90 go gate:** The next stage has a viable data or specimen route, an accountable qualified lead, an independent review of the design, and a realistic replication plan. A data-access denial, inconclusive reanalysis, or failed replication may justify revising or stopping the chosen question.

**Do not claim:** a target is validated because it scores well in an AI literature summary, or a family diary demonstrates treatment efficacy.

### By 6 months Test the strongest question with proper controls

**Outputs conditional on access and approvals**

- Complete the planned reanalysis, external prediction validation, cell-model replication, or carefully governed service-evaluation pilot that was actually feasible.
- Publish or preserve a reproducible report with methods, limitations, null findings, and permitted code or aggregate results.
- Convene an independent scientific and patient-centered review: is the effect real, important, replicable, and worth the next risk or cost?
- Update the evidence library with new trial results and explicitly revise beliefs where findings conflict.

**Six-month go gate:** The candidate survives the relevant artifact, confounding, safety, and replication checks. Before a larger study, decide whether uncertainty is best reduced by another laboratory, a new dataset, a measurement study, or a controlled trial.

**If prerequisites remain unavailable:** the milestone is a verified feasibility decision, funding-ready protocol, or a narrower public-data study. Do not start a lower-quality child experiment to compensate for a research-access problem.

### By 12 months Deliver a defensible decision on the next investment

**Outputs**

1. At least one completed, reviewable evidence product, if resources permitted: an externally validated analysis, an independently replicated mechanistic result, or a controlled implementation finding.
2. A written target or intervention decision: advance, revise, or stop, with supporting evidence and unresolved risks.
3. For a candidate ready for translation, a sponsor-reviewed clinical development plan covering safety, manufacturing or device quality, regulatory requirements, trial design, equitable inclusion, meaningful endpoints, and follow-up. Enrollment is conditional on approvals and funding, not on the anniversary date.
4. A plain-language annual report describing what was learned, what failed, what is still uncertain, and what the result changes for patients.

**Twelve-month go gate:** Advance only when the evidence justifies the proposed level of human exposure or financial commitment. Strong negative evidence that eliminates a weak target is a successful scientific output.

**Do not claim:** a one-year program has produced a safe lasting cure simply because a candidate has a plausible mechanism or a small uncontrolled clinical signal.

### Longer term Establish clinical value and durability

The appropriate sequence may take multiple years and has no guaranteed endpoint:

1. Resolve modality-specific preclinical, safety, feasibility, and regulatory questions.
2. Conduct appropriately controlled early clinical studies with prespecified meaningful outcomes and rescue care.
3. Replicate efficacy independently in diverse, adequately powered multisite studies, with blinded assessment wherever possible.
4. Follow participants long enough to distinguish transient relief from durable benefit and to detect important delayed harms; measure function and quality of life, not just tic counts.
5. Address pediatric developmental safety separately when inclusion is scientifically justified and ethically approved. Do not infer pediatric benefit or safety from adults alone.
6. Evaluate real-world access, cost, training, adherence, equity, and benefit across relevant subgroups.
7. Revisit any disease-modification or cure claim only against prespecified evidence standards, independent replication, long-term outcomes, and the perspectives of people living with Tourette syndrome.

### Decision record used at every gate

- Question and prespecified claim.
- Evidence obtained and its quality.
- What could explain the result other than the proposed mechanism.
- Benefit and burden from the participant's perspective.
- Safety, privacy, and equity concerns.
- Independent reviewer and remaining disagreement.
- Decision: advance, revise, pause for a defined prerequisite, or stop.
- Next test and the result that would change the decision.

### What success can realistically mean

Near-term success is better care decisions, fewer unsupported claims, reproducible science, and an achievable collaboration. Medium-term success is a validated mechanism, improved treatment access, or a well-supported candidate for controlled testing. A safe lasting cure remains an aspiration that must be earned by evidence. A child's wellbeing can improve substantially while that scientific question remains open.

## Search scope and limitations

This was a targeted scoping review conducted on 8 October 2026, not a registered systematic review or an exhaustive global directory. Searches covered CDC and AAN guidance; European Tourette assessment guidance; PubMed and PMC primary studies of behavioral treatment, natural history, genomics, organoids, imaging, and stimulation; HHS child-research safeguards; official university and hospital researcher pages; and dbGaP, ENIGMA, PGC, and NRGR data routes. Searches used combinations of “Tourette,” “tic disorder,” “CBIT,” “ORBIT,” “natural history,” “genetics,” “organoids,” “ENIGMA,” “data access,” and named institutions or investigators. Most searching was in English; a USP institutional report in Portuguese was also used.

Primary publications and official institutional pages were preferred. The May 2026 ENIGMA analysis is explicitly labeled a preprint. Some PMC full-page requests returned a browser-check page; indexed source passages, PubMed records, or the publisher version were used where available. The PGC downloads endpoint could not be loaded, so no claim is made that its latest release was successfully downloaded. No participant-level data, biospecimens, full trial datasets, or individual medical records were obtained or analyzed. No inference of current recruitment, clinician availability, funding, collaboration consent, or data permission follows from a public directory or publication.

Website information can lag reality; the UCT leadership discrepancy is a concrete example. The map is strongest for the listed North American, European, Australian, Korean, Brazilian, and South African sources and leaves material geographic gaps. Methods proposed in this document require expert review and are not approved protocols. U.S. child-research guidance is cited as an ethics and regulatory example; any actual study must satisfy the rules and independent review requirements of its own jurisdiction.
