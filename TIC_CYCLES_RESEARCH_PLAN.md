# Tic waxing and waning: evidence and research plan

Public-science research brief | 8 October 2026 | Version 0.1

## Purpose and boundaries

The immediate scientific question is whether an independently measured process reliably changes **before** a rise in tic burden and helps explain the subsequent trajectory. A quiet–rise–peak–fall pattern is a useful observation, but its shape does not identify a cause or prove a biological oscillator.

This targeted evidence review is not an exhaustive systematic review, a clinical protocol, a medical order, or evidence that a cure has been found. It contains no individual or family medical information. Clinical care, child assent, comfort and participation take priority over data collection. Research can proceed through literature and existing datasets without recruiting any particular child.

This brief supplements, rather than replaces, the optional low-burden framework in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md). Proposed tests of hypotheses are research questions, not instructions to obtain clinical tests or conduct home experiments.

## 1. Separate the time scales before explaining the pattern

### Seconds to minutes: urges, bouts and context

Tics can cluster within bouts. A 1998 study of 22 medication-free participants analysed inter-tic intervals from short video recordings and found temporal dependence and scale-related patterns. Its proposed fractal or chaotic interpretation was explicitly tentative. The short recordings cannot establish a weeks-long biological oscillator. [1]

In an experiment involving 17 people with Tourette syndrome, reported urges tended to rise before and fall after tics within a window of approximately ten seconds either side. Suppression changed the urge–tic relationship. This is evidence about momentary sensory–motor interactions, not an explanation of multiweek exacerbations. [2]

Visible tic frequency can also change with attention, setting and suppression. In a study of 31 children and adolescents, acute laboratory stress increased physiological stress measures while visible tics became less frequent. Therefore, fewer observed tics need not mean lower internal burden or less stress. No stress-provoking experiment is proposed here. [3]

### Days to weeks or months: waxing and waning

The clinical phenotype includes fluctuating frequency, intensity, complexity and repertoire. Current assessment guidance recognizes substantial within-person variation and the possibility of suppression during appointments. It does not supply a universal cycle length or an individual biomarker of cycle phase. [4]

A multiweek wave should not be inferred from minute-scale bout structure. Nor does repeated rise and decline alone prove periodicity: as a statistical possibility, persistent but nonperiodic fluctuations can also produce apparent waves. The first analysis should establish the observation interval, consistency of wave duration, measurement reliability and whether a periodic model actually forecasts better than a simple recent-severity model.

### Seasons and developmental years

Seasonal evidence is inconsistent. A 2024 Italian study followed 24 participants with prominent tic-related obsessive–compulsive symptoms at six assessments over 15 months and reported higher tic severity in warmer seasons. High attrition, selected phenotype and correlated environmental exposures limit causal interpretation. Its suggested immune and temperature mechanisms were not demonstrated by the seasonal association. [5]

A 2026 Calgary registry analysis included 370 children and 549 assessments. An unadjusted difference between months did not translate into a significant overall month effect after adjustment for other variables. Sparse individual sampling and an observational design also limit this study. Taken together, these studies do not establish a universal seasonal clock. [6]

Across years, many people experience improvement after early adolescence, while others continue to have substantial symptoms. This developmental trajectory is distinct from a short-term waning phase, and a brief quiet period should not be labelled durable remission. [7]

## 2. Biological hypotheses and the evidence they would require

### A. Changing circuit excitability or control

Human functional imaging implicates motor and control components of cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits. A study of 13 people with Tourette syndrome and 21 comparison participants found activity differences associated with tic generation and severity. Imaging associations and model-based directional analyses do not prove a causal circuit defect, a particular neurotransmitter abnormality, or the mechanism of longer waves. [8]

**Testable research prediction:** an independently measured, reproducible circuit signal changes before a within-person rise and adds predictive information beyond recent symptoms, context and treatment. A finding present only during tics may reflect movement, effort or their consequences. Functional imaging, electrophysiology and neurotransmitter studies are institutional research methods here, not routine investigations prescribed by this brief.

### B. Arousal, sleep, attention and environmental context

A two-year prospective study of 45 young people with Tourette syndrome and/or OCD found that antecedent psychosocial stress predicted subsequent tic severity, although the relation was less robust than for some other symptoms. This longer-horizon result can coexist with the acute laboratory finding above; different time scales and measurement contexts need separate models. [9]

A 2024 two-week actigraphy study included 12 children with Tourette syndrome and 22 controls. It identified differences in sleep efficiency and sleep-onset latency, but did not establish poor sleep as the driver of tic cycles. Sleep disturbance may be a contributor, consequence, comorbidity or treatment effect. [10]

**Testable research prediction:** naturally occurring changes in a prespecified sleep or arousal measure precede exacerbations and improve held-out predictions. Sleep measurement must be validated for the population; movement-sensitive wearables can be affected by motor activity. A predictive sleep measure is not by itself a mechanism or proof that modifying sleep will modify the disorder.

### C. Infection or immune-related processes

The prospective EMTICS cohort followed 715 young people with established chronic tic disorders for approximately 16 months. Across 405 exacerbations, group-A streptococcal exposure was not significantly associated with exacerbations under four exposure definitions. This weighs against assuming that ordinary tic waves represent repeated streptococcal triggers. It does not rule out every immune mechanism or settle all questions about distinct acute-onset neuropsychiatric presentations. [11]

**Testable research prediction:** a prespecified, independently measured immune signal precedes changes, replicates across appropriate cohorts, and survives controls for ordinary infections, treatment, age, timing and assay variability. A nonspecific blood abnormality during a peak is not enough. Any research-only sampling needs institutional oversight and a proportionate scientific rationale.

## 3. What would distinguish a driver from a correlate?

Evidence should be assessed in stages:

1. **Reliable observation:** distinguish tic counts, intensity, pain, functional impairment, urges and suppression effort. These are related but not interchangeable outcomes.
2. **Temporal order:** the proposed exposure or biological signal changes before the relevant symptom transition, measured prospectively rather than reconstructed after a peak.
3. **Incremental prediction:** it improves on simple baselines, including recent tic burden, developmental age, context and treatment exposure. Account for autocorrelation: consecutive observations are not independent replications.
4. **Counterexamples and alternative explanations:** look for exposure without worsening, worsening without exposure, reverse causation, measurement changes, common causes and missing observations during difficult periods.
5. **Independent testing:** select predictors and timing windows before examining the validation data. Test on later periods and independent participants or sites where feasible. Report uncertainty and failed predictions.
6. **Mechanistic evidence:** measure the claimed biological process independently of the symptom labels. Calling a fitted latent symptom state “dopamine” or “inflammation” does not make it a biological measurement.
7. **Causal evidence:** when justified, an ethically governed intervention that selectively changes the proposed process and improves relevant outcomes can strengthen causal inference. Mediation, specificity, safety and replication still require scrutiny. No family-led treatment experiment is proposed.

Temporal association, even when repeated and predictive, is insufficient to establish causation. A useful predictor could still improve care without being the disease mechanism. Likewise, a cause of an exacerbation need not be the root cause of Tourette syndrome or a route to cure.

Starting an intervention near a peak creates a particular interpretive problem: subsequent improvement may reflect the natural decline or regression to the mean. Uncontrolled before–after changes cannot resolve this.

## 4. Optional care-support observation: minimum necessary burden

### First decision: would observation help a clinical decision?

The default remains the roadmap's optional one-to-two-minute weekly check-in, with an explicit option to skip. This is a discussion aid, not a validated measurement protocol. Brief parent-only observations may be appropriate if acceptable to the child and clinician; they must not become covert surveillance or override a child's refusal.

Child assent comes first. The child should understand that participation is optional, that ordinary care does not depend on participating, and that there are no correct symptom scores. A clinician and family should choose the least burdensome approach that answers a specific care question. [15]

### Frequency and duration depend on the actual time scale

- Clarify whether the suspected waves occur within a day, over weeks, or over months before selecting a cadence.
- Weekly notes may support a conversation about longer changes while missing short transitions. That limitation is a reason to narrow the question or accept uncertainty, not automatically to impose daily tracking.
- A more frequent, brief observation period should occur only if the clinician identifies a concrete benefit and the child agrees. Daily tracking is not the default recommendation.
- Set a clinician-agreed review date and stopping rule. No fixed number of weeks, observations or cycles guarantees an adequate sample; irregularity, missingness and changing care may prevent reliable inference.
- Do not postpone needed treatment, change prescribed treatment, or extend burdensome monitoring to obtain a “clean” dataset.

### Minimal content, when useful

1. A child-chosen goal or functional outcome: easier, similar, harder, or skip.
2. Pain or interference worth addressing, without requiring tic counting.
3. A brief description of an overall change in tic pattern, if already noticed.
4. Only naturally known context, such as an illness, unusual tiredness, school change or clinician-directed treatment change.
5. A question for the clinician, if any.

If clinical assessment is needed, the clinician can use a validated instrument such as the YGTSS alongside history and assessment of impairment. A home impression is not interchangeable with a clinician-rated score. [4]

Do not interrupt school, play, sleep or enjoyable activities for observation. No continuous video/audio, home EEG, consumer biometric package or compulsory tic diary is part of this proposal. If a short video would answer a particular clinical question, agree separately on consent, assent, secure transfer, access and deletion. Never record secretly or share publicly.

Do not induce stress, sleep loss, illness or tic-provoking challenges; do not require suppression to test a hypothesis. No home trials of medicines, supplements, restrictive diets, cannabis products or electrical/magnetic stimulation are authorized or recommended. Prescribed treatment changes belong to the treating clinician.

Stop or simplify if monitoring produces distress, blame, forced suppression, altered activities to obtain better scores, or family conflict. Missing observations are not failures. Seek care for increasing pain, injury, major sleep disruption or loss of participation rather than waiting for a research review. An immediate threat to breathing, consciousness or safety requires urgent local medical care.

## 5. Clinical testing: indications, not a shopping list

For a typical tic presentation, the useful starting point is clinical history, examination, medication review and assessment of impairment and relevant co-occurring conditions. ESSTS guidance states that routine neuroimaging, EEG and further investigations do not add value in establishing a tic-disorder diagnosis. Testing should answer a specific concern identified by the treating clinician; medication safety monitoring is a separate indication. [4]

Gradual recurrent tic waves alone are not evidence for PANS. Abrupt, dramatic OCD or restrictive eating with other acute neuropsychiatric changes warrants clinical assessment. New altered consciousness, seizures, cognitive regression or focal neurological findings require evaluation for alternative conditions. The AAP's 2025 clinical report recommends a phased, symptom-guided approach and does not endorse routine broad immune/infectious screening or the Cunningham panel. Strep testing should follow clinical indications rather than tic severity alone. [12]

A 2026 case-control investigation compared 51 children with PANS and 58 with idiopathic OCD and/or tic disorders. Nonspecific abnormal laboratory results were common in both groups without significant group differences. This cautions against interpreting extensive testing as a reliable explanation of symptoms; it does not imply that an indicated investigation should be withheld. [13]

Research measures of dopamine, inflammation, genetics or microbiome composition are not established clinical cycle-phase tests. The hypothesized biological categories in this document must not be converted into medical orders.

## 6. Institutional research work packages

### Priority 1: establish whether predictable phases exist

Begin with suitable existing longitudinal data, subject to access and governance. Prespecify how a rise, peak, decline and quiet interval will be defined without using future observations to claim real-time prediction. Compare a periodic or state-transition model against simple recent-severity and nonperiodic autocorrelated models. Use forward-in-time evaluation; split by participant and site for broader validation. Report measurement interval, missingness, treatment changes and uncertainty. Do not presume that every participant has the same phase structure.

**Useful output:** evidence that a phase model improves prospective prediction, or a transparent negative result showing that the available data cannot support it.

### Priority 2: test independently measured precursors

Only after a credible measurement and sampling plan exists, assess a small prespecified set of biological or contextual candidates. A potentially informative institutional design compares quiet, rising, peak and falling periods, including rising and falling observations at similar symptom severity. This can help separate a pre-transition signal from a correlate of current severity, but phase comparisons remain observational and vulnerable to confounding.

Additional sampling, imaging, wearables, participant recruitment or intervention require the responsible institution's clinical, ethical and data-protection review. Pediatric studies require affirmative age-appropriate assent and parental permission under applicable rules, a justified burden, and a withdrawal plan. No such study, data access, collaboration or recruitment is represented as already active. [15]

**Useful output:** a replicated precursor with uncertainty, a falsified hypothesis, or a specific feasibility gap. Even a positive result is not evidence of cure.

## 7. Search record and access limitations

Searched on 8 October 2026 using web discovery of PubMed, primary journals, official guidance and institutional sources. No date restriction was applied. These were targeted searches rather than a registered exhaustive strategy; no screened-record count or PRISMA completion is claimed.

Examples of exact queries used:

- `Tourette syndrome waxing waning tic frequency longitudinal study daily fluctuations time series`
- `European clinical guidelines Tourette assessment laboratory tests EEG MRI not indicated 2022`
- `Tourette prospective study stress tic severity predicts future symptoms Lin 2007`
- `EMTICS group A streptococcal exposure tic exacerbations prospective cohort Neurology 2021`
- `Tourette tics sleep daily diary actigraphy longitudinal study`
- `"The effect of calendar month on tic severity" "Results"`
- `AAP 2025 PANS clinical report testing Cunningham panel not recommended abrupt onset OCD`
- `"Recommended Medical Investigations" "51" "58" PANS 2026`

Access was mixed. PubMed abstracts and publisher/PMC indexed excerpts supplied much of the study-level information; direct opening of several PMC pages returned access-check screens. Publisher pages, official guidance and indexed article excerpts provided additional methods and results. Not every complete paper, supplement, registration or analysis plan was inspected. This brief uses only claims supported by the material retrieved and does not claim a complete risk-of-bias or correction/retraction review. Full-text and supplement review should precede preregistration of an analysis or any clinical research application. Search-engine crawl dates were not treated as publication dates.

## Privacy and next evidence step

Keep this repository limited to public evidence, generic plans and original scientific summaries. Do not add family history, clinician correspondence, diary entries, raw physiological or genetic data, recordings or child identifiers. A private repository is not a clinical-data system.

The next authorized literature task is to identify longitudinal datasets with enough within-person resolution to evaluate transition models, then document measurement cadence, outcome validity, governance, access requirements and whether independent biological measures exist. This does not authorize contacting investigators, accessing controlled data or enrolling participants.
